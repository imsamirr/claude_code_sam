# COM458: Travel/NYC Iteration ("Worst Shoes for NYC")
## Directing Guide & Editorial Blueprint

### 1. Directing Overview & Creative Architecture
COM458 is the New York City travel adaptation of the proven winner COM376 ("Worst Shoes for Disney"). The structural engine of COM376 relies on rapid-fire, noun-literal visual matching, high-status contrarian authority (AI Podiatrist), authentic location-grounded UGC, 3D anatomical pain visualization, physical product proof demos, and a high-urgency promotional close.

* **Target Run Time:** ~130 to 140 seconds
* **Shot Count:** ~95 to 105 cuts (median duration: 1.1 to 1.4 seconds per shot)
* **Visual Rhythm:** Cut on nearly every semantic caption phrase. Every specific noun, location, or physical sensation gets an immediate visual cut.
* **Tone:** Candid travel insider warning backed by podiatric biomechanical common sense.

---

### 2. On-Screen Text System (Headlines vs. Supers vs. Captions)

To match the clean visual hierarchy of COM376 and avoid cluttering the screen with competing text blocks, on-screen text is strictly tiered into four functional layers:

| Text Layer | Visual Treatment | Screen Position & Timing | Editorial Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hook Headline Box** | White rounded card with drop shadow. Line 1 in bold red uppercase; lines 2-3 in bold black uppercase. No running captions underneath. | Mid-frame (center-center), seconds 0.0 to 3.0 of each hook. | Instant scroll-stopper; establishes high-stakes contrarian controversy. |
| **Section Headlines** | High-contrast white bold uppercase font with clean 2px black outline (or yellow accent for number). | Top third of screen (approx. 18-22% down). Held continuously across the opening 3-4 shots of each numbered section, then cut. | Establishes the listicle structure so viewers know a clear 3-part teardown is unfolding. |
| **Feature / Proof Badges** | Floating pill badges or animated highlight tags (e.g. "ROOMY TOE BOX", "ZERO-DROP FLAT ARCH SUPPORT"). | Appears directly over product close-ups in Section P5. | Anchors specific physical features to the exact biomechanical solution. |
| **Offer Stickers & Badges** | 3D popping badges: red "40% OFF" burst, angled red "FREE SHIPPING" ribbon, gold metallic "30-DAY WEAR TEST" seal. | Mid-screen to lower-third over blurred product/lifestyle background in Section P7. | Drives direct-response conversion urgency without feeling corporate. |
| **Running Captions** | Heavy bold sans-serif black text encased in individual white rounded pills, 2 to 4 words per line, 1 to 2 lines max. | Lower third (approx. 68-72% screen height). Active across all shots except Hook 0-3s. | Guarantees 100% sound-off comprehension while keeping the focal center free. |

#### Specific Super vs. Caption Decisions for Script Lines:
* **Hook 1 Super ("HOKAS & ON CLOUDS ARE THE WORST SHOES FOR NYC"):** Render as the mid-frame **Hook Headline Box**.
* **Hook 2 Super ("YOU'LL REGRET PACKING HOKAS OR ON CLOUD FOR NYC"):** Render as the mid-frame **Hook Headline Box**.
* **Hook 3 Super ("WHY NYC TOURISTS TAP OUT BY 2 PM"):** Render as the mid-frame **Hook Headline Box**.
* **Body P1 Super ("BUILT FOR 40-MIN RUNS / NOT 20,000 STEPS ON NYC CONCRETE"):** Keep as **Running Captions**. Let the fast cuts of treadmill runners contrasted with NYC concrete do the storytelling. A fixed super here would compete with the incoming Section #1 headline.
* **Body P2 Super ("1. HOKA FOAM PACKS FLAT / ON CLOUD PODS FLATTEN"):** Use as **Section Headline #1**: `#1 - SOFT FOAM PACKS FLAT` displayed in top third across shots 14 to 17.
* **Body P3 Super ("2. HOKA MAKES YOU WOBBLE / ON CLOUDS ARE SLICK ON SNOW & ICE"):** Use as **Section Headline #2**: `#2 - WOBBLE & SLIPPERY OUTSOLES` displayed in top third across shots 37 to 40.
* **Body P4 Super ("3. ON CLOUDS TOO NARROW / HOKAS TAPER"):** Use as **Section Headline #3**: `#3 - NARROW TOE BOX & BLISTERS` displayed in top third across shots 65 to 68.
* **Body P5 Super ("BUILT FOR REAL LIFE / ROOMY TOE BOX..."):** Do NOT use as a static super. Instead, use an intro top-third banner: `COMFORTWEAR: BUILT FOR REAL LIFE` for 3 seconds, followed by dynamic feature callout tags pinned over the hands-on product demos.
* **Body P6 Super ("DO NOT MISS OUT / KEEP UP ALL DAY / ZERO FOOT PAIN"):** Keep as **Running Captions**. P6 is pure emotional travel aspiration (Brooklyn Bridge, Broadway, Soho); keeping the screen free of rigid banners lets the UGC lifestyle footage breathe.
* **Body P7 Super ("HOLIDAY SALE / UP TO 40% OFF..."):** Use as animated **Offer Badges and Final End Card** rather than text captions.

### 3. AI Podiatrist Placement Strategy Across Hooks

The AI Podiatrist ("Dr. Moore, DPM" in lab coat with clinical background) provides immediate medical legitimacy and transforms a potential "brand attack" into an educational biomechanical insight. Here is where the doctor belongs across the variants:

* **Hook 1 (Direct COM376 Match):**
  * **Placement:** Shots 4 to 6 (approx. 3.0s to 6.5s) on the lines: *"I know that'll upset people, but it is not the brand. It is the design."*
  * **Visual:** Mid-shot of Dr. Moore speaking directly to camera in modern clinic, hands gesturing calmly. Lower-third graphic: `Dr. Moore, DPM` with blue subtitle pill `PODIATRIST PHYSICIAN`.
  * **Strategic Function:** Exactly mimics the viral credibility anchor of COM376.

* **Hook 2 (Travel Suitcase / Packing Hook):**
  * **Placement Option A (Creator-Driven Open):** Keep shots 1 to 5 entirely within travel creator suitcase packing UGC. Cut to Dr. Moore at shot 6 (around 7.5s) on the line: *"Those plush gym shoes are built for forty minutes on a treadmill, not 20,000 steps on unforgiving city concrete."* Dr. Moore is shown holding a cut-away sneaker sole or gesturing in front of a foot skeleton model.
  * **Strategic Function:** Delivers creative diversification against Hook 1 by leading with packing anxiety, then bringing in the medical expert to explain why the shoes fail.

* **Hook 3 (2 PM Tourist Reality Check):**
  * **Placement:** Keep the hook (0.0s to 6.5s) entirely focused on gritty street observation (tourists sitting on curbs, rubbing arches, hailing yellow cabs in Midtown). Introduce Dr. Moore immediately at the transition to Body P1 (shots 12 to 14) on the line: *"These shoes are built to move a runner forward for forty minutes, not for someone exploring New York City."*
  * **Strategic Function:** Validates the tourist's pain on the street first, then cuts to the doctor for the authoritative diagnosis.

### 4. Competitor Framing & Legal Guardrails

Because the script names Hoka and On Running directly, all visuals, text overlays, and editorial treatments must adhere to strict FTC and trademark compliance guidelines:

1. **Opinion & Biomechanical Intent Framing:** Every comparison must be rooted in *intended use* rather than claiming the competitor shoes are defective products. The central thesis is: "Great running shoes for runners; inappropriate geometry for 8-hour standing and concrete walking."
2. **On-Screen Legal Disclaimer:** On the opening competitor callouts and throughout sections P2-P4, place a micro-disclaimer at the bottom border (opacity 60%, 10pt clean sans):
   `*Creator opinions based on travel experience and biomechanical design. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Hoka or On Running.*`
3. **Logo Treatment in B-Roll:** Use real, organic UGC where shoes are shown on feet in the wild. Avoid extreme macro blowups of competitor brand logos or trademark badges. The visual emphasis should always be on the *geometry*: the tall foam stack height, the hollow pods under compression, and the tapered toe box.
4. **No Defamatory Alteration:** Do not show broken, torn, or destroyed competitor shoes. Show them functioning as built, but compressing flat under static standing loads.

---

### 5. Sourcing & Asset Taxonomy

Every shot in the directing breakdown is assigned to one of five production tiers:
1. **NYC_UGC:** Raw, organic vertical video (9:16) captured on smartphone by real NYC tourists and residents. Natural ambient lighting, authentic city noise, zero commercial polish.
2. **COMPETITOR_UGC:** Real footage of Hokas (Bondi, Clifton) and On Running (Cloudmonster, Cloud 5) worn on city streets, subways, and running paths, showing foot movement, heel strike, and sole compression.
3. **PRODUCT:** High-definition close-up footage of the ComfortWear Ortho Stretch sneaker (knit slip-on sneaker, hands flexing wide toe box, stretching upper knit, pulling out removable arch insole, bending non-slip tread, model walking effortlessly).
4. **AI:** Photorealistic generated footage (e.g. Midjourney / Kling / Runway / Sora) for clinical scenarios where organic UGC is impossible or legally sensitive.
5. **ANIMATION:** 3D biomechanical motion graphics showing foot skeletal structure, plantar fascia tension, toe splay vs. pinch, and red/orange heat-map pressure zones.

### Sourcing & Editing Rules for the Video Editor
1. **Pacing is King:** Maintain a median cut length of 1.1 to 1.4 seconds. Cut on the exact noun or action in the voiceover. If the VO says "bagel", show the bagel line; if it says "metal grates", show the grate instantly.
2. **Audio Design:** Mix in subtle, realistic organic NYC foley beneath the voiceover: distant subway train rumble, turnstile clicks, crosswalk beeps, yellow cab horns, and winter wind gusts. This grounds the ad in visceral reality.
3. **Sound-Off Caption Styling:** Render running captions as black bold sans-serif text inside white rounded rectangle pill boxes at 68-72% screen height. 2 to 4 words per line max.
