# DR Video Ad Editing Playbook: WearOrtho

Paid social (Meta + TikTok) direct-response editing reference for the WearOrtho creative strategist and editor.
Audience: women 45–75, foot pain, plantar fasciitis, bunions, nurses and others who stand all day.
Compiled 2026-11-14 from 40+ sources (see §13). Source tags in [brackets] map to §13.
Where sources disagree, both views are given. Lines marked *(inference)* are my own synthesis, not a source claim.

---

## 0. The 20 rules that matter most

1. **The first 3 s decide the ad.** Put your best shot first. If a clip is a thumb-stopper, use it again later, because people miss it the first time [Recharm]. Meta/Nielsen attribute up to 47% of campaign value to the first 3 s and up to 74% to the first 10 s [Flowjam].
2. **Stack the hook: visual + on-screen text + spoken line, all in the first frame.** About 63% of top auction ads put text in frame 1 [Segwise-Anatomy, Billo-HR]. No "Hey guys" intros. Start mid-action [Flighted].
3. **Show the product in the first 1–2 s on demos** [TikTok-Guide, Flowjam], and show it working by about second 7–8 [VidPros].
4. **Cut every pause longer than ~0.3 s** [Billo-Edit]. Cut before the energy drops, not after it drops [Flighted].
5. **B-roll clips run 1–2 s, or 3 s if there is a lot of motion** [Recharm]. Another source says 0.8–1.7 s [Gattozzi]. For our 45–75 audience, run toward the slower end (see §4).
6. **Say it, show it.** Every claim spoken on camera gets matching B-roll at that moment. Mismatched visuals are the most common failure [Gattozzi, Useclip-B, Influee-B].
7. **Readability beats beauty.** Keep text in ONE zone near the product, never top and bottom at once. Hold text on screen for as long as it takes to read it aloud [Recharm].
8. **Assume sound is off.** Burn in captions rather than relying on auto-captions [Billo-Edit, VidPros]. Run a full QC pass with sound off before export [Savannah-200].
9. **Older audiences get more breathing room.** Slower talent and longer holds are fine for 45–75 [Recharm, Gattozzi, SmartMarketer], but never hold a static clip for ~5 s without action [Gattozzi].
10. **Pacing follows attention, not the music.** Don't hold a clip just to land a beat [Recharm].
11. **Use SFX sparingly:** no more than 3 per 30 s, and only to mark a change [Clip-Audio]. Music sits at ~15–25% volume under the voiceover, with no vocal tracks under speech [Clip-Audio].
12. **Lock V1 before you version.** Don't deliver four versions and two aspect ratios of an unapproved cut [Recharm].
13. **Test 3–5 hooks per body** [Billo-Edit, CreativeOS, TikTok-BP]. Each hook needs a visually different opening (different subject, framing or motion in the first 2–5 s). Text-only swaps get grouped as the same ad [Savannah-200, Nikhil].
14. **Real concept diversity beats hook tweaks.** About 90% of winners are net-new concepts, and iterations add roughly +20% profitable spend to a winner [Cooper-100]. Under Andromeda, near-duplicates collapse together [Loomer, Nikhil].
15. **Read metrics in order: hook rate → hold rate → CTR → CVR → CPA** [Runneth-Metrics]. Each one points to a different part of the edit to fix (§10).
16. **Benchmarks to beat on Meta:** hook rate ~25% is average and 30%+ is strong. Health & Beauty averages 28% [Billo-HR]. Thumbstop rises with viewer age [SmartMarketer], so judge WearOrtho against its own account median.
17. **Native beats polished.** "Pretty ads stand out and get skipped" [Cooper-Hott]. Smartphone footage beat studio footage in 84% of Stories tests (Motion via [Segwise-5]).
18. **Make bad footage work by adding motion:** punch-ins, flips, fake whip pans, split screens, green screen, native UI, pop-in graphics [Recharm].
19. **Never address the viewer's medical condition in the second person, and never promise cure or treatment.** Describe comfort and design, not outcomes (§11) [AuditSocials, ADM-Health].
20. **Editor = strategist.** Get dashboard access. Review losers as well as winners, since ~90% of ads fail [Pawliw-NoBS]. Bring ideas. Don't wait on a 70%-baked brief [Recharm].

## 2. Hooks (0–3 s)

### 2.1 Rules
- **A hook does three jobs:** stop the scroll, earn the next ~5 s of watch time, and filter for the right buyer [CapInt, Motion-Bootcamp]. A high hook rate that pulls the wrong people is a loss. Cooper has seen winners with hook rates under 15% [Cooper-Hott].
- **Three layers fire together:** visual, text, audio/verbal [CapInt, Motion-Bootcamp]. Stack them, e.g. a demographic call-out plus an emotional trigger word: "For busy moms who hate meal prep, this feels illegal" [CreativeOS].
- **Text must be on screen in frame 1** for muted feeds, and the frame should show something specific [Flowjam, Segwise-Anatomy].
- **Enter mid-action or mid-conversation.** Show the result before the explanation [Flighted, CreativeOS].
- **Front-load your best asset.** Repeat a great thumb-stop later in the ad [Recharm].
- **The hook is a promise, and second 4 is where the viewer checks it.** If the body doesn't pay off the hook by ~10 s, you lose the sale [Flowjam]. High hook rate with low hold rate means the payoff in ~8–20 s is weak, so fix the body, not the hook [Segwise-Anatomy].
- **Match the hook to awareness level** [Flowjam]:
  - Unaware: open on a recognizable scene (a nurse's 12-hour shift), not on the product.
  - Problem-aware: name the problem in the customer's words. This is usually the right first test for us.
  - Solution-aware: show the difference from alternatives in frame 1.
  - Product-aware/retargeting: lead with the offer or what's new.
- **Hooks Flowjam says to avoid:** "Stop scrolling" openers, fake notification UIs, fake "POV" that isn't a real point of view, AI avatars posing as surprised customers. Savannah, by contrast, runs fake TikTok comment overlays and iMessage screen-records successfully [Savannah-Summary, Savannah-30]. Sources disagree here. Test it rather than ban it.
- **Bold claims are fatigued.** In Motion's 550k-ad BFCM 2025 study, "Bold claim" hooks had a 7.19% hit rate and low spend share. Leaders were Newness 11.37%, Sale announcement 11.35%, Price anchor 10.89%, Urgency 9.73%, Announcement 9.59%, Offer-only 9.29% [Motion-500k]. **It's mid-November, so lean on offer and newness hooks through BFCM.**
- **Top visual formats by hit rate (same study):** Unboxing 9.83%, Offer-first banner 8.68%, Demo 8.11%, Founder/BTS >8%. "Scale" formats are offer-first banner and demo. Unboxing and POV win often but plateau [Motion-500k].
- **Billo's top UGC hook types:** Authentic moment, Audience call-out, Humor. List hooks, FOMO, comparison and "secret" framing were mostly absent from its top tier [Billo-Edit]. Motion's BFCM data favored FOMO and urgency, so the sources disagree. That's seasonal context: BFCM versus evergreen.
- **Open loops:** keep 2–4 open loops in a short video. Don't close them too early, and always close them eventually [Socialync-OL]. Pawliw teaches open loops and curiosity gaps, used sparingly [Pawliw-Hooks].

### 2.2 How many hook variations
| Source | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Billo [Billo-Edit] | 3–5 hook variants per piece of raw footage. Brief, produce and approve each hook as its own asset |
| CreativeOS, attn, TikTok [CreativeOS, TikTok-BP] | 3–5 per creative; TikTok: 3–5 creatives per ad group |
| Segwise [Segwise-Anatomy] | 5–10 hooks against one core body, changing one variable at a time |
| Flowjam [Flowjam] | Write 10 opening lines, test 5 opening shots, keep the body identical from the same frame onward |
| Savannah [Savannah-200] | Per brand per week: 2 written hooks plus 2 opening *visual* variations. Meta treats a variant as distinct only when the visual changes in the first 3–5 s |
| Nikhil [Nikhil] | If two ads look alike in the first 2 s, assume Meta groups them as one. Run 8–12 distinct concepts × 2–3 variations each |

**WearOrtho default *(inference)*:** for each new body, cut 4 hooks: 2 different *visual* openings × 2 text/VO lines. Never ship four text-only swaps over the same first frame.

## 3. Structure, scripts, formats, lengths

### 3.1 Core DR beat sheet
Motion's DR formula: **Hook → Problem → Solution → Value prop (2–3 benefits) → Social proof → CTA** [Runneth-Scripts]. Savannah's minimum: **Hook → Story → CTA**, and deviate only on purpose [Savannah-200].

| Beat | 30 s ad | 45–60 s ad | Editing job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–3 s | 0–3 s | Stacked V+T+VO; product visible in ≤2 s on demos |
| Problem / agitate | 3–9 s | 3–15 s | Relatable first-person pain. Howland says 60% of the ad should go to problem+agitate and 40% to solution, while "most brands flip it" [Segwise-5] |
| Mechanism / USP | 9–15 s | 15–25 s | Show *why* it works: arch, toe box, cushion, removable insole |
| Demo / proof of use | 15–21 s | 25–38 s | Product working, not just existing [Influee-B]. Value shown by ~7–8 s on short cuts [VidPros] |
| Social proof | 21–25 s | 38–48 s | 3–5 creators or reviews saying the same point in different words [Inbeat] |
| Offer + CTA | 25–30 s | 48–60 s | Specific verb + outcome. On screen ≥3 s and spoken [Segwise-Anatomy]. VidPros says 1–2 s end card (they disagree; for 55+ use ≥3 s *(inference)*) |

- **Hook stacking through the body:** give a new reason to keep watching every 5–15 s. Mention the offer early (5–10 s) when there's a discount [UGCSocial].
- **Mashups:** one claim per mashup. Use 3–5 creators in the proof stack. Cut on a shared noun or the product name. Add one objection handle and one demo. Normalize loudness, crop and caption style. The CTA restates the hook's promise in the same verb [Inbeat]. Adcrate said 85% of its 2023 winners were mashups [Cooper-Motion].
- **Word count:** an ad under 20 s fits about 60 words [Runneth-Scripts].
- **Barry Hott:** film the longest usable cut. His winners run from 6 s to 3 min [Cooper-Hott].

### 3.2 Format menu (with WearOrtho angle and editing notes)
| Format | Editing notes | WearOrtho use |
|---|---|---|
| **Lo-fi "yapper" UGC** | Talking points, not a script. Phone only. Fewer cuts are OK [Runneth-UGC] | Nurse in car after a shift |
| **Problem/solution UGC** | Scripted but loose. Hook → problem → solution [Runneth-UGC] | Morning-stiffness → shoes → day out |
| **Testimonial / review** | Quote overlays, screenshot-style reviews, documentary-style interviews. Specificity sells [Segwise-5]. Polished testimonials underperform phone-filmed ones [Segwise-Anatomy] | Real customers 55–75. Cooper is "bullish on real customer stories" [Cooper-100] |
| **Founder / BTS** | Hit rate above 8% in Motion's BFCM data [Motion-500k] | "Why I started WearOrtho": factory, insole layers |
| **Us vs them** | Split screens, side-by-side quality shots, per-unit comparisons. Educational, not an attack [Segwise-5]. The "them" can be "life before" or a generic category [Bestever]. One dataset had comparison ads failing 88% of the time on landing-page congruence [Sicard] | Flat fashion sneaker vs WearOrtho press/flex test |
| **Street interview** | Footage is cheap; hook timing, response order, product-reveal timing and CTA drive results [Stradvert]. Some say the format is getting overused. Needs paid/staged disclosure review [NetInf-Street] | Outside hospitals and at walking groups |
| **Podcast-style / talking-head explainer** | Two-shot podcast set; cut between angles; B-roll every few seconds | Podiatrist/PT "shoe myths" (credentials real) |
| **Demo** | No VO needed; minimal production; product working [Segwise-5]. A "scale" format [Motion-500k] | Press test, flex test, slip-on, walking on concrete |
| **Unboxing** | Highest hit rate in the BFCM study [Motion-500k] | Gift-wrapped box (Oodie gift angle [Echelon]) |
| **Reasons why / listicle** | Numbered, skimmable. Each reason under 4 s on TikTok [TikTok-Guide, Segwise-5] | "4 reasons nurses switch" |
| **Green screen** | Speaker in front of reviews, press or awards [Runneth-Scripts, UGCSocial] | Creator over a 5-star review wall |
| **Ad inception / native UI** | Phone-in-phone, scroll, swipe to the site as CTA [Savannah-24] | — |
| **Offer-first banner / static-to-video** | Text and offer in frame 1; slow, consistent pace (OOFOS ran several [Motion-OOFOS]) | BFCM bundle |
| **ASMR** | No music or VO, process sounds only. "Extremely high watch times" [Savannah-24] | Box, tissue, insole flex, Velcro |

| Placement | Sources | WearOrtho default *(inference)* |
|---|---|---|

## 4. Pacing and scene changes

| Element | Rule | Source / note |
|---|---|---|
| Dead air | Cut any pause over ~0.3 s; trimming a 30 s cut this way typically drops it to ~24 s | [Billo-Edit] |
| B-roll shot length | 1–2 s, or 3 s with heavy motion | [Recharm] |
| B-roll shot length (alt) | 0.8–1.7 s; "if you're itching for the next clip, it's too late" | [Gattozzi] |
| Text-bearing shots | Minimum ~3 s between frames so text is legible | [Savannah-200]. **Conflict:** in hook/trend formats she keeps most scenes under ~1 s [Savannah-30]. Resolution *(inference)*: fast cuts are fine under a persistent text layer; just don't change the *text* faster than it can be read |
| Visual change cadence | Something new every 2–3 s (cut, angle, zoom, B-roll, graphic) | [Segwise-Anatomy, Influee-Raw, Moonb]. Talking-head cutaway every 3–4 s as one option [Search: Foreplay/IMPACT] |
| B-roll inserts in a 30 s UGC ad | 6–10 cutaways of 1–2 s each; TikTok cuts faster than Meta | [Influee-B] |
| Max static hold | ~5 s only if there's lots of action | [Gattozzi] |
| Talking-head jump cuts | Tighten breaths and pauses; hide cuts with B-roll or punch-in/out zooms | [Recharm, Gattozzi, NoFilmSchool] |
| Punch-in size | ~8–15% scale jump (≈108–115%), ease-in under 12 frames | [Pippit] (vendor). Punch in on key words [Savannah-200] |
| First shot change | Early, e.g. 1.2 s in | [VidPros] |
| Value/demo shown | By s 7–8 | [VidPros] |
| Listicle reasons | Under 4 s each (TikTok) | [TikTok-Guide] |
| Re-hook cadence | New reason to keep watching every 5–15 s | [UGCSocial] |
| Text on screen | As long as it takes to read aloud | [Recharm] |
| **Older audience (45–75)** | Slower pacing and more breathing room; slower-talking talent is OK; avoid fast editing | [Recharm, Fluxnote, SmartMarketer, Inbeat-Seniors]. Recharm notes exceptions both ways. Test it |

**WearOrtho pacing spec *(inference, blending the above for 45–75)*:**
- B-roll: 1.5–2.5 s.
- Shot or visual change: every 2–4 s.
- Each caption chunk held for its full read-aloud time.
- Hook section can run faster (≤1 s cuts) under a fixed headline.
- Testimonial A-roll can hold 4–6 s if the speaker is emotional, with a slow 3–5% push-in.

**Cut technique checklist**
- Cut on action: a foot stepping in, a hand pulling the insole [Foreplay].
- Use J/L cuts to smooth jump cuts and to let VO lead into B-roll [NoFilmSchool].
- Use audio micro-fades on cut words [NoFilmSchool].
- Make bad footage move: punch in/out, boomerang, flip, fake whip pan, split screen, green screen, TikTok UI frame, pop-in graphics [Recharm].
- AI rescue: auto color/white balance, Premiere Enhance Speech, Topaz for shaky clips, Runway motion brush for static shots [Recharm].

## 5. B-roll: what to show for each beat

Rules:
- Show the product whenever it's named; demonstrate every feature you describe; match the emotion of each beat [Useclip-B].
- Layer B-roll over A-roll so the creator's audio continues and hides the cuts [Influee-B].
- Record real sound during B-roll (Velcro, box, footsteps) [Influee-B].
- Shot list for a 30 s ad: 20–30 shots, ~10 s each, 2–3 angles per action [Influee-B].
- Shoot B-roll before A-roll, while the box is still unopened [Influee-B].

| Beat | B-roll types | WearOrtho shot list |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook** | Pattern interrupt, unusual angle, prop, reaction, before/after split | Shoe drop/bounce slow-mo; floor-level steps over lens; post-it on fridge; nurse clocking out at 7am; split-screen worn old heel vs new |
| **Problem** | Relatable everyday struggle (first-person, no medical imagery addressed to "you") | Kicking off shoes in the car; sitting on a bed edge in the morning; pausing at the kitchen counter; leaning on a shopping cart; a "shoe graveyard" closet of abandoned pairs |
| **Agitate** | Escalation, time-passing, cost of the problem | Clock time-lapse on a shift; skipped walk with friends (empty trail); stacks of insoles and inserts already bought |
| **Mechanism / USP** | Close-ups, texture, cutaway graphics or 3D, comparison tests | Thumb press-and-rebound on insole; arch profile close-up with an animated line; wide toe box top-down with a toe-splay overlay; insole pulled out and flexed; sole twist test vs a flimsy flat (split screen) |
| **Demo** | Product working in real context | Slip-on with pull tab (no hands); walking on hospital linoleum or concrete; stairs; standing at a nurses' station; grandkid chasing in the park; 10,000-step watch screenshot |
| **Lifestyle / identity** | Aspirational but real, in the target age band | Women 50–75 at a farmers market, travel, wedding outfit, church, pickleball; nurse in scrubs. Don't cast a decade younger than the buyer [Inbeat-Seniors] |
| **Social proof** | Review screenshots, multi-creator stack, press logos, numbers | Real 5-star reviews (age/role visible), 3–5 creator mashup ("I forgot I had them on"), real press logos only, real "pairs sold" counts |
| **Offer** | Bundle shot, guarantee badge, sizes/widths grid | Flat lay of 3 colorways; "Free exchanges" badge; widths grid; gift box |
| **CTA** | Phone screen-record of site, hand tapping, product hero | Screen-record of the PDP and size picker; soft lo-fi CTA for UGC. "A salesy CTA ruins the organic aesthetic" [Runneth-UGC] |

**Stock vs creator footage:** creator or phone footage is the default. Polished cutaways underperform phone-filmed content [Segwise-Anatomy, Segwise-5]. Use stock only for neutral establishing shots, and never stock people presented as customers *(inference + §11)*.

## 7. Transitions, motion and SFX

| Device | When to use | When it hurts | SFX pairing | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hard cut** | Default for everything | Never | None | — |
| **Jump cut + punch-in (8–15%)** | Talking head, emphasis words | Every sentence feels robotic | None or a soft "tick" | [Recharm, Savannah-200, Pippit] |
| **Whip pan (real or faked)** | Location or outfit change, energizing dead footage | Older-audience testimonial (disorienting) | Short whoosh, peak on the motion | [Recharm, SFX-search] |
| **Zoom/push reveal** | Product reveal, mechanism close-up | Stacked back-to-back | Low pop/hit on landing | [SFX-search] |
| **Split screen** | Us vs them, before/after, face + feet | Small screens get unreadable; keep ≤2 panes | None | [Recharm, Runneth-Scripts, Motion-OOFOS] |
| **Green screen** | Reviews, press, awards behind creator | Low-res backgrounds | None | [Runneth-Scripts] |
| **Stop-motion / reverse / phone-drop / magic tap** | Hook only, trend formats | Body of a 55+ ad | Pop/whoosh | [Savannah-30, Savannah-24] |
| **Pop-in text/graphics** | Key benefit, stat, price | Every caption popping | Light "pop" on 2–3 key moments max | [Recharm, SFX-search] |
| **Glitch / heavy transitions** | "Innovation" brands | Comfort/health brand for 45–75 *(inference)* | — | [Recharm] (music analogy) |
| **Animated CTA / site scroll** | Hi-fi ads | Lo-fi UGC (feels salesy) | Click / soft ding | [Runneth-UGC] |
| **Smooth/seamless transitions** | Polished edits | Over-polish signals "ad" | — | [Billo-Help, Cooper-Hott] |

**SFX rules**
- No more than 3 SFX per 30 s by default [Clip-Audio].
- Only to mark a change: transition, reveal, text pop, CTA [Clip-Audio, SFX-search].
- Don't repeat the same whoosh on every cut [SFX-search].
- Keep SFX very low under speech, and duck them under the voice [SFX-search].
- Every SFX should have a visual cue (flash, motion accent, text pop) for muted viewers [VidPros].

## 9. Iteration, versioning and editor–strategist workflow

### 9.1 Workflow (Gattozzi's 12 steps plus Savannah's brief format)
1. The strategist briefs. Savannah's brief has columns for **VO line | on-screen caption | suggested visual** per version, plus a link to the inspiration ad with a "why" comment [Savannah-200].
2. Organize and tag footage by attribute (product, shot type) so you can search it [Gattozzi]. Recharm's tool indexes at shot/moment level [Recharm-YT].
3. A-roll to script → B-roll per line → tighten pacing and movement → captions and text → music → SFX → color → export [Gattozzi].
4. Get a **fast V1 to review**. Don't polish before feedback [Gattozzi]. **Lock V1 before versions or resizes** [Recharm].
5. Review in passes:
   - Watch once with no notes: does it persuade?
   - Visuals match intent (timestamped comments).
   - Talking fluidity.
   - Re-check comments.
   - Captions, text, color, music.

   Use Frame.io, Air or Dropbox Replay [Gattozzi, Savannah-200, Runneth-UGC].
6. Resize to 4:5 and 1:1 only after sign-off. Export all formats for the media buyer [Gattozzi].
7. Naming, following Pawliw's pattern: `[type]-[iteration]-[editor]-[product]`, e.g. `video-iter1-UGC-backpain` [Pawliw-100k]. For WearOrtho *(inference)*: `WO_[concept]_[hookID]_[bodyID]_[len]s_[ratio]_[editor]_v#`.
8. Cadence: film Mon–Tue, edit Wed–Thu, deliver Fri. Weekly Motion review of drop-off points [Savannah-200]. Strategists should join editor meetings bi-weekly and share dashboards [Recharm].

### 9.2 Iteration menu
- **Hook swap:** same body, 3–5 new openings, each with a different *visual* [Billo-Edit, Savannah-200].
- **Body swap:** keep the winning hook and change the problem framing, proof stack or offer. Rebuild only the weakest module [Inbeat].
- **One variable at a time on a winner:** a different creator with the same script, a new clip order, or a new length [Savannah-200].
- **Reshoot with different creators:** different age, ethnicity or job (nurse vs teacher vs retiree). Show persona cues in the opening visual [Cooper-Iter].
- **Headline bars:** add a 4:5 headline bar to winners and test messaging. Test the copy as statics first [Cooper-Iter, Cooper-search].
- **Mashup:** keep the winning hook and weave in other creators [Cooper-Iter, Inbeat].
- **Format repurpose:** motion graphics, 3–5 slide carousel, GIF, static from the hook or problem [Cooper-Iter].
- **Length ladder:** 15 / 30 / 60–90 s [Billo-Edit, Pawliw-Search].
- **Effort scales with spend:** a $500k winner deserves more iterations than a $50k one. Don't pre-set a 75/25 new/iteration split [Cooper-Iter]. Nikhil suggests refreshing every 2–3 weeks at 50% new / 30% refined / 20% proven [Nikhil]. TikTok says refresh every 2–4 weeks [TikTok-Guide].
- **Volume:** 15–25 active variants per campaign beat fewer than 5 [Billo-Edit]. Pawliw: 5–10 new ads per week, test cap = daily budget ÷ target CPA [Pawliw-NoBS, Pawliw-100k].
- **Judging:**
  - Hott tests on the broadest, coldest audience and wants ~20 purchases before a call [Hott-PEC].
  - Pawliw gives an ad set ≥7 days [Pawliw-100k].
  - Kill signal: CPA 25%+ over target for 48–72 h [Runneth-Metrics].
  - Inbeat: don't judge mashups on average CPA; compare marginal cost at matched spend [Inbeat].

### 9.3 Andromeda / similarity
- Meta now matches creative to people. Diversity means different concepts, angles, formats and personas, not tweaks [Loomer].
- Same footage recut, new captions, or the same talent/set with a new script all probably collapse into one Entity ID [Nikhil].
- Similarity-score thresholds (40% / 60%) are unofficial heuristics [Search-Andromeda].

## 10. Metrics → what the editor changes

Definitions:
- **Meta hook rate** = 3-s plays ÷ impressions.
- **Meta hold rate** = ThruPlays ÷ 3-s plays (ThruPlay = 15 s or completion).
- **TikTok hook rate** = 2-s views ÷ impressions; TikTok hold = 6-s ÷ 2-s views [Billo-HR].
- **Watch out:** some tools (and Pawliw) define hold as 15-s plays ÷ *impressions*, which gives a much smaller number [Pawliw-100k, Billo-HR].
- Dara Denney calls hold rate flawed because ThruPlay triggers at 15 s, so cross-check average watch time [Denney-search].
- Score hooks by **hook rate × hold rate**: 28% × 30% = 8.4% beats 40% × 15% = 6% [Flowjam].

| Metric | Benchmarks (Meta unless noted) | If low, the editor changes… |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook rate** | Avg 25.4% (H1 2026, 88k ads); Health & Beauty 28.1%; pause <21% [Billo-HR]. Target 30–40%, <25% = creative problem [Runneth-Metrics]. 40%+ elite [Segwise-Anatomy] | First frame, first text line, first VO line (one at a time) [Billo-HR]. Add a pattern interrupt (smash cut, zoom, prop), a face with eye contact, bold text, native cues. Move the best shot to frame 1 [Recharm] |
| **Hold rate** | 40–50% avg, 60%+ strong [Runneth-Metrics, Segwise-Anatomy]. Billo uses 25% as its pacing alarm. Compare to your account median | Pacing: cut slow beats right after the hook, reach the product sooner, add interrupts after s 3, make s 4–10 deliver the hook's promise, add proof [Billo-HR, Flowjam] |
| **Avg watch time / retention curve** | ~50% of duration [Runneth-Metrics]; <50% at 10 s = context/reveal problem [Segwise-Anatomy] | Trim at the drop-off timestamp; re-hook every 5–15 s; shorten |
| **Completion** | 30–40% strong [Runneth-Metrics] | Shorter cut; move CTA earlier (Reels: visible from ~20 s [Segwise-Anatomy]) |
| **CTR (link)** | Meta 0.9–1.5% avg, >1.5% strong; TikTok ~0.84% avg, >1% good [Runneth-Metrics]. Fail signal <1% Meta [Segwise-Anatomy] | Stronger offer and CTA (verbal + on screen ≥3 s), clearer mechanism, headline bar, add social proof. A good hook with a weak CTR means the hook over-promises [Billo-HR] |
| **CVR** | Meta ~8–9% [Runneth-Metrics] | Usually not the edit. Check ad ↔ landing-page congruence (Pawliw: congruent LP 10% vs generic 1% [Pawliw-NoBS]). Denney calls exact matching a myth [Denney-search]. Make sure the ad's claims, offer and price match the LP |
| **CPA / ROAS** | 25%+ over target for 48–72 h = kill [Runneth-Metrics] | Diagnose upstream metrics first. Hott: "Why did it fail? Can I adjust it?" [Hott-PEC] |
| **Frequency ↑ + performance ↓** | Rotate when frequency is over ~2.5, or every ~14 days [VidPros] | New hooks/visual openings or a new concept; fresh footage beats more recuts [Inbeat] |

Cooper warns against "glorifying" hook and hold rates. Scale on CPA/ROAS and use attention metrics to diagnose [Cooper-Motion, Search-HookHold].

## 11. Compliance for foot-pain claims (Meta and TikTok)

*Not legal advice. Third-party summaries of Meta and TikTok policy; verify against the current Meta Advertising Standards (Health & Wellness, Personal Attributes) before launch.*

**Rules**
- **Personal attributes.** Copy can't assert or imply that the viewer has a health condition. The main trigger is "you/your" plus a condition, and the March 2026 update reportedly also flags *implied symptoms*. Don't pair condition imagery with copy that addresses the viewer [AuditSocials, ADM-Health].
- **No cure, heal, treat or eliminate claims.** Describe design and comfort ("arch support designed for all-day comfort"), not outcomes. Avoid "guaranteed", "100%", "proven to", "clinically proven" without a study [AuditSocials, ADM-Health].
- **Before/after.** Avoid transformation framing on the body. Product or lifestyle shots are safer [ADM-Health, AuditSocials]. *(inference)* "Old shoe vs new shoe" product comparisons are lower risk than body or feet transformations.
- **Body shaming.** No negative statements about appearance ("ugly feet", "stop hiding your bunions") [ADM-Health].
- **Sensational framing.** Avoid "secret / they don't want you to know", fake urgency (resetting timers, fake "only 3 left"), and fake news or editorial styling [AuditSocials].
- **Authority.** Never imply credentials the creator doesn't have (scrubs + "as a doctor…") [UGCSocial]. AI "patients" or fabricated testimonials are a compliance risk [Search-Podiatry].
- **Street interviews or staged UGC.** Viewers may not realize the people are paid; get legal review and disclosure [NetInf-Street].
- **Substantiation.** Keep evidence for every number (e.g. OOFOS "absorbs 37% more impact" is a mechanism claim with a test behind it [Motion-OOFOS]). The landing page must match the ad's claims, offer and price [AuditSocials].

| Risky | Safer rewrite |
|---|---|
| "Do you suffer from plantar fasciitis?" | "Shoes designed with firm arch support for long days on your feet." |
| "Your bunions will thank you." | "A wide, roomy toe box, with no pinching at the sides." |
| "Cure your heel pain in 7 days." | "Cushioned heel designed for all-day comfort." |
| "Before: limping. After: pain-free!" | Split screen of a worn-out old shoe vs WearOrtho; "Hour 12 still feels like hour 1" (first-person, from a real customer) |
| "These cured my heel pain!" (testimonial) | "I forgot I had them on halfway through my shift." (product experience) |
| "Stop hiding your ugly swollen feet." | "Supportive shoes in three widths, that actually look cute." |
| "Doctor-recommended" (unsubstantiated) | Only with a real, documented, disclosed endorsement |

*(inference)* First-person creator statements ("My heels used to ache by noon") read as testimony rather than a claim about the viewer, so they're generally lower risk than second-person copy. Still no medical-outcome promises. Keep the on-screen headline general.

## 12. Pre-export QC checklist

- [ ] Frame 1 has a specific visual, a text hook and the start of the VO. Product visible by ~2 s on demos.
- [ ] Best shot is in the first 3 s, and repeated later if it's a stunner [Recharm].
- [ ] No pause over 0.3 s; no static shot over ~4 s without motion.
- [ ] Every claim has matching B-roll at that moment (say it, show it).
- [ ] Captions burned in, 72–84 px Bold, stroke or box, one zone, inside the universal safe zone. Checked against TikTok and Meta overlay templates.
- [ ] Watched on a phone, full-length, **sound OFF**, 1–2× for typos and comprehension [Savannah-200, Inbeat-Seniors].
- [ ] Watched on headphones for levels: music 15–25% under VO, ≤3 SFX/30 s, no lyric clash [Clip-Audio, Savannah-200].
- [ ] CTA spoken plus on screen ≥3 s; offer and price match the landing page.
- [ ] Compliance pass (§11): no "you + condition", no cure/treat words, no body before/after, credentials real, numbers substantiated.
- [ ] Key content inside the centre 4:5 (y 285–1635) for feed crops.
- [ ] Advantage+ auto-captions/music/overlays off where they'd double up [VidPros, Pawliw-100k].
- [ ] Named per convention; V1 approved before versions [Recharm].
- [ ] "Take Pride" check: would you publish it? [Savannah-200]
