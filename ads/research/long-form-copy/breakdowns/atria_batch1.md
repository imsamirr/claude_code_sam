## 1. The master template (with typical % positions)
1. **Hook + promise (0–5%)**: a confession, call-out, or photo callout, often with a stated payoff ("by the end you'll be furious", "Last weekend, I shut him up").
2. **Identity + stakes (5–12%)**: age, years in a role, who depends on them. Ages cluster at 44–72.
3. **Escalation / life shrinking (10–30%)**: concrete losses (kid's game, trip, photo, dinner table, Thursday games). Usually 2–4 losses, each planted for a later payoff.
4. **Failed solutions catalogue (15–30%)**: named products and treatments, with dollar totals ("$800... two car payments", "$150 a month across six containers", "$2,000"). Doctors give dismissive one-liners ("rest", "cut back on salt", "normal for your age").
5. **Rock bottom (20–35%)**: a sitting-in-the-car or couch moment ("sat in my car in the parking garage for a long time"). This appears in 8+ ads.
6. **Stranger/insider encounter (30–50%)**: an *older or adjacent* expert, rarely the narrator's own doctor: the 38-yr nurse mother-in-law, Teresa at pickup, Sal the 83-yr shoemaker, a sporting-goods kid, the prep cook's sister, the nurse sister Maya, Deb the ex-hygienist, Dr. Voss at the conference, a Reddit comment 40 deep.
7. **Mechanism reveal (35–60%)**: the "real cause" plus why every past fix failed. This is the longest single block (15–25% of copy).
8. **Villain (optional, 40–60%)**: the industry re-skinning shoes, "a billion-dollar industry", cheap Cassia, Amazon fakes. Doctors are framed as well-meaning but downstream, not as evil.
9. **Product bridge (45–70%, median ~62%)**.
10. **Results timeline (65–90%)**: week 1 "nothing" → week 2–3 first sign → week 4–8 a payoff of each planted loss → month 3 doctor confirmation.
11. **Callback close (85–95%)**: the opening image reversed (the peas back in the freezer, the second drawing, "She asked for Iris", "I just walked into the room").
12. **Offer / guarantee / P.S. (90–100%)**.

## 2. Hook formulas that recur
- **Shameful domestic image**: "The bag of frozen peas on my feet was supposed to be my kids' dinner"; "Mine drew me on the couch with two green squares on my feet". The image is usually what the static photo shows.
- **Photo-misdirection**: "No. Nobody punched me. I know what this photo looks like." (Helios)
- **"Stop doing X" specialist**: "I'm a diabetes specialist, and here are 3 things you need to stop doing"; "Please STOP sizing up your tennis shoes."; "I've been an edema specialist for 19 years... going to OUTRAGE you."
- **"By the end you'll be furious" + numbered 3-point preview**: identical in Sculptique and NovaPod ("there are three things happening right now. One..."). A shared swipe template.
- **Relationship mystery**: "my wife stopped riding to Saturday open play with me"; "I made her quitting comfortable".
- **Revenge**: "For 11 months, my ex told our friends I was 'struggling'... Last weekend, I shut him up."
- **Scam reversal**: "SNATCHED IS A SCAM. That was the title of the post I wrote last month."
- **Interactive task**: "Right now. Go grab them... Now flip them over." / headline "Flip your shoe over and look at the sole 👇"
- **Years of hidden shame + wrong-organ twist**: "I spent 16 years turning my head... I'd been treating the wrong organ."
- Profanity as a pattern interrupt appears in 2 ads ("I don't give a f*ck if this upsets you", "f*cken done").

## 3. Open-loop patterns
- **Plant/payoff ledger**: 2–4 specific losses introduced early and each closed by name in the results (OrthoFit PF: farmers market → game → Portugal; Orthora: "You always say maybe" → "You didn't say maybe"; CUURT: the Gordon fence pause; Helios: probe numbers 5-5 → 3-3).
- **Answer location named but withheld**: "the truth turned up on a shelf in our garage" (held ~50%).
- **Numbered promises**: "three things" / "two problems got in my way" / "3 things you need to stop".
- **"Remember that. It matters later."** and "you'll see why": explicit IOUs.
- **Saved emotional card in the P.S.** (Metabolae father's death; Helios "something did do this to my face").
- Presells may **never close** the product loop (Morning Vitality's unnamed mineral, NovaPod with no brand name). The click closes it.

## 4. Mechanism patterns
- **Hidden-location mechanism**: the problem lives where your current tool can't reach (below the gumline, inside porous acrylic, in the gut 30 ft from the mouth, in the shape of the toe box, upstream of the kidneys). Line types: "Geography."; "cleaning the exhaust pipe... The engine was thirty feet south"; "Everything in that bag worked around her foot. Nothing in it worked on the shoe."
- **"Treating the symptom while wearing the cause"** / faucet vs drain / flood vs pipe / ceiling stain vs attic: there is always an *upstream* reframe.
- **Explain the reader's past failure with the mechanism**: foam rebounds overnight so "you're restarting the clock"; creatine bloat = missing potassium; sizing up = "a running start into that same point"; more CPAP pressure = more contaminants.
- **One everyday analogy** that everyone has physically experienced: kitchen sponge, pool float with a slow leak, camera tripod, flashlight through fingers, greasy pan, fever spike averaged out.
- **Free self-test** the reader can do tonight: thumb in the heel, trace the foot + lay the insole on top, stand on the insole, check the creatine label for sodium/magnesium, flip the shoe over, smell test, "let them read you the numbers."
- **Ingredient ↔ mechanism 1:1 mapping** for supplements (NOURI 3 sources/3 ingredients; Helios red=tissue/blue=bacteria; Metabolae species/concentration/delivery).

## 5. Bridge patterns
- **Late EDIT reveal** (Orthora): the story is brand-free; "EDIT: A lot of you are asking what shoes... one small American company called Orthora" at ~95%. This mimics an organic post.
- **Criteria-then-product** (~60%): the narrator defines what a solution must do, then "Then I found NOURI" / "Metabolae is built to get all three right" / "The only pair that passed was CUURT."
- **Object handed over** (Kaori bar from the canvas bag; the CUURT box on the garage shelf; the NovaPod with a note on the vanity).
- **Peer-group discovery** (Facebook group, Reddit, prep cook): "the same device kept coming up, comment after comment."
- **Brand-first** only when the brand *is* the conflict (Snatched scam reversal) or for short brand-voice ads (PAP MD, ~50%).
- **Standard bridge sentence**: "I looked into different brands and found one small American company called X that does it the right way." / "What she plays in now is the X." / "It's called X, and it's the only formula I've found built as a complete system."

## 6. Narrator archetypes
Witness spouse (CUURT husband ×2, NovaPod wife), sufferer-mom (Orthora ×3, Helios, OrthoFit PF), sister-as-advocate (Sculptique), specialist whistleblower (Metabolae, Morning Vitality, CUURT surgeon), founder (CUURT), redeemed critic (Snatched), divorced woman seeking vindication (Kaori), anonymous user (PAP MD). Even the expert narrators usually get *taught* by an older or outsider expert. That humility beat is close to universal.

## 7. Close patterns
- Guarantee framed as a **trial on real life** ("45 days to wear them on real shifts"), or length justified by the mechanism (Metabolae: 90 days because the "biology" needs 90).
- BOGO "one for the court, one for everything else" (CUURT), "costs less than a bottle of perfume" (Kaori), "less than the consult" (Helios).
- Soft scarcity ("small batches", "clinical grade LEDs", "I'm not saying that to pressure you").
- P.S. = homework (do the test tonight) or share-to-loved-one ("To the daughters reading this", "If a woman you love has started smiling with her mouth closed").
- Several end with **no offer**, only a click to the advertorial (NOURI 144d, Morning Vitality 106d, NovaPod, OrthoFit PF).

## 8. Style norms
One sentence per line dominates (Orthora, NovaPod, Snatched, Morning Vitality). Medium paragraphs suit older-male or literary voices (CUURT husbands, Kaori, Helios). Reading level is about grade 4–7. Dialogue is heavy and often reported without quotes. Callbacks are written as one-word lines ("Bleed.", "Years.", "Geography."). Typos and British spellings are left in, which reads as authentic. Disclaimers appear in the better brands ("This story is a dramatization...", "Let me be careful here. I'm the husband.", "I'm not a doctor"). An honest bad week 1 ("Week one, she didn't love them"; "Nothing happened... I nearly boxed it up") appears in 6+ ads and builds trust.

## 9. Length norms
- Range 2.1k–16.6k chars, with most at **10–14k (~1,900–2,700 words)**.
- Short variants (2–6k) work when the landing page carries the story (NOURI 5.6k, 144 days; Orthora short cut 2.1k; PAP MD 3k).
- The brand tests long and short versions of the **same story** side by side (Orthora 2.1k vs 5.4k vs 7k, all launched the same day).

## 10. Long-running vs short-lived
- **Longest runners (100+ days)**: NOURI, PAP MD, Morning Vitality, Kaori. They share (a) **one simple, upstream mechanism** stated in a sentence (wrong organ / dirty air / zombie cells / an oil soap can't touch), (b) **an ending that sends the click** (an advertorial or an offer headline), not a hard sell in-body, (c) moderate length (3–11k), and (d) little or no fake hard data. Kaori and NOURI are emotional/social (shame, intimacy) more than clinical.
- **Top rank but shorter-lived**: OrthoFit exposé (rank 1, 28 days, ended) and Sculptique (rank 1, 30 days). These are aggressive "furious/scam/industry" angles that spike and burn out (or hit compliance limits). The hard-sell conspiracy framing (fabricated podiatrist forum) seems to burn fastest.
- **CUURT** (12–28 days, all top 4% of the brand's 400 ads) is the most crafted, honest-toned set. It rotates POVs (husband, husband, surgeon, founder) on **one shared mechanism and offer block**. That is the modular approach worth copying: swap the narrator and hook, and keep the mechanism, objections, and offer identical.
- Unknown/new: Orthora (4 days) and NovaPod (0 days) aren't proven yet.

## 11. Compliance flags to avoid copying literally
Fake doctor personas (Metabolae, Morning Vitality, CUURT "Dr. Neil Hartman"), fabricated forum quotes (OrthoFit), disease-treatment claims (diabetes, edema "gone in 2 days"), and a domestic-violence misdirection image (Helios). The structural craft transfers. These specific claims do not.
