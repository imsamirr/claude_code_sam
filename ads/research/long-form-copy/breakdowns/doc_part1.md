## Ad 1 — NovaPod denture cleaner ("Example 1")
**ID:** NovaPod (mynovapod.com/pages/advertorial0), ultrasonic + UV-C denture cleaner. ~1,740 words / ~10.6k chars. Ad ID, run days and status: n/a. Australian cues: "called 000", "GP", "dressing gown".

**Narrator/POV:** A furious wife of a 74-year-old denture wearer, writing in first person. Her credibility comes from being a witness rather than an expert. A third party carries the expert authority: "a young doctor doing the evening round" first, then "a prosthodontist" who "built something to close it".

**Hook:** "I'm fucking furious writing this. But my husband said if it stops even one person going through what we went through, it's worth it." The ad then cuts straight to "a hospital corridor at 2 in the morning... after he collapsed in our back garden." It plants the villain object at once: "that box of tablets on our bathroom shelf. The one we trusted for eleven years." **Type:** profane rage confession + a cold open on the crisis + an indicted everyday object.

**Beat map:**
- 0–5%: hook and crisis cold open.
- 5–17%: "Let me back up." Symptoms build up (exhausted, stiff joints, metallic taste, sore gums). Failed fixes: GP says "bloods came back fine", plus fish oil, turmeric and magnesium.
- 17–20%: Rock bottom: "on one knee in the dirt, grey in the face."
- 20–24%: Hospital "all clear", then the young doctor's question.
- 24–40%: Mechanism of the problem: porous acrylic, biofilm, why the tablet fails, "denture stomatitis".
- 40–50%: Villain: "If they told us the real solution upfront... We would buy it once and be done."
- 50–60%: Discovery: "Ultrasonic cavitation... UV-C". The prosthodontist "built something".
- 60–72%: Demo ("Cloudy. Yellowish. Particles floating") and a timeline running from week one to month two.
- 72–86%: Objection: "Most devices sold cheaply... are built for jewellery... glowing blue LED".
- 86%: Product named.
- 86–92%: Sister testimonial.
- 92–100%: "I am not selling anything" and a "Do not wait..." anaphora close.

**Open loops:**
1. "that box of tablets... not doing what we thought" is opened in the first lines and closed at 24–40% ("It cannot penetrate into the acrylic").
2. "asked a question that nobody else had thought to ask in eleven years" is closed in the next line, "How does he clean his dentures."
3. "what she told me made me want to put my fist through the wall" opens the mechanism.
4. "Before you go looking for an ultrasonic cleaner I need to tell you something important" is a late loop that sets up the differentiation.

**Mechanism:**
- Problem: "Denture acrylic is porous... microscopic scratches... biofilm... A structured colony anchored inside the material itself." The tablet "works on the surface".
- Solution: ultrasonic cavitation that dislodges biofilm "from within", plus "true UV-C... around 253 nanometres", run at "42,000 pulses per second".
- Simplification: surface versus inside, and a dissolving tablet versus physical dislodging.

**Product bridge:** The category arrives at about 54%: "I kept reading until I found what actually works. The solution dental clinics have been using for years." The brand name appears very late, at about 86%: "It is called the NovaPod." It is named only after the cheap alternatives have been disqualified.

**Proof/specificity:** 74 years old; eleven years; "Twenty something dollars a box"; 253nm; 42,000 pulses; "Five minutes"; "Gone by day four"; the sister's nine years. Sensory details: "metallic taste", "grey in the face", "the garden had gone swimmy".

**Emotional engine:** Anger and betrayal, then fear, then vindication and relief. The turn is the dirty-water demo: "That was in my mouth this morning."

**Style:** Short declarative sentences and fragments ("Not loudly. Just once. Flat. Wrong."). Heavy anaphora: "Everything the dentist told him. Everything the box said." and "While the biofilm built. While the soreness built." Light dialogue. Grade 5–6 reading level.

**Close:** There is no offer or guarantee. The CTA sends readers to the advertorial: "There is a full explanation of what is actually living inside the material... Read it today. Click below." A "Do not wait for..." triple carries the urgency.

**Steal this:** Indict a trusted everyday ritual ("the tablet every night"). Then make the reader see the proof with a dirty-water reveal from an item that had just been "cleaned".

## Ad 2 — Rosabella beetroot ("Variation 2", pharmacist)
**ID:** Rosabella organic beetroot capsules, BOGO landing page (tryrosabella.com). ~5,100 words / ~28.9k chars. Ad ID, run days and status: n/a.

**Narrator/POV:** "Karen Albrecht, 54... licensed pharmacist for 27 years — the last 19 at a Rite Aid in Harrisburg". She is a whistleblowing insider. Credibility rests on numbers ("340 statin prescriptions a week. I counted once on a slow Tuesday in March"), on training ("4 years of pharmacology"), on database access ("PubMed. Cochrane.") and on a personal risk ("could cost me professionally").

**Hook:** "I fill 340 statin prescriptions a week. I won't fill my own. Let me tell you why I drive 45 minutes to an Amish farm in Lancaster County instead." **Type:** insider-hypocrisy paradox + a curiosity destination (an Amish farm).

**Beat map:**
- 0–8%: Hook and identity.
- 8–15%: Counter ritual, then the patient decline in stages: "three months later... six months later... a year later". Climax: "Deceased... 14 times in the last three years".
- 15–20%: Named casualty Gerald Nowak, with "LDL... 131... 'well-controlled'... Gerald was dead."
- 20–24%: Her own bad labs (LDL 172).
- 24–30%: Mechanism 1: the statin's "switch" also makes CoQ10.
- 30–33%: Refusal scene with her doctor: "I never filled it."
- 33–38%: Mechanism 2: oxidized LDL (the avocado analogy).
- 38–45%: Research. Betalain is introduced as a "sniper instead of a shotgun".
- 45–50%: Flaw-hunting. "The catch isn't betalain. The catch is the beet." This covers sugar-bred beets and spray-drying.
- 50–56%: Heirloom Blutwurzel, the drive to Lancaster, and the Amish son.
- 56%: "That company is Rosabella."
- 56–62%: Results: LDL 172 to 124 in 8 weeks.
- 62–66%: "Five things. All five." checklist.
- 66–75%: Family and coworker proof; "23 people... Average LDL reduction: 38 points".
- 75–85%: Risk/mission restated; offer.
- 85–100%: Six P.S.s.

**Open loops:**
1. "why I drive 45 minutes to an Amish farm" is opened at 0% and closed at about 52%. It is the longest loop.
2. "what I'm about to tell you could change everything."
3. "Let me tell you about one of them" is closed by the Gerald story.
4. "Here's what they don't put on the patient label" is closed by CoQ10.
5. "I know something she doesn't about what's actually killing them" is closed by oxidation.
6. "And I found it" (the flaw) is closed by "The catch is the beet."

The loops are stacked so that each mechanism reveal opens the next question.

**Mechanism:**
- Problem: the statin flips the "HMG-CoA reductase" switch, which also makes CoQ10 ("the spark plug in every cell"). Cholesterol only kills "when it oxidizes" (the cut-avocado analogy).
- Solution: betalain is "targeted... Like a sniper instead of a shotgun" and "crosses into the LDL particle itself".
- Second-order mechanism (why other beet products fail): sugar breeding "bred the betalain down", spray-drying kills it, and the shelf product is "Red-colored dust".
- Summary line: "Lower the number. Miss the damage. Drain the defense."

**Product bridge:** The ingredient (betalain) arrives at about 38%. The brand arrives at about 56% through a gatekeeper story: "They'd partnered with one company — only one — that agreed to their terms... That company is Rosabella." The scarcity and authenticity frame is built before the name appears.

**Proof/specificity:** 340/week; 14 deceased; Gerald, 63, "Pirates cap", "March 14th, 2024"; 1,300mg; Route 283 to 30; the "Turkey Hill gas station"; 10–20x betalain; her mother's LDL 146 to 94; Dave's 168 to 119; 23 people averaging 38 points; Wageningen and Bologna universities.

**Emotional engine:** Guilt and complicity ("I hate myself a little more every time"), then grief, then defiance. Vindication: "I don't need her to believe it. The lab printout believes it."

**Style:** Mixed paragraph length with heavy anaphora: "I see them... I see them...", "Not one of them..." and the tagline "I fill... I won't fill my own." Dialogue scenes with the doctor. Ritual callbacks: "take it with food if it bothers your stomach".

**Close:**
- Offer: BOGO, "Only through the one website the Lancaster County family authorized", and a "90-day money-back guarantee tied to your actual lab results."
- CTA: "please order a bottle. Not for me. For whoever's sitting across from you at the dinner table tonight."
- Sign-off: "— Karen A., RPh".
- Six P.S.s, each handling one objection: not anti-medication; already on a statin; a stubborn husband ("You just have to get the bottle in the house"); the guarantee; Amazon knockoffs; and a final scene with "Deborah, 62" new to statins.

**Steal this:** Make the narrator's own refusal the hook. "I sell X 340 times a week and won't take it myself" turns insider credibility into the whole argument.

## Ad 3 — Rosabella beetroot ("Variation 3", "Ghost" biker)
**ID:** Rosabella beetroot, tracked link (track.tryrosabella.com). ~8,740 words / ~47.3k chars, the longest in the set. Ad ID, run days and status: n/a.

**Narrator/POV:** Robert "Ghost" McAllister, 59, a welder and biker. He is estranged from the daughter his ex-wife took away 31 years ago. Expert authority is handed off to "Boone", a club member who is a "Retired paramedic. Twenty-two years on ambulances". Robert's own credibility comes from being a skeptic and from being burned once (the Amazon beet).

**Hook:** "My daughter spent 31 years believing I was a dangerous man. Last month she pulled me over for a broken taillight — and I prayed she wouldn't recognize me." **Type:** a human-interest melodrama hook unrelated to the product (a reunion story). Health is not mentioned until about 20%.

**Beat map:**
- 0–20%: The pure story: the traffic stop, the birthmark, the coffee-shop reveal, the grandchildren.
- 20–24%: Rock bottom: "I made it to the fourth step" carrying his granddaughter, then chest pressure.
- 24–26%: Daughter's ultimatum: "I'm not watching you die."
- 26–30%: Doctor: LDL 194, statin offered, refused.
- 30–34%: Villain backstory: Duke's hands ruined by rosuvastatin.
- 34–37%: Tremor scare at work; diet alone gives LDL 188.
- 37–50%: Callahan's bar and Boone's napkin lecture: oxidation ("rust"), the "wheel", the CoQ10 "spark", "carpet bomb vs smart missile".
- 50–58%: False solution: Amazon beetroot. He feels better but LDL is 191, then 189.
- 58–64%: Boone's diagnosis: "This isn't the same thing... Brown." Then the four-point checklist.
- 64–67%: Cross-referencing, then "Rosabella. Four for four."
- 67–75%: Crimson capsule test; "Seventeen minutes"; day-5, day-10 and week-2 results; LDL 151, then 139 at the doctor.
- 75–88%: Emotional payoff: "I'm glad you're okay, Dad"; Lily asleep on his chest.
- 88–93%: Moral: "The doctor watches the chart. The people who love you watch the man."
- 93–94%: Product summary and link.
- 94–100%: Five P.S.s.

**Open loops:**
1. The daughter not recognizing him, closed at about 9% ("I have a photo").
2. "borrowed time has a price. You have to be alive to spend it" opens the health story.
3. "I was saying no because of Duke" is closed by the Duke backstory.
4. "You want the real conversation or the one where I tell you to eat more fish?"
5. A false-resolution loop: the Amazon product "is working", then the "LDL: 191" rug-pull, closed by Boone's "Brown."
6. "Which one do you take?" / "I'll text you", which defers the brand name.

The father–daughter loop (whether she will call him Dad) runs the whole length and closes at about 85%.

**Mechanism:**
- Problem: a napkin drawing. "This is an LDL particle... free radicals... It rusts." Then the self-perpetuating "wheel" ("The rust drives you to overproduce"). The statin "bails water as fast as it can and never once finds the hole."
- CoQ10 "spark": "Same switch. You can't shut off one without the other."
- Solution: "Stop the rust. Leave the spark."
- Purchase criteria: a four-point checklist (strain, shade-dried, 1,300mg, lab report), plus a self-test ("deep crimson... If it's brown, the betalains are dead").

**Product bridge:** The ingredient arrives at about 51% ("It wasn't a drug. It was a pigment."). A generic version fails first (55–58%). The brand arrives at about 67% as the survivor of a checklist: "One brand kept coming up... Rosabella." This is the strongest "failed competitor" device in the set.

**Proof/specificity:** Highway 49; "six pounds eleven ounces"; "March 14th, 1993"; "a sixteenth of an inch"; LDL 194, 188, 191, 189, 151 and 139; "Seventeen minutes"; Callahan's bar; Snake, Tony and Boone.

**Emotional engine:** Loss and longing, then the fear of dying holding a child, then the betrayal of the false product, then vindication and love. The final payoff is the word "Dad."

**Style:** Novelistic, with heavy dialogue and short scene paragraphs. Callbacks run through the whole piece: the fourth step, the sixteenth of an inch, the crescent moon, the "fuzzy" beard.

**Close:**
- Expectation-setting device: "you'll feel it in the first 20 minutes... The rust work comes later, on the bloodwork."
- Safety disclaimer delivered through story: Duke came off his statin with his doctor.
- Women-buyer P.S.: "Don't ask. Tell him. 'Just take it. For me.'"
- Scarcity: "one heirloom seed line... When a season's batch is gone, it's gone."
- Offer: "buy 3 get 3 free... three for the person you've been thinking about this whole time", plus a 90-day guarantee.

**Steal this:** Insert a "cheap version failed" chapter with numbers that got worse. It inoculates the reader against Amazon alternatives and makes the brand the checklist survivor.

## Ad 4 — Alevia Amla ("Variation 4", librarian + labradoodle)
**ID:** Alevia Amla capsules (alevia.com/amla/33). ~4,030 words / ~20.9k chars. Ad ID, run days and status: n/a.

**Narrator/POV:** Maureen, 58, a widowed part-time children's librarian in Fairfield, CT. Her husband Pete died of heart trouble. She positions herself as "a librarian who reads too much", not a doctor. Authority transfers to a stranger, Elaine, a "retired... middle school science" teacher, and to her niece.

**Hook:** "I had a stretch of three good days in a row last spring and I almost forgot what was wrong with me. That's the part nobody warns you about. Not the bad days. The good ones." Then: "a labradoodle pulls you down a sidewalk... you sit on a stranger's stone wall for 22 minutes trying not to die." **Type:** a counterintuitive observation + a vivid, specific incident teaser.

**Beat map:**
- 0–10%: Hook and life details: Pete "sat down on the floor", the braided rug.
- 10–14%: The ad states its loops: "This story is about Charlie... a stone wall... what I learned in those 22 minutes".
- 14–30%: "Let me back up." BP climbs 142/88, 146/90, 148/92 while the doctor says "Let's keep watching." Life shrinks: the elevator, skipped diner trips, the abandoned garden.
- 30–40%: The Charlie incident and the 22 minutes on the wall.
- 40–52%: Elaine (the stranger encounter): "Amla."
- 52–62%: Mechanism lecture: "the number is a symptom... It's the smoke." Endothelium, nitric oxide, the garden-hose and wheel-well rust analogies; pills "paper over the symptom."
- 62–72%: "I'm not a doctor"; the research was "just sitting there".
- 72%: Product named.
- 72–85%: Results timeline: week 2 slope, week 3 stairs, week 5 garden, week 7 beach; sister Donna 138/86 to 122/78; doctor reads 124/80.
- 85–90%: Pete reveal: "Watching is what they call it when nobody does anything."
- 90–100%: CTA and three P.S.s.

**Open loops:**
1. "what I learned in those 22 minutes that I think saved my life", with "I'm getting ahead of myself" announcing the deferral. Closed at about 45%.
2. "I want to tell you the thing about Pete I haven't told you", a late loop opened at 85% and closed immediately: he was also told "keep watching".
3. "That sentence went into me like a hook" ("not waiting for the other shoe to drop") returns as the CTA line.

**Mechanism:**
- Problem: stiff vessels from oxidative damage to the endothelium, leading to less nitric oxide. "Kettle on the back burner" is the felt metaphor.
- Solution: amla antioxidants "support the lining."
- Claims are hedged compliantly: "has been associated with helping support healthy blood pressure already in the normal range."

**Product bridge:** Category ("Amla") at about 46%, through an incidental stranger. Brand at about 72%: "Three days after I met Elaine, I ordered a bottle of Alevia Amla. I picked Alevia because... sourced and tested in the U.S... 90 day money back guarantee." The brand is justified by its features, not by any magic.

**Proof/specificity:** BP series; "62 pounds. I'm 137"; "April 23rd"; "47" Beach Road; "white colonial with green shutters"; "Stop and Shop"; "The Very Hungry Caterpillar"; Donna's numbers; Elaine 118/76.

**Emotional engine:** Quiet dread and denial, then fear on the wall, then the hope given by a stranger, then joy (laughing alone in the foyer). Underneath runs grief and regret over Pete.

**Style:** Warm, conversational and gently funny ("dumb as a bag of doorknobs"). The refrain "Let's keep watching" serves as the villain line. Medium-length paragraphs.

**Close:**
- Offer: two capsules a day, 90-day guarantee.
- CTA: "You don't have to wait for the other shoe to drop."
- P.S. 1: Elaine's Ireland trip.
- P.S. 2: the guarantee reframed ("nobody offered to refund the years I lost").
- P.S. 3: a symptom checklist for a husband or sister ("taking the elevator she used to skip").

**Steal this:** Make the doctor's passivity the villain through a repeated line ("Let's keep watching"). Then pay it off with the reveal that the dead spouse heard the same words.

## Ad 5 — Alevia Amla ("Variation 5", paralegal daughter)

- 35–45%: Research mechanism: "not 14 separate problems... one problem with one root"; arterial lining; "smoke vs fire."
- 45–52%: Rejected options (Omega 3, beet root, hawthorn, CoQ10), then amla at about 52%.
- 52–58%: "Amla works on the fire, not the smoke."
- 58–62%: Brand: "a company in Texas called Alevia".
- 62–66%: Skepticism (turmeric from a TV ad, fish oil, a rash).
- 66–78%: Timeline: nothing for 2 weeks, almost quit at day 11, then sleep, rings, walking, and at week 9 "LDL had dropped 31 points... 124 over 79".
- 78–92%: Grave visit; "I am breaking the chain."

**Product bridge:** Ingredient at about 52%, through solo research. Brand at about 58%, through sourcing credentials ("family farms in India... test it independently in the United States").

**Emotional engine:** Grief, then defiance ("I am not doing what she did"), then hope, then generational duty.

**Style:** Plain, with Southern voice cues ("baby"), dialogue without quotation marks, and repetition of "She said, I know."

**Close:**
- Offer: 90-day guarantee ("the only reason I had the courage to start"), plus a human touch ("a real person in Texas who didn't rush me off the phone").
- P.S. 1: a responsible medical disclaimer.
- P.S. 2: the list now in her own Bible.
- P.S. 3: "If you have a daughter, please give her a bottle too", which pushes a second buyer.

**Steal this:** Open with a physical artifact of a dead loved one (a list, an object). Then frame the purchase as "breaking the chain" for the next generation.

He said if he scanned yours tonight, after the way you've been drinking, you'd be shocked..." then "I drank five Aperol Spritzes on a rooftop in Rome and ended up in an Italian ER." **Type:** a foreign-expert secret + a second-person accusation + an exotic incident. The opening is slightly garbled ("Except I drank..."), which suggests a spliced hook variant.

**Beat map:**
- 0–20%: Rome scene, pain, ambulance.
- 20–33%: Bloodwork; "Your liver is not in crisis. But it is exhausted."
- 33–45%: Mechanism: the liver drops everything to process alcohol, stores fat, inflames and stiffens; "no nerve endings."
- 45–48%: Solution named as an ingredient by the doctor: curcumin.
- 48–58%: Objection: "I'd already tried turmeric"; the doctor replies "That powder is maybe two or three percent... cannot absorb it without fat." The prescription slip reads 95% + black pepper + MCT.
- 58–69%: Search fails at CVS and Whole Foods; the brother-in-law NP names "BioRoot Labs."
- 69–80%: Results by week 2, week 4 and week 6 ("Your liver enzymes are back to normal").
- 80–100%: "I hadn't quit... My body just stopped punishing me for it"; generalization; scarcity; anti-Amazon; 60-day guarantee.

**Open loops:**
1. "something... no American doctor ever had", closed at about 33%.
2. "Then he told me the part that actually scared me."
3. "He held up one finger" sets up the failed-turmeric reversal.

**Mechanism:**
- Problem: alcohol hijacks the liver, which leads to fat storage, then inflammation, then dead tissue, felt as "why you wake up tired."
- Solution: curcumin "helps the liver... Burn that fat off."
- Why past attempts failed: dose (2–3%) and absorption ("Without fat, it passes straight through you"). "It wasn't the ingredient that failed you. It was the formula."

**Product bridge:** Ingredient at about 45%, prescribed by the doctor. Brand at about 69%, from a second professional: "The only brand I trust for this is BioRoot Labs." The ad uses a two-expert relay.

**Proof/specificity:** Five Aperol Spritzes; the Pantheon; "Four, five nights a week"; 95%; "up to 2,000%"; six weeks.

**Emotional engine:** Fear, then relief. The audience-permission promise ("Still having my beers") is the real hook for drinkers.

**Style:** Short paragraphs and mid-length sentences; a cinematic opening.

**Close:** Scarcity ("limited batches, and they sell out regularly"), "If you see BioRoot Labs in stock, grab it", a 60-day guarantee, and the sign-off "Your liver has been showing up for you every night. Time to return the favor."

**Steal this:** Pre-empt the "I already tried that ingredient" objection by having the expert explain why the reader's version failed (dose and absorption). Then sell the formula, not the ingredient.

## Ad 7 — BioRoot Labs Turmeric ("Variation 7", doctor listicle)
**ID:** BioRoot Labs Turmeric capsules (biorootlabs.com/products/turmeric-curcumin-capsules). ~425 words / ~2.6k chars, a short-form control within the set. Ad ID, run days and status: n/a.

**Narrator/POV:** An unnamed practitioner and founder ("I often tell my patients... So I created one myself"). Credibility is implied rather than proven.

**Hook:** "I often tell my patients to take turmeric every morning. And every time, I get the look. Wide eyes. Raised eyebrows. That 'You mean the stuff in curry?' face." **Type:** a professional's counterintuitive recommendation + the patient's predictable reaction.

**Beat map:**
- 0–15%: Hook and personal use ("every day for over 10 years").
- 15–55%: "5 life-changing benefits", a numbered list that actually contains only 4 items (inflammation, mobility, replaces painkillers, immune).
- 55–75%: Problem with other brands ("Too weak... doesn't get absorbed"); "I tried dozens."
- 75–79%: "So I created one myself."
- 79–95%: Product named and emoji spec bullets.
- 95–100%: Ritual line and link.

**Open loops:** One only: "Why? Because of these 5 life-changing benefits."

**Mechanism:** Thin. Joint pain is "often chronic inflammation". Absorption needs black pepper ("2000% better") and coconut oil.

**Product bridge:** The ingredient is in the first line. The brand comes at about 79% as the founder's creation.

# CROSS-AD SYNTHESIS

## 1. The dominant structural template ("Witness → Messenger → Mechanism → Brand")
Nearly every long ad (1–6, 8, 10, 12, 14/15, 16, 17) runs on this skeleton. Typical % positions:

| Beat | Typical position | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (crisis cold-open or confession) | 0–3% | Open in the middle of the worst moment, then "Let me back up." (Ads 1, 4) |
| Origin and identity, with hyper-specific life details | 3–12% | Age, town, job, dead spouse/parent |
| Symptom creep and "life getting smaller" | 10–25% | "Each one was just a small, reasonable adjustment" |
| Failed solutions (the list) | 15–30% | Mainstream (doctor "keep watching", pills) plus DIY (gummies, teas, glucosamine) |
| Rock bottom (public or physical humiliation) | 18–35% | Collapsing in the garden, stuck on the stairs holding a grandchild, "Nana, are you broken?", sitting on a wall for 22 minutes |
| Messenger encounter (stranger, doctor, old friend, paramedic) | 20–45% | Usually by chance: a supermarket aisle, a neighbor's wall, a biker bar, a book club, a wellness-expo line |
| Mechanism reveal (problem), with an analogy | 25–55% | Often drawn: a napkin, 3 circles on exam paper |
| Villain (the drug, the box, "the system", the doctor's passivity) | interleaved 20–55% | |
| Ingredient or category named | 38–55% | Before the brand |
| Second-order mechanism: why other versions of this ingredient fail | 45–65% | Dose, absorption, wrong strain or type, heat processing, wrong wavelength |

A second, minor template is the short brand-voice or landing-page format: the listicle (Ad 7), the future-pace (Ad 13), the retargeting protocol (Ad 11). These run 2.5–4k characters, introduce the product within the first 30%, and carry little emotion.

## 2. Top hook formulas
1. **Insider paradox:** "I fill 340 statin prescriptions a week. I won't fill my own." (Ad 2). "I'm breaking doctor-patient confidentiality" (Ad 16).
2. **Death or catastrophe from a "minor" thing:** "My mother died from a UTI" (Ads 14/15). "Lost my cat... job... boyfriend... because I thought a cough was a hairball" (Ad 17).
3. **Rage confession:** "I'm fucking furious writing this" (Ad 1).
4. **Grief artifact:** "my mother's medication list folded inside a Bible" (Ad 5).
5. **Victory-turned-horror:** "lost 47 pounds... And I lost my face doing it" (Ad 8).
6. **Decision cliffhanger:** "the reason he's still waiting on my answer" (Ad 12).
7. **Foreign or outsider expert:** "An ER doctor in Italy told me something... no American doctor ever had" (Ad 6).
8. **Off-topic melodrama:** the taillight reunion (Ad 3). The product is invisible for the first 20% of a 47k-character ad.
9. **Counterintuitive truth:** "the good days... lie to you" (Ad 4). "Most people who fail their taper are not failing because of willpower" (Ad 11).

Formats 1, 2 and 5 all use the same move: the reader's assumed-safe behavior is the cause of the disaster.

## 3. Open-loop patterns
- **Master loop plus nested sub-loops.** A destination or decision is promised in the hook ("why I drive to an Amish farm", "why he's still waiting on my answer", "what I learned in those 22 minutes"). It closes only at 50–90%. Each mechanism reveal then opens the next question ("The catch isn't betalain. The catch is the beet.").
- **The "question nobody asked" device:** "asked a question that nobody else had thought to ask in eleven years" (Ad 1); "Did your wife wear dentures?" (Ad 16); "Have you ever taken collagen?" (Ad 12). The question is closed in the next line, but it opens the mechanism.
- **Explicit deferral language:** "But I'm getting ahead of myself. Let me back up." "I want to tell you the thing about Pete I haven't told you." "Do you want the real answer or the polite one?"
- **Relationship loops that run under the sales story:** the father–daughter "Dad" (Ad 3), the sister Diane (Ad 8), the granddaughter's "are you broken?" (Ad 12). They pay off after the product works, so the product gets credit for the emotional resolution.
- **False-resolution loop:** the narrator buys a cheap version that "works", then the lab number gets worse (Ad 3: LDL 191). This is the strongest anti-competitor device in the set.

## 4. Bridge patterns
- **Ingredient first, brand second (about 10–25 points later).** Betalain at 38%, Rosabella at 56% (Ad 2). Amla at 46–52%, Alevia at 58–72% (Ads 4, 5). Curcumin at 45%, BioRoot at 69% (Ad 6).
- **The brand arrives through a gatekeeper or filter**, never as an ad:
  - an exclusive partner ("They'd partnered with one company — only one", Ad 2);
  - checklist survivor ("Four for four", Ad 3);
  - a professional's personal pick ("The only brand I trust for this", Ad 6; "one she keeps pointing customers towards", Ad 12);
  - a crowd source (Reddit thread with 600 comments, Ads 14/15; overheard in a smoothie line, Ad 8; a PubMed comment, Ad 10);
  - the narrator's own spreadsheet or spec that the product matches line for line (Ads 8, 10, 14/15, 16).
- **Unbranded expert spec, then the product matches it.** The doctor writes "Type V... 30mg HA... L. acidophilus, L. gasseri, L. fermentum" and the narrator finds "Everything she told me to find was in one capsule." This keeps the expert clean.
- **Device ads name the brand last (86–92%)**, after numeric specs have disqualified every competitor (40 kHz, 253.7nm).

## 5. Mechanism patterns
- **"The number is the smoke, not the fire."** This is the most reused frame (Ads 2, 3, 4, 5, 12, 14/15): the drug lowers a metric while the real damage continues. Variants: "Lower the number. Miss the damage. Drain the defense." "turning off the smoke alarm"; "treating the alarm, not the fire"; "We don't have an acid problem. We have a valve problem."
- **The drug is doubly guilty:** it misses the cause and it actively harms (statins drain CoQ10; antibiotics kill lactobacillus). "That's not a side effect. That's the mechanism."
- **Surface vs inside:** a tablet on the surface vs biofilm inside acrylic (Ads 1, 16); antioxidants that "bounce off" vs betalain that enters the LDL particle; antibiotics that kill "what's floating" vs bacteria "stuck to the wall" (Ad 13).
- **A coined, capitalized name for the problem:** "the Scavenging Cascade", "the Triple Drought", "bacterial adhesion", "the rust".
- **Analogy kit:** rust or a sliced apple or avocado (oxidation), a spark plug (CoQ10), a sniper vs a shotgun or a smart missile vs a carpet bomb, a kettle on the back burner, a garden hose, a sponge, a rotten floorboard and roof leak, a broken door or lock, velcro hooks, a wheel chasing its tail. Some are physically drawn on a napkin or on exam paper.
- **A second mechanism for why other products fail:** sugar-bred and spray-dried beets ("red dust"), 2–3% curcumin without fat, collagen types 1/3 vs 2, 35 kHz vs 40 kHz, blue LEDs vs UV-C. This is what justifies the specific brand.
- **Multi-part "all X together" logic** ("Five things. All five." / "Four for four" / "Three things... each one is useless without the other two") makes single-ingredient competitors look incomplete.

## 6. Proof and specificity norms
Every winning-style ad includes: the exact age; the town or street; a named doctor; named relatives; dated events ("April 23rd", "March 14th, 2024"); lab values before and after (LDL 172 to 124; BP 148/92 to 124/80; pH 4.3); dollar totals ("$186", "$247 a month", "Over $12,000... Radiancy was $49"); and a timeline to the day ("Seventeen minutes", "day four", "week two"). A doctor re-test scene with the line "Whatever you're doing, keep doing it" appears in Ads 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 and 14/15.

## 7. Emotional engines
- **Guilt or grief over a loved one** (dead spouse or parent): Ads 2, 4, 5, 14/15, 16, 17.
- **Rage at a trusted authority or product:** Ads 1, 2, 16, 17.
- **Humiliation:** Ads 8 and 12, and the pharmacist's laugh in Ad 10.
- **Love and legacy stakes:** Ad 3 (grandchildren), Ad 5 ("breaking the chain").

The turn is almost always a sensory proof moment: dirty water, crimson powder, four hairs in the hand, walking up a slope without thinking, standing up from the floor unaided.

## 8. Style norms
Short paragraphs, often one line each. Fragments for rhythm ("Flat. Wrong."). Anaphora in the villain and CTA passages ("Not... Not... Not...", "Do not wait for..."). Dialogue carries the mechanism, so the expert speaks it and it is never lectured. Grade 4–7 reading level. Explicit humility markers ("I'm not a doctor. I'm a welder.", "I'm not going to sit here and tell you this is a miracle"). Explicit medical disclaimers delivered through story ("Duke... made the call together" with his doctor), which double as credibility and compliance cover. Regional voice is used deliberately: UK in Ad 12, AU in Ad 1, Southern US in Ad 5.

## 9. Close patterns
- A guarantee of 60 or 90 days, reframed emotionally ("nobody offered to refund the years I lost", "tied to your actual lab results").
- Scarcity rooted in the mechanism ("one heirloom seed line... When a season's batch is gone, it's gone"; "they use the full clinical creatine dose... so they can't mass-produce").
- Anti-Amazon or knockoff warnings.
- Bundle offers framed as gifts for a second person ("three for the person you've been thinking about this whole time").
- A P.S. stack that handles one objection per P.S.: already on medication, stubborn spouse, the guarantee, knockoffs, timeline expectations.
- **"Second buyer" P.S.s:** "For the women reading this...", "If you have a daughter, please give her a bottle too", "Put it next to his coffee mug."
- Device and advertorial-bridge ads (1, 16) end with "read the article" rather than an offer.

## 10. Length norms
- Story ads: about 10k–47k characters (about 1,700–8,700 words). The median story ad is about 15–17k characters (about 3,000 words).
- Short formats (listicle, future-pace, retarget, older advertorial): 2.5–6k characters.
- The longest (Ad 3, about 47k) spends roughly 20% on non-product melodrama. This suggests very long copy works when the story itself is gripping, not because of density.
