## 5. Mechanism patterns
- **"Stops the damage but doesn't repair it"** / **"silences the alarm, never puts out the fire"** — explains why the standard fix "worked" yet failed (Purelia tank, GoodGrove alarm/fire, Nutriveen "holds dopamine, adds nothing").
- **Hidden layer the standard tool can't reach:** biofilm in denture acrylic, tonsil crypt film, gut "limescale", heel drop "ramp" — "works on the surface."
- **Form/processing villain:** heat destroys it (grocery cherry juice, roasted seeds), capsules can't absorb in a failing organ → liquid / cold-pressed.
- **Multi-source:** "three-source" breath, "five cherries = five organs", gut-liver-skin (Norse).
- **Economic mechanism:** price = clinic overhead, "$130 to make" (hearing aids).
- **Sensory/neuro mechanism without lab science:** "it's not behavioral, it's sensory" (UpAiry); "the knit swells with you" (Nimbao).
- Simplifiers: household analogies (fuel tank, kettle limescale, factory/shelves, mop vs. turn off water vs. find the leak, alarm vs. fire, hot stove), physical props (five cherries, thumb in heel, squeezing toe box, brown water), and one-line refrains ("The drink didn't change. The tank did.").

## 6. Proof & close conventions
- Lab values + dates + times-of-day ("5:42 AM", "ALT 174 → 41") in health; time-of-day + step counts in footwear ("by 2pm", "22,000 steps").
- Stat-stack closing line: "One pair. Seven days. Four ports. Zero pain."
- Disinterest disclaimers ("I don't work for Purelia. They don't know I exist.") on persona pages named after people (David Miller, Melissa Hayes, Keira James, Callie Kelly) or faux publishers (Daily Life Magazine, Better Living Daily, Golden Years Club, Raising Our ADHD Kids).
- Offers are often smuggled into dialogue (BOGO via husband/friend/sister); guarantees framed as dares ("Send it back if the water comes out clear. It will not.") or with reason-why (Nutriveen keeps no-return policy so mothers don't "do arithmetic").
- Many winners have **no offer in the body at all** (Cuurt chiropractor, UpAiry, NovaPod furious wife, NOURI) — the ad sells the click to an advertorial/listicle LP.

## 7. Style norms
- One-to-three-sentence paragraphs; heavy one-line punches ("Nothing held." "It turned brown." "Deceased."). NovaPod furious wife is almost one sentence per line.
- Reading level ~grade 3–6 for winners; Nutriveen (~7, literary) and Norse (brand voice) are the outliers.
- Dialogue everywhere, often with family members as foils (Janet, Gary, Raymond, Kathleen).
- Profanity used only as a hook pattern-interrupt (furious-wife / PT sister), not throughout.

## 8. Length norms
- Range in batch: 2.5k–45k chars. Long-runners: 4.4k (Artuvate), 5.2k (Cuurt), 8.3k (UpAiry), 11.4–11.5k (Nimbao), 13.9k (Modern Hearing), 18k (Purelia). Sweet spot for longevity here is **~5k–18k**.
- Extremes didn't clearly win: the 45k Nutriveen ran 32d; the 33k GoodGrove 32d; the 2.5–2.9k Cuurt/Norse short tests ran 23–38d.

## 9. Longest-running vs short-lived — differences
1. **Pain-first beats product-first** for the same product: NovaPod furious-wife story (46d, ~19k reach) vs NovaPod "Frank" spec-first cut (20d, ~7k reach); Cuurt chiropractor (117d+, expert + villain, no offer) vs Cuurt friend-handoff + BOGO siblings (23d).
2. **Aspiration/leisure stakes beat workplace humiliation** in the Nimbao set: Disney/cruise (103d, active) and flight-crew secret (86d) vs pharmacy/teacher (44–45d). The winners stage a desirable event the reader is planning (Disney with grandkids, a cruise) and lead with the result.
3. **Guilt-removal reframes run longest:** UpAiry ("it's not behavioral, it's sensory" — 322d), Nimbao ("It was never your feet"), Cuurt ("it wasn't my body").
4. **A named expert/authority who examines something physical** (chiropractor squeezing the shoe, young doctor looking in the mouth) appears in long-runners; brand-voice copy (Norse) had the weakest rank and a falling trend.
5. **Market/offer fit matters as much as copy:** identical Brian Harrison copy ran 193d for US Modern Hearing ($249, Medicare gap, neutral news-domain advertorial) vs 34d for Canadian Lunova ($300, OHIP, store-domain "report").
6. **Mega-length tragedy templates (30k+) haven't proven longevity yet** — the 18k Purelia version did 107d; the 33k/45k extensions of the same template did ~32d. Length past ~18k shows diminishing returns in this sample.
7. **Founder/integrity stories travel well** for gadgets: Artuvate's "I said no to the retailer" has 165d and 4 duplicates.
