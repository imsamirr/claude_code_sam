## Synthesis (batch 4: 19 assigned + 12 additional full breakdowns + 75-ad inventory)

### 1. The master template (with typical % positions)
Across the 30 unique full breakdowns, the same skeleton shows up again and again. Positions are medians; a 30k+ char ad stretches the story half (0–60%) and keeps the commercial half about the same absolute length.

| Beat | Typical % | What it does | Signature lines |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | 0–3% | Oddly specific scene, number or confession | "My husband died on a dialysis chair… April 28th at 3:47 PM"; "I fill 340 statin prescriptions a week. I won't fill my own." |
| 2. Identity/credibility | 3–10% | Profession + years, or a plain-folks identity with texture | "nephrologist for 18 years"; "drove a school bus… twenty-six years" |
| 3. Decline/symptom escalation | 8–30% | Concrete, dated losses | Year-by-year ladder (B10), quarterly eGFR slide (A16, B11), week 1/2/3 of failed device (A14) |
| 4. "Did everything right" + failed fixes | 10–35% | Removes self-blame, names competitors | Named brands: HumanN SuperBeets, Affresh, Miralax, Bigelow, Polident, Amazon blends |
| 5. Dismissive authority quotes | 10–35% | Builds the villain | "We'll keep an eye on it." / "Monitor it." / "It's just aging." / "Have you considered a therapist?" |
| 6. Rock bottom | 20–55% | Emotional floor | Grandpa's arms give out lifting the toddler (B10); "sat in the bathroom 22 minutes"; parking lot 2.5 hours |
| 7. Encounter/discovery | 25–50% | Someone hands over the key | Pathologist, shelter vet, sister, son, hospice-nurse widow, Whole Foods stranger, forum thread, 3 a.m. PubMed |
| 8. Mechanism (problem) | 30–60% | Reframes the cause; explains past failures | "-itis means inflammation; your tissue is degenerating"; "flood vs drain"; "rust in the filter" |
| 9. Ingredient/category reveal | 40–70% | Usually introduced *before* the brand | "True Ceylon cinnamon." / "Betalain." / "Pumpkin seed oil." |
| 10. Category disqualifier | 45–75% | Kills cheaper versions of the same ingredient | "Wrong species. Wrong dose. Wrong delivery."; "The catch isn't betalain. The catch is the beet."; 35 vs 40 kHz |
| 11. Brand | median ~60% (range 0–99%) | Often one short sentence | "I found Everly." / "That company is Rosabella." / "The brand I recommend is Metabolae." |
| 12. Results timeline | 60–85% | Week-by-week, sensory first, labs later | "Week 2… my fingers feel like fingers again"; "eGFR 48 → 56" |
| 13. Doctor reversal | 75–90% | Authority validates | "Whatever you're doing, keep doing it." (in 9+ ads); "Improving. Not managed. Improving." |
| 14. Testimonial ring | 75–90% | Friends, family, colleagues | mother/brother/coworker (A12), sister/neighbor/brother-in-law (A16) |
| 15. Close | 88–100% | Guarantee vs pharma, scarcity, P.S. stack | "Lipitor has never come with a money-back guarantee"; harvest/batch stock-outs; 3–6 P.S.'s |

### 2. Hook formulas that recur
1. **Insider refusal / mea culpa:** "I'm a nephrologist and I've been telling patients the wrong thing… for 22 years" (A7); "I fill 340 statin prescriptions a week. I won't fill my own." (A12); "I'm breaking doctor-patient confidentiality to write this." (B8)
2. **Death or catastrophe with a timestamp:** "April 28th at 3:47 PM" (A16); "It was 7:15 AM when I found my 72-year-old husband collapsed" (B6); "I lost my cat. Then I lost my job. Then I lost my boyfriend." (A18)
3. **Cryptic remark from a loved one:** "My husband told me he 'missed my scent'" (A1; same script ran 248d for Nuora).
4. **Specialist correction "It's not X":** "The most dangerous sign of kidney failure is not back pain…" (A3, the only Carter variant still running); "What if… it was never actually plantar fasciitis to begin with?" (A5)
5. **Visual symptom callout:** foamy urine "like someone poured dish soap" (A7, A9); "low to the ground, extends their neck… violent heaving cough" (A18)
6. **Caught between authorities:** "My new doctor called my old doctor 'negligent'" (A13); "periodontist… 'non-compliant'" (inventory)
7. **Oblique literary vignette:** "a man in a Carhartt jacket put back a gallon of milk" (B10); "the woman who saved my husband's life… stirring a glass of milk" (B11). Both are 30k+ chars and ran 150+ days.
8. **Injustice counterfactual:** "If normal people lost the ability to eat… there'd be government grants" (A19); "When my husband got his ONE UTI in 20 years… specialized urology referrals" (B4)
9. **Us-vs-them PSA:** "Ladies, stop letting boys who got sex ed from Pornhub tell you about YOUR vagina" (B3; 6 copies, 4 of them ran 240+d)
10. **Category redemption / "I left the 1-star review":** "I Wasted $42 On Amazon Flea Repellers That Were Actually Designed For Roaches" (A14)
11. **Sensory anaphora:** "Every time I walk into the room, I smell it…" (B1, 446d)
12. **Lapsed-user confession:** "I thought I was being smart trying Provitalean while on Ozempic" (B5, retention)

### 3. Open-loop patterns
- **Retrospective outcome hooks** give away the ending ("the woman who saved my husband's life") and sell the *how*. That loop stays open for ~80% of the copy.
- **Planted objects** that close much later: Robert's half-full pill organizer (A16, closed in the P.S.); the coffee mug Frank can't wrap his hands around (B10, 4% → 82%); Walter's broken crystal radio (B11, 39% → 85%); the RN's "two words" that pay off on a reread (A16).
- **Explicit promises:** "by the end of this, you're gonna be furious" (A10); "a two-part problem… both parts" (A2); "I'm going to walk you through those nine years" (B10).
- **Section-end teasers in sales-letter dividers** ("========" + "Ready for the solution that changed everything?") in the BB Co/Radiancy family and its clones (B4, A19, B5).
- **Deadline loops:** "My appointment with Dr. Kessler was in nine days" (A13); nephrology appointments every quarter (A16, B11).
- **Short serial loops for 8–10k ads** (Carter A3: four loops of 5–10% each). **Long nested loops for 30k+ ads** (A12: the Amish-farm loop runs 0 → 57%).

### 4. Mechanism patterns
1. **Wrong target.** The standard treatment aims at the wrong thing: "-itis vs -osis" (A5); A1C "tracks the water level, not the current" (A3); "LDL number vs oxidized LDL" (A12); "Cranberry is for the bladder. It doesn't touch the filter." (A7); "glucosamine doesn't support compressed nerves" (A15)
2. **Hidden reservoir/biofilm.** Vaginal biofilm "like plaque on teeth" (A1, B3, B7); the washer's outer tub (A17); porous denture acrylic (B8, B9). Each one explains why surface cleaning or old fixes "work for a few days then come back."
3. **Depletion/missing piece.** CoQ10 drained by statins (A12, B10); taurine blockade (A18); Bifidobacteria decline after 60 (B6); anthocyanins "the only antioxidant small enough to reach the nephron" (B11); 90-day colonization window (B5)
4. **Disqualifying the cheaper version of the same ingredient** (the most-used device in this batch): species/dose/delivery (Metabolae); juice vs standardized extract (Alevia); sugar-bred beet + spray-drying (Rosabella); heat-processed vs cold-extracted nitrates (BeetWise); fannings vs whole leaf, dust vs whole flower (PiPi); 20kHz fixed vs 25–65kHz variable (Repellix); 35 vs 40 kHz and 395 vs 253.7nm (Denture Dome).
5. **Economic mechanism** (no biology): markup on $100–$150 components (B12).
- **How they simplify it:** one domestic metaphor carried through the whole ad: flood/drain, rust/filter, sand through a mesh screen, kitchen counter (mevalonate), screened porch, dashboard light/coolant, rancid avocado, scalpel in Polident, garden hose at a house fire, sniper vs shotgun.

### 5. Bridge patterns
- **Ingredient first, brand second** in ~70% of ads. The gap is usually 5–20 points (e.g. Ceylon 52% → Metabolae 71%; betalain 39% → Rosabella 61%; pomegranate 49% → Alevia 64%).
- **The brand arrives through a trusted human:** a stranger in Whole Foods (A13), a sister (A14), a son (A18's vet, B9's son), a church friend (B10), a hospice-nurse widow who *hands over a bag* (B11), a colleague on a tech forum (A17), a cardiologist friend (A7).
- **Fake-organic link drop:** "Edit🚨: Sorry guys, heres the link" (A1); "EDIT: My bad, I didn't even mention the brand. My DMs are full" (A18). Both ran 120–210d.
- **No brand name in the body at all:** Koprez (A5, URL only), Everly dad version (A10, URL only), KittySupps (A18, EDIT link only).
- **Brand in line 1** only works for product-aware or offer audiences: Provitalean retention (B5), Modern Hearing investigation (B12), Pet-Lab (B1).

### 6. Length norms and longevity
- **Longest-running unique creatives (≥150d)** span 2.1k–42.6k chars, median about 9k: Pet-Lab 2.3k (446d), AlphaCleanse 5.9k (298d), Nuora "missed my scent" 4.1k (248d), Nuora PSA 2.8k (241d), Everly 8.4k (216d, still #1), Radiancy 8.2k (214d), KittySupps 13.4k (210d), Provitalean 12.8k (200d), Elvera 9.5k (198d), Nuora affiliate 7k (195d), Everly-dad 12.8k (187d), copper bottle 2.1k (168d), Uproot 12.5k (162d), PiPi oolong 42.6k (162d, still #1–2), Denture Dome 9.9k/10.3k (159d/153d), PiPi hibiscus 33.8k (151d).
- **Short-lived (≤35d)** examples: Keller RN 17k (18d), Carter "5 symptoms" 14.8k (31d), Rosabella pharmacist 29.4k (34d, though #1 in-account while it ran), KIN founder spec-sheet 6.9k (11d), five Margaret Coleman Elvera spin-offs 11–22k (3–22d), Margaret & Jim hearing spin-offs about 7.7k (17–27d), BB Co pure-offer ads (1–7d).

**Differences between long runners and short-lived ads**
1. **The original outlives its spin-offs.** Elvera's original ran 198d, its variants ≤66d. Healthy Living Digest ran 117d, its persona variants ≤27d. Radiancy ran 214d, the Ploise clone 57d. Carter's "not back pain" version (A3) ran 116d and is still active, while the "how often," "5 symptoms" and nurse variants ran 18–59d. When a winner is iterated, most new variants don't beat it.
2. **Long runners keep one tight emotional engine.** Grief/guilt (A16, B8), a spouse watching decline (B10, B11) or intimate shame (A1, B3). The short-lived variants widen the audience ("5 aging symptoms") or pile on angles.
3. **Softer closes survive.** The #1 Everly ad (216d) has no offer, no guarantee and no link: "Maybe it can help you too." Pet-Lab (446d) has no offer. KittySupps (210d) has no guarantee. Pure offer/discount copy (BB Co Provitalize) ran only 1–7d. The story does the selling and the LP does the offer.
4. **Founder/brand voice with spec sheets underperforms disguised first-person narrators** (KIN 11d vs pet-owner Repellix 133d and KittySupps 210d).
5. **Mega-length (30k+) can win, but only as real fiction.** Novel-grade scene writing with planted objects and a single relationship at the center (PiPi B10/B11) did. The 17k nurse version and 29k pharmacist version did not last, despite strong mechanics.

### 7. Cross-ad operator observations (useful for swiping)
- **Same scripts, different storefronts:** "missed my scent" ran for AllFemme Flora+ (A1) and Nuora (248d). Koprez/InvigorFate ran identical copy on two pages. The Radiancy template was cloned almost line-for-line by Ploise (A19): "And I was DONE being abandoned," husband's "Another miracle cure?" → "He kissed my forehead," testimonials named Margaret/Teresa/Jennifer, the same emoji offer stack, "Sometimes healing begins when…".
- **Kidney is the most crowded health angle in this batch** (Metabolae ×4, Alevia ×2, Everly ×2, PiPi hibiscus). They share a visual vocabulary: foam "like dish soap," eGFR ladders, "the chair," $90k/yr dialysis, and the villain word "monitor."
- **Recurring villain phrases:** "keep an eye on it," "monitor," "it's just aging," "talk to a therapist/antidepressant" (B10, B11, A13), "non-compliant" (A13, inventory), plus patent-less-plant and pharma-profit arguments.
- **Guarantee framed against pharma** ("No medication… has ever come with a money-back guarantee") appears in A7, A12, A16, B10 and B11.
- **QA warning:** A17 (Uproot, 162d, still active) shipped with an internal copywriter note and a broken paragraph in the live copy. Strong story chassis can tolerate errors, but proofread.
- **Signal caveats:** days_running lags and can include low-spend tails. AlphaCleanse (298d) disclosed only 778 EU reach. Impression rank is within-advertiser, so "#1 of 3" (Elvera) is weaker than "#1 of 274" (Everly).
