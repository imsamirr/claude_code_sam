## A. Strategy layer (Jamil Rashid)

1. **One hero mechanism, 10–20 angles.**
   - Every concept names its **angle**: the symptom or problem / pocket of buyers.
   - Every angle traces back to the brand's ONE hero mechanism (section D), backed by proof or a logical chain the customer can follow.
   - Do not invent a new mechanism per ad. Vary the angle, keep the mechanism.
   - Several angles may be stacked in one ad, as long as each one routes back to the mechanism.
   - The mechanism must make sense in the customer's head ("this causes that, so this fixes it"). Reject vague or "AI-sounding" mechanism lines.
2. **Format fatigue: niche × avatar × format.**
   - The cycle: a format works → everyone copies it → results fade → find the next one.
   - Before planning, check what is spending in our niche, and what is spending in OTHER niches that hasn't hit ours yet. A format that feels late in supplements can still be fresh in footwear or oral care.
   - Women 45+ over-index on **drama ads, Pixar-style story / mini-movie ads and song ads**. Keep at least one of these formats in rotation where the routine's format allows it.
   - Keep a running "working formats" list in the brand's research notes, and tag each concept with its format.
3. **Research engine.** Besides the Atria library, mine **organic** Reels/TikTok (e.g. StefanBrain `research_shortform`) for hooks and visual gags. Several of Jamil's $500k+ winners came from organic concepts.
4. **Systems don't make winners, good creative does.** Volume comes from a repeatable process (below), not from lowering the bar.

## B. Copy-process layer (Stefan Georgi / StefanBrain)

1. **References are king.**
   - Every concept is built on a specific reference: our own winner (first choice), a scaling ad from another brand, or an organic post.
   - Brief StefanBrain to **"model the structure of the reference closely, for our brand"**. Include the reference's transcript or copy, plus our closest winning script as a second reference when one exists.
   - Name both references in the concept file.
2. **Outline first, then write**, wherever the workflow allows a follow-up:
   - Ask StefanBrain for the outline / beat list first.
   - Review it for structure (hook → bridge → problem → mechanism → proof → offer → CTA), transitions and one-pain-point focus.
   - Send 1–2 short notes, then have it write the script / primary text.
   - Automated one-shot tools (`stefanbrain.py copy/write`): judge the output against this list and send weak items back with `followup`/`revise` and **short, specific notes**. Do not rewrite them yourself.
   - Once a format is dialed in within a chat, keep using that chat. "Next concept, same structure" snowballs quality.
3. **One winner → many ads.**
   - Name the winner's **core dominant emotion** (e.g. COM284 = "I'm not the one slowing everyone down"; a DentalPULSE winner = disgust/shock at what was living on it).
   - Keep the structure and emotion; change the **spokesperson, situation, location and story details**: 10–20 new situations per winner.
   - Ask for concepts as "big idea / situation / main character / what happens", a few sentences each, not full outlines.
4. **Hooks**
   - Ask StefanBrain for many hooks per concept, then follow up with the "make them weirder" note: *"avoid generic ones every LLM would give; make them weirder, scroll-stopping, include unique situations; 10X more creative, 10X less generic."*
   - Ask for 1–2 **single-sentence, curiosity-heavy, completely open-ended** hooks per concept. For example: "So the other day my boyfriend's golden retriever tried to lick my face." That's the whole hook; the open loop is what happens next.
   - Best hooks are **situational micro-shame moments** plus a vivid simile. Example: "I swatted his hand away like a stray wasp."
   - Reject AI tropes: "we need to have a serious talk", "you need to see this", "here's the truth", "let that sink in", over-explained hooks that close their own loop.
   - Hooks depend on format. A yapper hook ≠ a faceless AI-voiceover hook ≠ a drama or song hook.
5. **Stem hooks (one concept → every format)**
   - Generate 12–16 "stem" ideas across the 8 buckets:
     1. daily habits;
     2. timing/usage riddles;
     3. friction & mechanics (more effort = worse result);
     4. competitor/status-quo attack;
     5. micro-human quirks and coping habits;
     6. location/micro-environment;
     7. long-term fear / ticking clock;
     8. outside trigger.
   - Translate the best stems into: casual yapper, authority (expert) yapper, faceless AI voiceover, drama, song, native caption / long-form.
6. **Yapper and story scripts**
   - **Write like people talk.** Contractions, run-ons, "and she's like… and I'm like…", sentences that start with "And". A conversational, slightly Valley-girl-light register works for women, including women 50+.
   - **One dominant pain point per ad.** E.g. plantar fasciitis OR bunions; denture stomatitis OR yellowing. Never two at once.
   - **Reveal setup, pick one of three:**
     1. an appointment with an expert, where she apologizes for her body and the expert explains;
     2. on the phone or texting with a friend who happens to be an expert;
     3. at brunch or in person with an expert friend.
   - Occasionally a family member or a self-discovery story instead.
   - **Watch the transitions.** After the expert's explanation, the natural line is "wait, what does that even mean?"
   - **Casual vs authority yapper:** the casual narrator *discovers* the truth; the authority narrator ("As a podiatrist…", "As a dental hygienist…") states it with confidence.
   - **Stakes, or nobody cares.** Something lost, risked or embarrassing. First person, past tense; don't resolve the story in the hook.
7. **Song ads.** Winning song ads often **don't use a classic rhyme scheme**. Brief "follow the reference's structure, less classic rhyme".
8. **Creator and casting** (for briefs and production notes)
   - The right creator can turn a flop into a winner with the same script. When a yapper underperforms, recommend a re-shoot with a different creator before killing the concept, then keep using the creator who wins.
   - Women outperform men for these audiences.
   - Casting younger than the avatar (40s for a 60+ audience): **test it, don't assume it**. Stefan's evidence is from a telemedicine landing page, and ComfortWear's plus-size / 50+ identity call-outs win.
9. **Rules hygiene.** Keep the rules we add short and soft. Over-engineered hard rules fight new formats later.

## C. Checklist for every concept or brief (add to the concept file)

- [ ] Angle named; traces to the hero mechanism in one sentence.
- [ ] Reference(s) named, with the structure being modelled.
- [ ] Core emotion named.
- [ ] One dominant pain point.
- [ ] Format, and why it fits this avatar right now (fatigue check).
- [ ] Hook is situational / specific, open-loop and trope-free, with at least one single-sentence variant.
- [ ] For scripts: spoken voice; one of the three reveal setups or a clear story engine; clean transitions; stakes.
- [ ] Outline reviewed, or one-shot output graded, before it ships; weak items sent back to StefanBrain with short notes.

## D. Brand hero mechanisms (working drafts; use as the spine of every concept until Samir edits them)

**ComfortWear**
- **Mechanism:** soft foam (memory foam, max-cushion running shoes) compresses under body weight within hours, so by afternoon the arch collapses and the heel and plantar fascia take every step on hard ground.
- **Fix:** ComfortWear's anatomical arch support holds its shape all day; the rocker sole rolls you forward so the heel isn't absorbing every strike; the roomy stretch-knit toe box gives room as feet swell.
- **Angles that trace back to it:**
  - heel stab / plantar fasciitis;
  - bunion pressure by afternoon;
  - knees and back;
  - nurse hour 8;
  - teacher on her feet all day;
  - plus-size (more weight crushes foam faster);
  - Disney / NYC / Europe travel days;
  - GLP-1 weight change;
  - pickleball / walking;
  - grandkids;
  - fear of losing independence.
- **Proof chain:**
  - thumb into flattened foam vs. ours;
  - "fine at breakfast, hour 8 is a different story";
  - competitor teardown with prices (approved by Samir);
  - $300 orthotics anchor;
  - podiatrist- / doctor-recommended.

**DentalPULSE**
- **Mechanism:** bacteria live in a biofilm that sits in the tiny scratches and pores of dentures, night guards, retainers and aligners. Brushing, soaking and tablets only clean the surface, so the film grows back and the appliance yellows and smells.
- **Fix:** 42,000 Hz ultrasonic cleaning in one 5-minute cycle reaches where brushes and tablets can't (approved claims: eliminates 99.9% of bacteria; clinical-grade; tap water only).
- **Angles that trace back to it:**
  - aspiration pneumonia, denture stomatitis, mold, cavities (via the narrator's diagnosis and attributed research, per brand rules);
  - yellowing / white film;
  - smell;
  - adhesive residue;
  - tablets weakening the appliance;
  - the cost of replacing appliances;
  - cheap pods that don't clean ("6 of 7 pods leave live bacteria");
  - caregiver for a parent;
  - teens' retainers / aligners.
- **Proof chain:**
  - murky brown first-cycle water;
  - the 90-day lab test of 7 pods;
  - dentist-recommended;
  - 120-day money-back guarantee.
