## Ranking

### 1. Denture adhesive: the best opportunity for DentalPULSE

It has the most intense, most frequent pain, it sells straight to the 55% of buyers the ad account already converts, and it is a cheap regulatory path.

**What customers are solving:**
- Eating a real meal without the plate moving. This is mostly about the **lower denture**.
- Not losing their teeth in public.
- Keeping food out from under the plate.
- Cushioning sore gums.
- Bridging a bad fit when a reline or implants are unaffordable.

**What they hate about current products:**
- **Hold fails (57% of Amazon negatives):** it dissolves with coffee and gives out mid-meal.
- **Goo and removal:** "took me OVER AN HOUR to get these things off"; scraping the gums with fingernails.
- **Burning and irritation**, mainly from the "gentle" challengers.
- **Trial-and-error waste.**
- **Products discontinued:** Cushion Grip had a hiatus, Ezo is gone, and Haleon cut SKUs in 2026.

**White space:**
- Strong hold that also releases cleanly. Nobody claims both.
- A product built for lower dentures.
- A clean formula that still holds. Every clean-label product today rates 3.3–3.9★.
- An end-of-day removal step that pairs with the Pod.
- An auto-ship subscription.

**Competitive warning:** Ploise, a direct ultrasonic rival, is already running "Tablets don't lift the glue" ads and selling "loose denture" strips. Zyara (a $49.99/month thermoplastic subscription) grew traffic 166% month over month and is running heavy Meta ads.

**Risk:** results vary from mouth to mouth, which is why every challenger rates below 4★. Product testing on lower dentures has to come before launch.

### 2. Oral probiotic: the most differentiated, best as an add-on

**Why it fits:**
- No one targets denture or appliance wearers.
- It is the only one of the three with peer-reviewed support specific to denture wearers (Candida reduction RCTs).
- It extends the "bacteria and smell" story from the appliance to the mouth.
- Margins are high and repeat purchase is built in.

**What holds it back:**
- **The trust problem:** ProDentim and the affiliate copycats target the same 60+ audience. ProDentim rates 1.9★ on Trustpilot, and "prodentim scam" is a top related search.
- **Claim limits:** no gum, cavity, thrush or stomatitis claims.
- **Non-responders:** about 30% of reviews say "didn't work".
- **Taste and sweetener complaints.**
- **Market size:** the smallest of the three.

**How to sell it:** as a bundle or subscription with the Pod, not to cold traffic.

### 3. Toothpaste: the largest market, the weakest fit

**Why it ranks last:**
- It goes head-to-head with P&G, Colgate and Haleon, plus Boka and Hismile.
- Mass gum pastes already rate 4.7–4.8★ at $5–8.
- The #1 complaint about DTC pastes is price (45–76% of value-for-money mentions are negative).
- A gingivitis claim makes it an OTC drug.
- Full-denture wearers, the core of the ad account, can't use it.

**Separate or combined?** If you do it, make **one combined product, gum-first, with gentle whitening as the secondary benefit**: stannous fluoride 0.454%, low abrasion, SLS-free.
- The incumbents' winning combos are gum or sensitivity bases with a gentle-whitening variant: Parodontax Whitening, Colgate Total Whitening, Sensodyne Sensitivity & Gum Whitening.
- Nobody sells "max whitening + gum", because abrasives and peroxide hurt receding gums and stannous fluoride can stain.
- Two separate SKUs would double the regulatory and creative cost for a small customer base.
- A standalone whitening paste is the most crowded lane and the worst fit for a 60+ denture brand.
- The best audience is night-guard, retainer and aligner wearers, plus partial-denture wearers who want to keep the teeth they have left.

## Suggested sequence

1. **Denture adhesive first:** a lower-denture-capable, zinc-free cream with easy release, sold as a subscription and as part of the Pod denture bundle. Optionally add a removal step (wipe or rinse) that ties into the Pod.
2. **Probiotic second:** a strain-named, licensed K12/M18 or L. reuteri formula in a dry-mouth-friendly format, positioned as the step after cleaning the appliance. It goes in bundles and subscriptions, and needs legal review of claims.
3. **Toothpaste only later,** if at all, as a refill add-on for natural-teeth customers.

## Caveats

- **Traffic:** Similarweb figures are modeled estimates.
- **Amazon:** "bought in past month" badges are capped, so they are floors.
- **Market sizes:** the published reports disagree widely.
- **Full-denture share:** the 27–39% estimate assumes a full-vs-partial split; no data on that split was found.
- **Atria coverage:** the Atria plan's included MCP calls had run out, so the read-only lookups cost about 20 workspace credits. Rate limits also left some Meta ad checks incomplete (Fixodent, Denttach).
- **No ad copy was written in this research.** All quotes are verbatim from customers.
