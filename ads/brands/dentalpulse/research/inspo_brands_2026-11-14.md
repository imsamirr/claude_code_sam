## Why ours looks wrong next to them
1. **Ours look like AI stock photos of a bathroom with text pasted on.** The Pod sits small in a busy, glossy, evenly lit vanity scene, the same scene 40 times. Theirs is either a **product-hero studio shot** (Sonic, Zima, PRIMALS, KittySpout, Larine: product 35-60% of the frame, one clean color field, premium light) or a **genuinely ugly real phone photo** (Denture Care Tips 120-160d, the yellow night guard on a grey towel 101d, PRIMALS' grimy sink, Hollow's handwritten sign). We sit in the uncanny middle: polished but fake.
2. **Our copy explains; theirs provokes.** HiSmile: "Do you want f*ck off white teeth?". Mars Men: "Man boobs gone or your money back", sticky notes "Dad bod = Low T bod". Larine: "Attention: your gum may be made of plastic". Hollow: a roadside sign "Yes compression socks are tight & itchy... so we made ones so comfy we guarantee them". PRIMALS: "For dads who did the research". Remi (night guards, 222d): "Lost your $600 night guard? Replace it for $99." / "Wrong tool. Wrong job." One idea, said with attitude, in 3-8 words.
3. **Too much on every ad.** Our ads stack a headline, labels, bullets, a badge and a sub line. Theirs: one headline + product (+ one offer line). The long story goes in the primary text (Denture Care Tips, Receding Gums, Deborah Rustad: long first-person stories over a plain photo).
4. **Weak offers.** Theirs make the free stuff the hero: HiSmile "Get more free than you're buying", "buy 6 get 9 free" + gift box; KittySpout iOS Notes list with each extra struck through "($50) FREE"; Mars Men "Your FREE launch kit is waiting" with FREE tags on every item; Hollow "FREE beanie + 3 pairs free right now" handwritten on a sign; Hike "3 pairs for $99 / Last 100 pairs". Ours shows a small case and bag next to "40% OFF".
5. **No repeatable brand look.** Each brand has one: Sonic = dark/purple gradient + white sans + outlined pill; Zima = soft pastel + rounded sans; Hike = solid red + thin caps; PRIMALS = forest green + cream + gold stars strip; KittySpout = warm seasonal photo + stainless product. Because every one of our ads copied a different brand's layout, our batches have no identity. The "every ad recreates a different reference" rule caused that.

## New direction (proposed; needs the user's OK where it changes their rules)
1. **Three looks only, used consistently:** (A) **Product hero**: the Pod large (35-55% of the frame), premium light, ONE solid DentalPULSE color field (navy #0E283C, white, or soft blue) or a close, shallow-focus bathroom counter; one 3-8 word headline in Inter Bold; optional one offer line. (B) **Raw proof**: real-looking phone photo of the disgusting appliance (crusty retainer, yellow night guard, slimy denture, brown water in the basin), little or no text; the story lives in the primary text. (C) **Loud offer**: the free items are the hero (Notes list with struck values -> FREE, handwritten sign on the product pile, "more free than you're paying for").
2. **Copy with attitude** in the customers' own words (see `customer_voice_2026-11-14.md`): short, provocative, a little rude, never a claim we can't back.
3. **Compose, don't generate text.** Kie makes text-free photos only; layout and type are set in HTML with real Inter/Barlow (`ads/compose/render.js`), like Comfort Wear's composed pipeline. No more melted letters, no AI-looking text panels.
4. **Fewer, better ads**: depth over volume (the Comfort Wear "production method v2" lesson).
