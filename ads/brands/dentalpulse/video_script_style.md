# DentalPULSE video scripts: house style (from the owner's edits, 19 Nov 2026)

How the owner wants video scripts to read. It comes from the owner's line edits to DEN336 ("Best gift for night guard wearers") and from the last 60 days of our own Meta data (`atria_video_performance_60d_2026-11-19.md`). Brand rules and claims still come from `brand.json` and the project instructions. This file only covers voice and hooks.

## 1. The owner's edits: what changed and why

| StefanBrain draft | Owner's version | Rule it shows |
|---|---|---|
| "this is the one Black Friday gift they will genuinely thank you for every single morning" | "this is the one Black Friday gift they need to get rid of the yellow stains" | Hooks name the concrete problem and result (yellow stains), not a feeling or a sentimental payoff. |
| "If your spouse or partner wears a night guard" | "If your partner wears a night guard" | One word where one will do. |
| SUPER "The best Black Friday gift for someone who wears a night guard" | "The best Black Friday gift for your night guard" | Short supers, said straight to the viewer ("your"). |
| "eliminates that stale morning smell" | "gets rid of the morning smell" | Everyday words: "get rid of", not "eliminate". |
| "Skip the novelty gadgets this year. If someone you love wears a night guard…" | "If someone you love wears a night guard…" | No warm-up line. Start on the call-out. |
| "simply cannot reach" | "can't reach" | Contractions; talk like a person. |
| "chemical soaking tablets … often leave behind … lingering odor" | "dental tablets … leave behind … a stale smell" | No hedges ("often"). Plain nouns ("smell", not "odor"). |
| "dentists and oral-care specialists" | "dentists and specialists" | Cut jargon. |
| "removing the odor at the source" | "removing the root cause of the smell" | Plain words over copywriter phrasing. |
| "That is the trapped buildup that manual scrubbing left behind" | "That's the plaque buildup that manual scrubbing couldn't remove" | Name the thing (plaque), say what failed. |
| "completely odor-free" | "completely smell-free" | "Smell", every time. |
| "you or your gift recipient four full months" | "you four full months" | Talk to one person. |

**Overall:** slightly less technical. Keep one simple line of mechanism (sound waves, tap water, one button, 5 minutes). Don't stack specs (Hz, cavitation, UV-C, microscopic bubbles) in one paragraph. Say what the viewer sees and feels: yellow, cloudy film, smell, the dirty water at the bottom of the bowl.

## 2. Voice

- Plain, spoken, 60+ friendly. Short sentences. Contractions. Words a customer would use: smell, yellow, gross, film, gunk, plaque, buildup.
- Avoid: odor, eliminate, utilize, optimal, oral-care specialists, cavitation, recurring subscriptions (say "no tablets to keep buying"), "genuinely", "every single".
- One idea per line, so the editor can cut on each line.
- Definite, not hedged, but within the approved claims.
- Serious and credible. No jokes or puns (project rule).

## 3. Hooks: the most important part

The owner's priority is better hooks. What the last 60 days of our account say (hook = 3-second views / impressions; account average 25.8%):

- **The highest hooks open on a specific person in a specific gross moment, or a surprising claim about something they already own.** The Polident history opener hooked 62%, the "she can smell my dentures" drama 60%, the biofilm warning 45%, and "if you catch that smell when you hug your mom or dad" 41%.
- **The highest-ROAS hooks are tests and comparisons that call out someone who already owns, or is about to buy, a cleaner.** The DEN81 hooks (2.1–2.6 ROAS) open with "If you already own an ultrasonic denture cleaner, watch this…" or "I bought the seven most popular…".
- **Weak hooks are generic benefit claims.** "This makes denture cleaning so much easier" (CP1, gift) hooked 34% and returned 0.79.

Hook checklist (each of the 3 hooks per brief):
1. The first three words name the appliance or the person: dentures, night guard, your mom's dentures.
2. Lead with one concrete, visible or smellable problem: yellow stains, the smell, white film, the dirty water, adhesive goo.
3. Open a loop: a surprise, a confession, a contradiction, or a test result the viewer must stay to see.
4. The first frame is the gross close-up or the proof (the dirty water), not the product or a person smiling.
5. Spoken hook: one or two short sentences. SUPER: under about 8 words.
6. The three hooks must differ in angle (e.g. confession / test / gross reveal), not reword the same line three times.

## 4. Body (keep what's working)

Pain in their words, then what they tried (brushing scratches, tablets leave film), then one simple mechanism line, then the proof (dirty water after one 5-minute cycle), then the offer and the 120-day guarantee, then one CTA. Promise for night guards and retainers: "stops it turning yellow / clear again / no film / no smell". Never promise old yellow vanishes.

## 5. Masterclass lessons

Also apply `masterclass_lessons.md`, the lessons from Nathan Perdriau's copywriting masterclass the owner shared on 20 Nov. In short: the 9 beats (hook, problem, promise, mechanism, objection handling, proof, offer, risk reversal, CTA), with the promise before the mechanism. The hook must bridge into line 2. Agitate one real accommodation from the VOC files, not just the symptom. Use specifics, not adjectives. Anchor on the dirty-water demonstration or the 6-of-7 lab test. Frame the 120-day guarantee as a reason to buy.
