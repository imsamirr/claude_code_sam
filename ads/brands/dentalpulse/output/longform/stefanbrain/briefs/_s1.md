# Brief: DentalPULSE long-form statics DLF11 and DLF12

Please write two long-form static ads (one phone photo + a long first-person story as the Meta primary text) for DentalPULSE (project "Dentalpulsepro Launch"). Both are net-new. The concept facts are below; the product, offer, claims, research and our winners' DNA follow; the format limits and return fields are at the bottom.

## DLF11: dentures + hospital/aspiration pneumonia, told by someone who should have known
- **Persona:** denture caregiver (adult daughter of a denture wearer).
- **Narrator (facts to build on; name her yourself):** a woman, about 57, a hospital infection-prevention nurse in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Her whole job is swabbing bed rails, call buttons and IV poles and auditing hand-washing. Her mother, about 84, a retired seamstress, wears full dentures and lives with her. Late summer into fall (hurricane season, gumbo, a granddaughter's quinceañera or homecoming as the event the mother wants to make).
- **What happens:** the mother gets aspiration pneumonia after a minor procedure / a stomach bug; in the hospital, the narrator, of all people, realises nobody (including her) ever swabbed the one object that sits in her mother's mouth all night. The research fits her job: O'Donnell 2016 (pneumonia germs on about 64% of hospital patients' dentures), Quinn 2014 (daily oral care cut non-ventilator hospital pneumonia by about a third), Iinuma 2015.
- **Planned hook type:** professional irony (new for us; BeetWise's 911-dispatcher irony ad was rank 1 in last week's market study). **Open loop:** the one surface she never swabbed.
- **Lead type:** story lead; awareness: problem-aware (knows dentures need cleaning, doesn't know the denture is the reservoir). Messenger: a colleague from her world (a respiratory therapist or the hospital dental hygienist).
- **Aggressive:** she blames the denture outright for her mother's pneumonia.

## DLF12: dentures + denture stomatitis, the sunk-cost object
- **Persona:** denture wearer, 60+, a man who cleans with Polident and a brush.
- **Narrator:** a man, about 64, a retired long-haul trucker in Billings, Montana, widowed or divorced, proud. He paid about $4,200 for new dentures, and for months the roof of his mouth burned so badly that they spent more time in a cup than in his mouth; he ate soup, skipped the diner with his old driving buddies, avoided a grandson's games. Two rounds of antifungal rinse, the burn came back each time (denture stomatitis: Candida living in the acrylic re-seeds the palate). Winter.
- **Planned hook type:** sunk-cost object (new for us; Volosso's "an $87,000 RV in my driveway, haven't driven it once" body ran 294 days and was relaunched as the brand's #1). **Open loop:** why a $4,200 set of teeth lived in a cup.
- **Lead type:** problem-solution lead; awareness: problem-aware. Messenger: someone from his world (a waitress at the truck stop whose husband had the same thing, a denture lab tech he meets at the VA).
- **Aggressive:** he blames the plate outright for the burn and the infections and says plainly what changed.
