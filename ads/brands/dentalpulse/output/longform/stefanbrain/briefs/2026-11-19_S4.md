## DLF18: clear aligners + white spots/cavities, the classroom petri dish
- **Persona:** clear aligner/Invisalign wearer, 25–45.
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 31, a high school biology teacher in Tulsa, Oklahoma, mid-treatment ($5,000+ aligners), rinses and brushes her trays, uses cleaning crystals/tablets. For a lab unit, her 10th graders swab objects and grow them on agar plates; a student swabs her "clean" aligner tray and it grows the busiest plate in the room. Then a white spot / early cavity at her orthodontic check-in. Fall semester.
- **Mechanism:** the tray is a lid: whatever film is on it is held on the enamel 22 hours a day; brushing scratches the tray, giving the film more places to live. Proof by measurement (market study: measurement and logs work as proof): the plates before and after the Pod (a later swab grows next to nothing). Never claim a lab result for the product beyond the approved claims; the class experiment is her story.
- **Planned hook type:** the classroom experiment / found result (new for us). **Open loop:** whose plate grew the most, and what the class saw at the end.
- **Lead type:** story lead; awareness: unaware. Messenger: a student's parent who is a dental hygienist, or the school's science department head.
- **Aggressive:** the white spot / cavity blamed outright on the dirty tray.

Real research, attributed by year and type (use only these unless you know a real source):
- Iinuma et al. 2015, Journal of Dental Research: 524 people 85+ followed 3 years; those who slept in their dentures had more than twice the pneumonia risk, plus more denture plaque and Candida.
- O'Donnell et al. 2016 (Journal of Prosthodontics): germs that cause pneumonia were found on the dentures of about 64% of elderly hospital patients sampled.
- Yoneyama et al. 1999, The Lancet: Japanese nursing homes; residents given daily oral care had less pneumonia (about 11% vs 19%) and fewer pneumonia deaths.
- Quinn et al. 2014, Journal of Nursing Scholarship: a hospital system that added basic daily oral care cut non-ventilator hospital pneumonia by about a third (about 37%).
- Aspiration pneumonia: in sleep and when sick or sedated, swallow and cough reflexes weaken and mouth germs slip into the lungs.
- Denture stomatitis: a red, burning palate under the plate, caused by Candida yeast living in the porous acrylic; up to about two-thirds of denture wearers; overnight wear and poor cleaning raise the risk; antifungals fail because the yeast re-seeds from the denture.
- Partials: studies of removable partial denture wearers (e.g. Zlatarić et al. 2002, Journal of Periodontology) found more plaque and gum disease on the teeth that hold the clasps than on other teeth.
- FDA, 2008: a public health notice on allergic reactions to persulfate in denture cleansers. Say "back in 2008", never "just issued".
- Tablets work on the surface. Biofilm lives in microscopic pores and scratches in the acrylic; brushing adds more scratches. Acrylic absorbs water and whatever is in it.
- Aligners and retainers are a lid: whatever film is on the tray is held against the enamel up to 22 hours a day (white spots, cavities).
- Night guards sit wet in a closed case about 16 hours a day; mold and bacteria grow there.
