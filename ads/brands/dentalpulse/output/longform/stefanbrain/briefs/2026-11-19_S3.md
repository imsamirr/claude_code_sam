## DLF15: dentures + nursing home pneumonia, the deadline and the found object
- **Persona:** denture caregiver (adult daughter).
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 49, a court reporter in Sacramento, California. Her mother, about 86, is moving into assisted living / memory care on a fixed Friday at 9 a.m.; the narrator is packing up the house with a deadline. Under the bathroom sink she finds her mother's denture cup and years of tablet boxes, and the intake checklist asks about the "denture cleaning routine". The facility's nurse tells her daily oral care is a pneumonia question (Yoneyama 1999, The Lancet: about 11% vs 19% pneumonia with daily oral care, fewer deaths; Iinuma 2015). Her mother already had one pneumonia the winter before. Early spring.
- **Planned hook type:** hard deadline + found object (new for us; PhotoStick's "anything left in the closets by Friday 8 AM" was rank 1). **Open loop:** what was in the cup, and the deadline itself.
- **Lead type:** story lead; awareness: problem-aware. Ends with the Pod in the mother's new room. A natural P.S.: a second unit for the other parent / sibling's house.
- **Aggressive:** the narrator blames the dirty plate for last winter's pneumonia.

## DLF16: night guard + mold, the insider voyeur
- **Persona:** teeth grinder (TMJ/bruxism), 30–60, night guard gone yellow and cloudy.
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 38, a housekeeper at a high-end resort hotel in Las Vegas, Nevada; she grinds (stress, two kids, night shifts). On the bathroom counters of the richest guests (penthouse suites, the touring performers) she keeps seeing the same thing: a small steel pod with a guard or retainer in it, never a cup of fizzing tablets. Her own guard lives wet in a closed case in her locker 16 hours a day, smells musty, has dark specks, and she has had a sore throat and sinus trouble for months. Summer heat.
- **Planned hook type:** insider voyeur / "the one thing the rich all have" (new for us; Fleece's "I'm a nanny in the Hamptons…" was rank 1). **Open loop:** what the guests keep on the counter and why.
- **Lead type:** secret lead; awareness: unaware (doesn't know the guard is the problem). Never name or brand-describe a competitor pod; the guests' pods are unbranded, and she buys the DentalPULSE Pod after a guest (a singer or a dentist on vacation) explains UV-C plus ultrasonic.
- **Aggressive:** mold and bacteria in the guard blamed outright for her throat and sinuses. No whitening promise (old yellow stains don't fully reverse; say the cloudiness and smell went, not that it looks new).

Real research, attributed by year and type (use only these unless you know a real source):
- Iinuma et al. 2015, Journal of Dental Research: 524 people 85+ followed 3 years; those who slept in their dentures had more than twice the pneumonia risk, plus more denture plaque and Candida.
- O'Donnell et al. 2016 (Journal of Prosthodontics): germs that cause pneumonia were found on the dentures of about 64% of elderly hospital patients sampled.
- Yoneyama et al. 1999, The Lancet: Japanese nursing homes; residents given daily oral care had less pneumonia (about 11% vs 19%) and fewer pneumonia deaths.
- Quinn et al. 2014, Journal of Nursing Scholarship: a hospital system that added basic daily oral care cut non-ventilator hospital pneumonia by about a third (about 37%).
- Aspiration pneumonia: in sleep and when sick or sedated, swallow and cough reflexes weaken and mouth germs slip into the lungs.
- Denture stomatitis: a red, burning palate under the plate, caused by Candida yeast living in the porous acrylic; up to about two-thirds of denture wearers; overnight wear and poor cleaning raise the risk; antifungals fail because the yeast re-seeds from the denture.
- Partials: studies of removable partial denture wearers (e.g. Zlatarić et al. 2002, Journal of Periodontology) found more plaque and gum disease on the teeth that hold the clasps than on other teeth.
- FDA, 2008: a public health notice on allergic reactions to persulfate in denture cleansers. Say "back in 2008", never "just issued".
- Tablets work on the surface. Biofilm lives in microscopic pores and scratches in the acrylic; brushing adds more scratches. Acrylic absorbs water and whatever is in it.
- Aligners and retainers are a lid: whatever film is on the tray is held against the enamel up to 22 hours a day (white spots, cavities).
- Night guards sit wet in a closed case about 16 hours a day; mold and bacteria grow there.
