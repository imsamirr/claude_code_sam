## DLF21: dentures + aspiration pneumonia, someone else's moment at the holiday table (witness narrator)
- **Persona:** denture caregiver (son-in-law watching his mother-in-law; he's the buyer).
- **Angle → mechanism:** aspiration pneumonia. She sleeps in / barely cleans her dentures; the biofilm in the acrylic's pores is what slips into her lungs at night when the swallow reflex is weak; tablets only clean the surface.
- **Narrator (facts to build on; names yours):** a man, about 50, a piano tuner in Chattanooga, Tennessee, who notices sounds for a living. His mother-in-law, about 81, a retired church pianist, full dentures, lives alone two streets over. Thanksgiving last year into January. He noticed her small wet cough at the table and the same cough every morning before anyone else did; in January she's admitted with aspiration pneumonia.
- **Reference structures to model:** (1) the Auniva long-runners' "dated domestic incident about someone else" opener (ordinary object + day/month + place + one small physical failure; narrator's own stake arrives at 8–12%; e.g. "My father dropped a coffee mug on his kitchen floor in El Paso last March…", 126 days); (2) the witness-buyer format (Cuurt): opens on a behavior mystery, an evidence device that becomes the results log (here: his tuner's notebook where he jotted the cough), a free at-home test, three checks, a cheap-copy kill, a social-repair payoff; (3) Ploise's sister-ER pneumonia story (153 days) for the stakes.
- **Core emotion:** dread, then guilt ("I heard it every week and said nothing").
- **Hook type:** dated domestic incident / someone else's moment (new for us). **Open loop:** what the cough was.
- **Reveal setup:** at the hospital, a respiratory therapist explains (he half-apologizes for not bringing her in sooner); then "wait, what does that even mean?". Lead type: story; awareness: unaware → problem-aware. Research: Iinuma 2015, Yoneyama 1999, aspiration while asleep.
- **Aggressive:** he blames the dentures outright for the pneumonia.

## DLF22: dentures + denture stomatitis, the lie she told her husband
- **Persona:** denture wearer, 60+, a woman, Polident and a brush.
- **Angle → mechanism:** denture stomatitis. Candida yeast lives in the porous acrylic of the upper plate, so the rinse heals the palate and the plate re-seeds it; tablets and brushing never reach the pores.
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 66, owns a small bakery in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, up at 4 a.m. For two years she told her husband the burning in her mouth was "acid reflux" and took antacids in front of him, because she was ashamed it was her teeth. She quit tasting her own pie fillings; a regular said the crumb cake "tasted off". Early spring.
- **Reference structures to model:** Anna Janeston's "I faked jaw pain to my husband for 3 years" (launched 11-16/17, many hooks on one 12.5k body): a secret lie to a spouse, the spouse drives her to a specialist, the specialist says "this isn't your [X]", the lie is exposed; concealment → confession loop. Plus our DEN64 native "read this before…" ($35k, 2.06 ROAS) for the mechanism beats.
- **Core emotion:** shame, then relief at being found out.
- **Hook type:** concealment / the lie told to a spouse (new for us). **Open loop:** what she was actually hiding and why the specialist laughed.
- **Reveal setup:** the appointment: her husband books a GI doctor for the "reflux", the GI doctor looks in her mouth and sends her to a prosthodontist/dentist who lifts the plate and shows the red print of it on her palate. Lead type: secret/confession; awareness: problem-aware.
- **Aggressive:** she names the plate as the cause outright.
