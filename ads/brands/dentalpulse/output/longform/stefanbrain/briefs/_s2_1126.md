## DLF23: dentures + the smell, and the outsider who told her son first (witness narrator)
- **Persona:** denture caregiver (adult son, buyer, long distance).
- **Angle → mechanism:** smell. Acrylic is porous like a kitchen sponge; the biofilm in the pores gives off sulfur gases; soaking and tablets clean the surface, so the smell comes back by lunch.
- **Narrator:** a man, about 52, an airline mechanic in Indianapolis. His mother, about 78, a retired school-crossing guard, lives in Dayton; he lives 2 hours away. The person who calls him is his mother's hairdresser of 30 years, who stands behind her chair every other Friday and finally says: "Honey, something's wrong with your mom's mouth." Mom had stopped going to her Friday card group. Winter.
- **Reference structures to model:** Marie Peterseon (seniors, 16k): "I hadn't opened my front door in 19 days. My daughter found out because the mail carrier told her." An outsider notices before the family does. Plus the witness-buyer format (behavior mystery: why she quit card night; evidence device; free test; three checks; cheap-copy kill; social-repair payoff: back at card night).
- **Core emotion:** guilt (a stranger noticed before her own son), protectiveness.
- **Hook type:** outsider notices first (new for us). **Open loop:** what the hairdresser smelled and why Mom quit card night.
- **Reveal setup:** a phone call with a friend who happens to be an expert (his college roommate is now a dental hygienist) who explains the sponge mechanism; then "wait, what does that even mean?". Lead type: story; awareness: unaware.
- **One pain point only:** the smell and what it took from her socially. No disease in this one.

## DLF24: dentures + tablets fail, told by the biofilm itself (drama format)
- **Persona:** denture wearer, 60+, who drops a Polident tablet in every night and feels "clean".
- **Angle → mechanism:** tablets only clean the surface. The narrator IS the biofilm living in the scratches and pores of a man's upper denture; it explains, smugly, how it survives the tablet every night (the fizz cleans the top layer, it waits in the pores), the brush (which carves it new scratches to live in), and the $30 pod; it shows the cost to him (the smell, the white film, the burning gums, his wife turning her cheek). Until the night the water starts shaking 42,000 times a second and the UV-C light comes on.
- **Host:** a man, about 70, a retired Amtrak conductor in Charleston, West Virginia. Wife of 45 years.
- **Reference structure to model:** Matter Nutrition's 176-second video (rank 1/17): the disease is personified and speaks in first person: "I am high blood pressure... cutting salt is not going to make me go away"; "monitoring means they are watching me win"; "a second tablet, a third". Personified-enemy monologue is fresh in oral care (format-fatigue check: nobody in dental runs it). Brand still named 50–65% through, as the thing the biofilm fears; end with the biofilm's last line as the water turns brown.
- **Core emotion:** disgust and indignation (it's been laughing at his nightly routine).
- **Hook type:** personified enemy monologue (new for us). **Open loop:** the one thing it's afraid of.
- **Lead type:** big idea / villain lead; awareness: solution-aware (he thinks tablets work).
- **One pain point:** tablets leave it alive. Keep the price inversion (years of tablets vs $99.95).
