## DLF27: night guard + winter chest infections (respiratory), the compliance paradox
- **Persona:** teeth grinder, 30–60, a man.
- **Angle → mechanism:** respiratory infections (owner's idea: "respiratory issues from night guards"). The guard sits wet in a closed case 16 hours a day, then goes into his mouth all night where he breathes and swallows over it; the film in its pores is what keeps seeding the chest; rinsing doesn't touch it.
- **Narrator:** a man, about 49, a commercial lobster/fishing-boat captain out of Gloucester, Massachusetts. He quit smoking in 2019 "for his lungs", runs, eats right; and every winter since he got the guard he's had bronchitis, twice it turned into pneumonia and he missed the best weeks of the season. Winter on the water.
- **Reference structures to model:** playbook hook 2, compliance paradox ("My hunting buddy quit drinking in July. He was dead by Halloween."); and the Try Pup Labs 161s video structure: tips listicle pivots to "the part nobody talks about" (mouth bacteria → organs). The witness here is his wife, who first connects the dates (the guard's receipt date and his first bronchitis in the urgent-care log).
- **Core emotion:** betrayal (he did everything right), fear for his livelihood.
- **Hook type:** compliance paradox (new for us). **Open loop:** what was really in his lungs.
- **Reveal setup:** in person with an expert friend (the respiratory therapist from his old crew's wife / a pulmonologist at urgent care) explains; "wait, what does that even mean?". Lead: problem-solution; awareness: unaware.
- **Aggressive:** he blames the guard outright for the infections and the lost weeks.

## DLF28: night guard + yellow film and tablets that made it brittle, an interactive self-test
- **Persona:** teeth grinder, 30–60, a woman, whose guard went yellow and cloudy, soaked in retainer tablets.
- **Angle → mechanism:** tablets weakening the appliance + yellowing. Tablets bleach the surface and leave the film in the pores; her $650 custom guard went cloudy, chalky, then cracked at the edge after a year of nightly tablets. (Don't promise the old yellow comes back clear: it doesn't fully reverse; the win is the film, the smell, and the next guard staying clear.)
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 41, a veterinary technician in Albany, New York, who cleans dogs' teeth with an ultrasonic scaler every day at work, and never connected it to her own guard. Fall.
- **Reference structures to model:** playbook hook 14, interactive task ("Right now. Go grab your shoes. Flip them over.") → here: hold your guard up to a window / scratch it with a fingernail; the free self-test is the hook AND the CTA. Plus the professional-irony twist (her job is the analogy: she uses ultrasonic on dogs, never on herself).
- **Core emotion:** disgust, then the "how did I not see it" self-laugh.
- **Hook type:** interactive task / self-test in line 1 (new for us). **Open loop:** what the reader will see on their own guard.
- **Reveal setup:** self-discovery (a vet on her shift asks why she doesn't run her guard in the scaler tank). Lead type: proclamation/test; awareness: problem-aware.
- **One pain point:** the cloudy, film-coated guard that cracked ($650 replacement).
