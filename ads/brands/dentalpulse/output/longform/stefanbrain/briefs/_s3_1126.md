## DLF25: dentures + the cost of replacing them, a near-miss at checkout
- **Persona:** denture wearer, 60+, a woman, whose plate smells and has a white film; she believes it's "worn out".
- **Angle → mechanism:** the cost of replacing appliances. The film and smell aren't age, they're biofilm living in the pores; a new $2,600 set would have the same film in a month with the same tablet routine.
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 71, a retired school-cafeteria manager in Asheville, North Carolina. Her finger was on the "sign" line of a $2,600 financing form for a new set of dentures at a denture clinic one Tuesday in late October when her grandson (a 19-year-old who works at a car-detailing shop and knows what an ultrasonic bath does to a dirty part) texts her a question. The trade analogy: the detailing shop's ultrasonic parts washer that pulls grime out of injectors no brush can reach.
- **Reference structures to model:** PhotoStick m1127648890268432 point-of-no-return near-miss ("my finger was half an inch above the glass signature pad…"), with its back half: timestamped failed fixes with prices, a relative at ~40%, brand at 43–55%, a 3-point block, a log, offer and P.S.; plus the "tradesman whose trade is the analogy" bridge (market study 11-23 §5).
- **Core emotion:** near-regret, then vindication (she kept $2,500).
- **Hook type:** point-of-no-return near-miss (new for us). **Open loop:** what the text said.
- **Reveal setup:** family member (the grandson) in person with the trade analogy. Lead type: story; awareness: problem-aware.
- **One pain point:** "I thought I needed new teeth." Careful: no whitening or "brand new again" promise; the win is the smell and film gone and the money kept.

## DLF26: night guard + cavities, the dentist thought she was lying about soda
- **Persona:** teeth grinder (bruxism), 30–60, woman, night guard 4 years old, rinsed and brushed.
- **Angle → mechanism:** cavities. A night guard is a lid: whatever biofilm lives in its pores is pressed against the back molars 8 hours a night; brushing the guard scratches it and makes more room for bacteria.
- **Narrator:** a woman, about 38, a court interpreter (Spanish/English) in Las Cruces, New Mexico (she drives to the El Paso courts). She doesn't drink soda, flosses daily, and at her cleaning the dentist found three new cavities on the back molars and asked, twice, how much soda she drinks. She felt accused, like a witness under cross-examination. Summer.
- **Reference structures to model:** misdiagnosis reversal (playbook hook 8: "Turns out I don't have plantar fasciitis, and you might not either"); and Smooch / James Miami MUA (281s): an expert shows the woman the product was the problem, not her ("My skin's just gotten old"), with a half-and-half demo: here the hygienist does a disclosing-tablet stain on the guard and it turns purple exactly where the cavities are.
- **Core emotion:** being disbelieved, then vindication.
- **Hook type:** misdiagnosis reversal / accused of a habit she doesn't have (new for us). **Open loop:** where the cavities were really coming from.
- **Reveal setup:** the hygienist appointment (she half-apologizes for her teeth; the hygienist explains; "wait, what does that even mean?"). Lead type: problem-solution; awareness: unaware.
- **Aggressive:** she blames the guard outright for the three fillings ($600+ out of pocket).
