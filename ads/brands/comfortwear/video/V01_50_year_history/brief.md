# V01: "What 50 years of shoes did to your feet" (Comfort Wear)

**Format:** animated storybook history lesson, long form (5:10), narrated, word-by-word captions
**Inspiration:** HIKE Footwear, "WHAT 50 YEARS OF SHOES DID TO YOUR FEET" ([Atria](https://app.tryatria.com/ad/m2259171631592792)), live since 2026-10-08, 5:09, 4:5, best impression rank 51 of 1,316 in its set
**Product:** Ortho Stretch Cushion Shoe, light grey colorway, $59.95 (was $90)
**Persona:** older women (47% of buyers are 65+) who have spent decades on their feet. The nurse thread in the story carries the winning nurse persona in with them.
**Deliverables:** `V01_50_year_history_4x5.mp4` (feed) and `V01_50_year_history_9x16.mp4` (Reels/Stories, cream pads plus headline)

## 2. The key adaptation: HIKE's enemy is foam. Comfort Wear *is* foam.

HIKE sells thin, flat, barefoot-style shoes, so its villain is cushioning and tall soles. The Ortho Stretch has a **chunky cushioned rocker midsole**, so copying the script would argue against our own product. The story was rebuilt around a villain that Comfort Wear actually beats:

> **For 50 years, shoes were built for the store shelf and for runners, not for feet that stand all day.**

| HIKE chapter | Comfort Wear chapter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2M years: wide, flat feet that felt the ground | 2M years: wide toes that spread to balance you, an arch that works like a spring, people who *stood* all day | Sets up the wide toe box and arch support, not ground feel |
| 1970s: foam arrives | 1970s: **shoes go jogging**. Built for a 30-minute jog, and then nurses, cashiers and waitresses stand in them for 12 hours | Proven "running shoes last 2 hours, ComfortWear lasts 12" angle |
| 80s/90s: marketing machine | 80s/90s: **the shelf wins**. New model every year, air pockets and gel pods, and toe room quietly disappears | Kept almost as is, aimed at the toe box |
| 90s: memory foam makes feet weak | 90s: memory foam is **"a pillow: press it all day and it stays pressed. By lunch you're standing on a pancake."** | Same competitor, but the problem is foam that *collapses*, not cushioning as such (matches concept C17, "foam gives up at hour 4") |
| 2002: the clog | 2002: the clog, **no back, so your toes clench** (fist demo kept) | Comfort Wear has a full heel that hugs, so "your toes can finally let go" pays it off |
| 2010s: tall soles → wobble | 2010s: **running shoes go to work**. Built to soak up bounce for an hour; standing is pressure, hour after hour; a narrow toe box; $170 | Avoids "thick soles are bad" (ours is thick) |
| Senior shoe: more of the problem | **The $300 fix**: insoles and orthotics, "a chair with no seat, then you rent the cushion" | Proven price-anchor angle (203 UGC, 3.05 ROAS); 11% of survey respondents use insoles |
| Today / "I'm just getting old" | Same: limping to the car after a shift, rubbing heels on the edge of the bed | Reframe kept |
| Going back | Ortho Stretch: wide toe box, **stretch-knit for bunions and swelling**, arch support built in, a real back, rocker sole "made for standing, not sprinting" | Every claim is in live Comfort Wear ads or brand.json |

## 3. Compliance and claims (from brand.json and research)

- **No money-back guarantee, "no questions asked" or "send them back".** Refunds are the #1 cause of 1★ reviews. HIKE's "30-day guarantee" was cut. The homework CTA stays, and the close points to the **size guide**, since 60% of unhappy customers got the wrong size.
- **No "orthopedic approved", "podiatrist" or "doctor recommended", and no cure claims.** Medical language is experiential ("aching heels", "angry bunions", "gives where bunions and swelling need room"). We don't say shoes *cause* bunions, which HIKE does.
- **No competitor logos or products** in the art. Crocs, Skechers and Hoka are named in the voiceover only, and the shoes are drawn as generic, unbranded versions. Comparative statements are framed as design facts or opinion (running shoes are built for running; a clog has no back; memory foam compresses). **Ask legal** whether you're comfortable naming brands in paid media. HIKE does it, but Hoka and Skechers are litigious. The "brandless" fallback hook is in §6.
- **No review counts or star ratings.** The 500k / 4.9★ figures in live ads aren't confirmed in brand.json, so the testimonial beat uses real Trustpilot phrases without counts.
- **Price:** $59.95, was $90 (brand.json). If a 40%-off promo is running when this launches, swap `s54`.

## 5. Production spec (how this cut was made)

- **Art:** Kie.ai `nano-banana-pro`, 4:5 at 2K. Every character scene gets `refs/characters.jpg` (grandma, granddaughter, nurse) as a reference so they stay consistent. Every product scene gets the studio photo `reference/shoe_light_grey.png`.
- **Voice:** OpenAI `gpt-4o-mini-tts`, voice "sage" at 1.25x speed (about 165 wpm, close to HIKE's pace), with leading and trailing silence trimmed from each line. Kie's ElevenLabs endpoint returned "Internal Error" on every try on 2026-10-29. To swap in an ElevenLabs voice later, regenerate `vo/` and rerun `render`.
- **Music:** Kie Suno V6 instrumental, a gentle acoustic storybook bed at about −18 dB under the voice.
- **Motion:** slow Ken Burns move per scene (push-in, drift, tilt), set by `move` in `scenes.json`. Hero scenes can be upgraded to real animation with Kie image-to-video by adding an `"anim"` prompt and running `python ads/video.py <dir> anim`.
- **Captions:** the script's own words aligned to Whisper word timings, with numbers shown as numerals ($59.95, 12-hour), 2 to 5 words per line, Poppins Bold white with a black stroke, lower third. The price and URL appear as large captions near the top.
- **Chapter cards:** a generated blank paper card with the title lettered in Patrick Hand.
- **Build:** `python ads/video.py ads/brands/comfortwear/video/V01_50_year_history all`, cached per step.

## 6. Test plan and variants

1. **Launch as is** in TOF against the current video winners (Kyro369v2, NurseNotRunners). Long-form explainers often show weak 3-second hold but strong conversion, so judge on CPA and ROAS after $300 or more, not on hook rate.
2. **Hook swaps** (re-cut only `s01`–`s06`, everything else stays):
   - A (current): "Crocs gave you clenched toes. Skechers gave you foam that goes flat by lunch. Hokas gave you a running shoe for a job where nobody runs."
   - B (brandless, legal-safe): "Your clogs gave you clenched toes. Your memory foam went flat by lunch. Your running shoes were built for a job where nobody runs."
   - C (nurse): "If you work 12-hour shifts, here's the 50-year history of why your feet hurt, explained like you're five."
3. **Cutdowns:** a 60 s version (hook, Ch. 5 fist demo, Ch. 7 $300 fix, last chapter) for retargeting; a 2 min version without Ch. 1 and Ch. 3.
4. **Spanish:** write the script natively, not translated ("los pies me mataban", "doble turno"), and reuse all the art.
