### Why It Works (Direct-Response Teardown)
1. **High-Authority Problem Callout:** Leading with a verified research fact ("women are up to 10x more likely to get Morton's neuroma") combined with a visual study cutout stops the scroll with objective credibility rather than a sales pitch.
2. **Instant Sensory Matching:** Describing the burning sensation and the phantom "pebble in my shoe" immediately triggers the private internal sentence of the target viewer. Sufferers self-select within the first five seconds.
3. **The Failed-Fixes Ladder:** Naming real, expensive brands that viewers have already bought (Hoka ~\$165, On Cloud ~\$180, Brooks ~\$140, Skechers ~\$90, and \$300 custom orthotics) validates the customer's frustration. It proves the creator is in the trenches with them and prevents the objection: *"Why wouldn't I just buy running shoes?"*
4. **The Doctor-Appointment Twist (The Insole Overhang Demo):** Instead of making the customer feel defective, the doctor character provides absolution. The doctor physically demonstrates that standard running shoes have narrow "triangle" toe boxes and squishy foam that lets the forefoot sink under load, mechanically crushing the metatarsals together.
5. **Belief Anchor & Sensory Relief:** The line *"their feet didn't hurt. Not hurt less, didn't hurt"* delivers the critical direct-response transition from skeptical management to actual day-to-day comfort.
6. **Physical Show-Me Proof:** Moving from talking head to tactile shoe inspection (stretching the knit, showing the rocker sole, slipping the foot in using the elastic no-tie laces, walking on hard floor) grounds the claims in visible reality.
7. **Collector / Multi-Pair Close:** Ending with multiple colors on camera and *"I got them in more than one color"* normalizes buying several pairs while driving home the Black Friday sitewide savings.
