## Facts you can't see (Comfort Wear, Thursday 2026-11-26 long-form batch, launches Thanksgiving week)

**Product (exact):** Comfort Wear Ortho Stretch Cushion Shoe, a women's shoe. $59.95 (was $90; older ads used "about $60, it was $120"). Features, each mapped to a failure: stretch-knit upper that gives as feet swell; structural arch support built into the shoe (nothing to pack flat, no insert); cushioned, firm-but-flexible stable sole (steady, not squishy); wide toe box; slip-resistant grip; light; looks like a clean, normal sneaker, not orthopedic. Women wear it; men appear only as side characters, narrators or gift-buyers.

**The one hero mechanism every concept traces back to (state it so it makes sense in the reader's head):** soft foam (memory foam, max-cushion running shoes) compresses under body weight within hours, so by afternoon the arch has nothing under it and the heel takes every step on hard ground; and feet swell during the day, so a fixed upper that fit at breakfast squeezes by 3 p.m.; and heavy shoes get lifted about 20,000 times a day. The fix: arch support built into the shoe that holds its shape all day, a stretch-knit top that gives as feet swell, a light shoe with a firm-but-flexible stable sole.

**Live offer on launch (Nov 26 to Dec 5, 2026): Black Friday Sale.** EITHER "60% off sitewide" OR "buy 2 pairs, get 2 free gifts (a 3-pack of cushioned socks + a travel bag)". Never both in one ad. Name the end date (December 5th). No dollar value for the gifts. No shipping speed, no "free/easy returns".
**Ready-to-paste swaps (write one per concept):**
- Christmas Sale, Dec 6 to Dec 31, 2026: "60% off, plus 2 free gifts when you buy 2 pairs" (ends December 31st).
- New Year Sale, Jan 1 to Jan 15, 2027: "40% off with free shipping" (ends January 15th).

**Confirmed claims (use):** 30-day money-back guarantee, full refund within 30 days (always 30); 500,000+ customers ("500,000+"); HSA/FSA eligible; doctor-recommended and podiatrist-recommended (doctor or podiatrist characters are allowed: a doctor who recommends the shoe, a doctor reversal). Never name a real doctor, never invent a count of doctors.
**Never:** "orthopedic approved"; cure or treatment claims (a diagnosis like plantar fasciitis can be the character's own experience, but the shoe never "fixes/treats/cures" it); star ratings; free or easy returns; shipping speed; "easy slip-on" / "hands-free" (about 280 complaints about a tight collar); extra-wide or "fits everyone"; instant relief; "walking on clouds"; waterproof or steel toe; bunion or X-ray claims. Unconfirmed product facts (B-width last, EU tooling, no half sizes) stay out. Don't imply the reader's religion, health condition, age or other personal attribute (Meta personal-attributes policy): tell it as the narrator's story.

**Our winners' DNA (prospecting; spend · ROAS to 2026-11-24; keep their wording):**
- Framing: "Comfortwear doesn't make shoes for athletes. They make shoes for people whose feet have to survive real life." / "Built for the shift, not the 5k." / "Not built for a runner, a lab, or a weekend."

**Objections to answer (voice-of-customer synthesis; each concept lists its own):**
1. Trust / "will I get my money back?" (#1 everywhere; Hike and Orthora buyers on Reddit describe partial-refund traps; "I'm old enough to know better than to buy shoes from a Facebook ad"). Answer: the plain 30-day money-back guarantee, a real company, 500,000+.
2. "Is it a flimsy knit sock with a sole?" (knit look-alikes returned for no support: "not for 2 hour shift work let alone 12 hrs"). Answer: arch built in, firm stable sole, "not a sock with a sole".
3. Sizing/width doubts (don't promise extra wide).
4. Scam / dropship suspicion.
5. Fit for MY job / dress code (teachers: "can't wear sneakers"; nurses: fluids).
6. Stability over squish (60+: "seniors falling in high-stack soft shoes"; Hoka "made me trip").
7. Durability (premium foam packs out ~6 months; cheap knit dies in 1–3 months).
8. Price: $60 sits between $12–34 dupes and $108–200 premium.

**The witness format (what is scaling in our category right now).** A same-category brand (Cuurt, comfort court shoes for 60+) found a long-form format in November that is now its best-ranked statics (7.5–12.5k characters, 12–18+ days, one concept in 8+ copies). Our LF15 (included below) is our version of it. The mechanics, never their lines or stories:
- The narrator is the BUYER who watches the sufferer (a daughter, son, niece, coworker). The sufferer is always a woman (women's shoe).
- The opening shows BEHAVIOR, not feet: she skips plans, sits down first, "holds the bags", snaps at someone she loves, blames "tired". It reads as a mystery about someone you love. The foot pain is revealed at 25–40%.
- Hook shape: "I [did an ordinary thing for an unrelated reason]. It showed me [her hidden behavior]. It took [an event], [a consequence] and [an unexpected messenger] to find out why." Names and exact numbers in sentence 2.
- An EVIDENCE DEVICE proves the decline and later becomes the results log, week by week, in observed behavior (not pain scores).
- A free 2-minute at-home test, repeated in the P.S.: the THUMB-DENT test (press the insole heel; if the dent stays, the foam is packed flat), the 3 P.M. SQUEEZE (fits at breakfast, pinches by afternoon, so the top doesn't give when feet swell), the BRICK test (it feels heavy in the hand; it's lifted 20,000 times a day).
- Three checks (arch built in, a top that stretches, light). Brand at 60–75% as "the only one that passed all three".
- A cheap-copy kill ($40–45 "memory foam" or "arch support" shoe: the same foam, cheaper).
- A mirror line from a third party ("nobody ever told them it was the shoes").
- A social-repair payoff (the apology, the plan she comes back to), a stacked P.S. ("check every shoe against the test, including Comfort Wear") and gift framing ("a pair for her and a pair for you").

**Creative notes from the team (Nov 23):** every concept is built on a reference: model its structure closely, for our brand. One dominant pain point per ad. Keep the winner's core emotion; change narrator, situation, location and details. Hooks: situational, specific micro-moments, open loops that the hook does not close; no AI tropes ("we need to talk", "you need to see this", "here's the truth", "let that sink in"). Of the 3 alternate hooks per concept, at least one is a single-sentence, curiosity-heavy, completely open-ended hook. Stakes: something lost, risked or embarrassing. Write like people talk.

**Market study 2026-11-23 (other brands' long-runners):** fixed bodies re-hooked 3 ways run 132–306 days; dated domestic incidents about someone else (a parent, a coworker) as openers ran 124–144 days; a market ad's first-week rank is not evidence; the prophesied timeline (the messenger predicts the first weeks; the narrator lives it "just like she said") is rising; "week one, nothing" reads false for a shoe you feel at once, so use a reframed first result.

## Format and what to return (every concept in this brief)
- Long-form static Meta ad: one phone photo + the story as the primary text. 8,000–13,000 characters (unless the concept says otherwise), grade 4–6 reading level, short lines. Measure it.
- Brand position as the concept says (witness: 60–75%; first person: 50–65%), with the price and price inversion. Every enemy appears with its price and failure mode as failed fixes. Results with an honest first beat, each loss paid off, the opening image reversed.
- Close: the live Black Friday offer with its December 5th end date, the 30-day money-back guarantee, 500,000+, HSA/FSA, a CTA, then 3 P.S. lines (the at-home test, a second pair or gift, a share). Then the two offer swaps (Christmas, New Year) as paste-ready close paragraphs at the end of primary_text under a line "OFFER SWAPS".
- 3 alternate hooks per concept (lines 1–2 only, so they sit on the same body), each a different hook type, at least one a single open-ended sentence.
- headline (≤40 characters), cta ("Learn More").
- scroll_script: a 650–720 word version of the same story for a scrolling-text video (same hook, story beats and close; live offer only, no swaps). No symbols at all (no $, %, &, #, emojis, bullets), numbers as spoken ("one hundred sixty-five dollars", "sixty percent off", "twelve-hour shift"), paragraphs of 1–3 short sentences, at most 35 words each, blank line between paragraphs. Count the words.
- persona: the persona from avatar_personas.md the concept is written for.
- why: angle and how it traces to the hero mechanism, core emotion, the one pain point, hook type, open-loop type, lead type, awareness level, one-belief sentence, narrator=witness|self, measured character count of primary_text, brand position %, and word count of scroll_script.
