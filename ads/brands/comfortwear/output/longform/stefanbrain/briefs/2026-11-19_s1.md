**Our winners' DNA (prospecting, 90 days to 2026-11-10 plus lifetime; keep the wording):**
- Framing: "Comfortwear doesn't make shoes for athletes. They make shoes for people whose feet have to survive real life." / "Built for the shift, not the 5k." / "Not built for a runner, a lab, or a weekend."
- Enemies with price and failure mode: Hoka $165–180 "felt great for 2 hours", foam built for straight-down impact so "on uneven ground your foot rolls side to side", "almost twice the weight" so "knees hurt more than their feet", unstable when you crouch · Brooks Ghost $140 built to move a runner forward, narrow tapered toe box "presses a bunion exactly where it hurts", "a hole worn clean through at the bunion" · On Cloud $180 "triangle-shaped toe box, not made for your feet; real tight by lunch" · Nike Air Max $130 built for "running tracks and gym floors", "the air bubble is there for the look, not your arch" · Skechers memory foam $70–90 "compresses under your weight and doesn't recover until you take them off", "least support right when you need it most" · Dansko clogs ~$150–200 rigid footbed "rock hard from day one", heaviest · Vionic $130 "rigid mold, one fixed shape, and feet don't hold one shape. They swell." · Birkenstock $110 cork "takes your foot shape and none of its impact", backless so "toes clench all day".
- Mechanism: "Your feet aren't wrecked from walking, they're wrecked from standing." Running foam is "tuned to spring you through a stride, not hold you up standing still", so it "packs flat by hour four". Feet swell up to half a size: "swollen like sausages by mid-shift", "the shoe that fit at breakfast feels two sizes smaller". Heavy shoes get lifted 20,000 times a day. Wet/mopped floors. Travel: cobblestones are uneven ground; running shoes roll.
- Price inversion: "$60 outscored the $180 pair." "The highest score on my board at the lowest price on it."
- Outcome lines: "Not hurt less. Didn't hurt." / "You're not the one sitting on a bench watching other people see what you flew there to see." / "Getting to the last bell with something left." / "Picked up an extra shift because I felt good enough to."
- Wasted-money opener (Aubrey UGC, $19.6k): "$180 for Hokas, $200 for Danskos, $150 for Brooks. All sitting in my closet."

**Top objections to answer (voice of customer synthesis, 2026-11-18; answer the ones marked per concept):**
1. Trust / "will I get my money back?" (#1 everywhere; Hike and Orthora buyers on Reddit describe partial-refund traps; "I'm old enough to know better than to buy shoes from a Facebook ad"). Answer: the plain 30-day money-back guarantee, a real company, 500,000+.
2. "Is it a flimsy knit sock with a sole?" (Orthora knit look-alikes returned for no support: "not for 2 hour shift work let alone 12 hrs"). Answer: arch built in, firm stable sole, "not a sock with a sole".
3. Sizing/width doubts (don't promise extra wide).
4. Scam / dropship suspicion.
5. Fit for MY job / dress code (teachers: "can't wear sneakers"; ER nurses: fluids).
6. Stability over squish (60+: "seniors falling in high-stack soft shoes"; Hoka "made me trip").
7. Durability (premium foam packs out ~6 months; cheap knit dies in 1–3 months).
8. Price: $60 sits between $12–34 dupes and $108–200 premium.

**Market study 2026-11-16 (other brands' long-runners):** fixed bodies re-hooked 3 ways run 132–306 days; footwear long-copy statics die fast when they read like ads; the median brand position is ~59% and footwear often names the brand late; "week one, nothing" reads false for a shoe you feel at once, so use a reframed first result; failed-fix refrain and silent-spouse loops are rising open-loop types; criteria-first bridge ("the one pair that passed") then a late reveal works.

## Format and what to return (every concept in this brief)
- Long-form static Meta ad: one phone photo + a first-person story as the primary text. 8,000–13,000 characters, grade 4–6 reading level, short lines. Measure it.
- Brand named 50–65% through (or a late reveal where the concept says so), handed over by a person from the narrator's world or a criteria check, with the price and price inversion. Every enemy appears with its price and failure mode as the narrator's failed fixes. Results with an honest first beat, each loss paid off, the opening image reversed.
- Close: the live Black Friday offer with its December 5th end date, the 30-day money-back guarantee, 500,000+, HSA/FSA, a CTA, then 3 P.S. lines (a self-test, a second pair or gift, a share). Then the two offer swaps (Christmas, New Year) as paste-ready close paragraphs. Put the swaps at the end of primary_text under a line "OFFER SWAPS".
- The hook and opening open loops matter most. 3 alternate hooks per concept (lines 1–2 only, so they sit on the same body), each a different hook type.
- headline (≤40 characters), cta ("Learn More").
- scroll_script: a 650–720 word version of the same story for a scrolling-text video (same hook, story beats and close; live offer only, no swaps). No symbols at all (no $, %, &, #, emojis, bullets), numbers as spoken ("one hundred sixty-five dollars", "sixty percent off", "twelve-hour shift"), paragraphs of 1–3 short sentences, at most 35 words each, blank line between paragraphs. Count the words.
- persona: the persona from avatar_personas.md the concept is written for.
- why: hook type, open-loop type, lead type, awareness level, one-belief sentence, measured character count of primary_text and word count of scroll_script.
