# Comfort Wear post-purchase survey: what our customers tell us

Source: Google Form "ComfortWear Customer Feedback (Responses)", 18,521 responses, 2023-04-15 to 2026-11-18. Aggregates and anonymous short quotes only (no names, emails or order numbers). Built 2026-11-18.

## Who buys (the avatar in numbers)
- **Age** (7,465 answered): 65+ 47%, 50-65 38%, 40-50 10%, 30-40 4%, 20-30 1%. 85% are 50+; nearly half are 65+. Last 6 months skew a bit younger (50-65 is the largest group, 40-50 rising to ~15%).
- **Gender**: not asked; the shoe is a women's shoe and every ad shows women.
- **Job** (18,031 answered): Retired 26%, Other / unclear 26%, Nurse / healthcare 19%, Retail / cashier 8%, Restaurant / hospitality 6%, Teacher / school 4%, Office / admin 4%, Homemaker 3%, Factory / warehouse / trades / driver 2%, Disabled / not working 1%. The two big buyer groups are retirees and people on their feet at work (healthcare first, then retail, restaurants, teachers).
- **Foot problem** (18,518 answered, multi-select): Plantar Fasciitis 25%, No foot pain (only need comfort) 22%, Arthritis 11%, Neuropathy 10%, Bunions 8%, Morton's Neuroma 1%, Other 1%, None 1%, Flat feet 1%.
  - 40-50: Plantar Fasciitis 36%, No foot pain (only need comfort) 30%, Arthritis 5%, Bunions 4%, Neuropathy 4%
  - 50-65: No foot pain (only need comfort) 28%, Plantar Fasciitis 26%, Neuropathy 10%, Arthritis 9%, Bunions 7%
  - 65+: No foot pain (only need comfort) 31%, Plantar Fasciitis 14%, Arthritis 13%, Neuropathy 13%, Bunions 8%
  Plantar fasciitis peaks at 40-65; arthritis and neuropathy rise with age. About a quarter have no pain and just want comfort.
- **Where they found us**: Facebook 63%, Google 18%, word of mouth 2%, Instagram 2%.
- **Usual size**: 9 10%, 8 10%, 8.5 8%, 10 8%, 7 6%, 7.5 6%, 9.5 5%, 11 4% (most are 7-10).

## Why they bought (#1 reason, themes; one answer can hit several)
- Comfort: 39%

- Work / on my feet all day: 17%
- Saw it in an ad, reviews, recommended: 12%
- Support / arch support: 9%
- Style / looks: 8%
- Walking / travel: 8%

## What they look for in a shoe
(15,740 answered) comfort 70%, support 20%, fit 8%, arch support 7%, style 7%, wide / room 5%, cushion 4%, easy on/off 3%, durability 2%, price 2%, light weight 1%, non-slip 1%. Comfort is almost universal; support second; style matters to a real minority (they don't want to look like they wear 'old lady' or medical shoes).

## What they used before (current solution for foot pain or comfort)
insoles 1149, orthotics 325, sketchers 325, hokas 298, massage 262, tylenol 167, stretching 161, barefoot 154, brooks 133, ice 117, compression 94, new balance 88, surgery 78, podiatrist 64, night splint 44, crocs 41, cortisone 15 (counts of answers naming each). Insoles/inserts and orthotics are the #1 old fix; Skechers and Hoka are the shoes they name most; many self-treat with massage, stretching, painkillers, ice.

## The expectation gap (design copy around this)
- Comfort rating 1-5: 1 = 46%, 2 = 17%, 3 = 15%, 4 = 8%, 5 = 14%. Stable every half-year since 2023. Low scorers really are disappointed (they mention problems ~3x as often as 5-star respondents); some answered before the shoes arrived.
- Size received: good fit 40%, too small/small 33%, too big/big 27%. Small-running is the biggest single complaint behind low scores.
- What disappointed them, in their words: shoes ran small or narrow despite 'wide' in the ad, didn't match the comfort they were promised, back or foot pain after wearing, hard returns, being asked for a review before delivery.
- Implication for ads: never promise 'extra wide', 'fits everyone' or 'instant relief'; say what the shoe does (stretch-knit upper that gives, cushioned heel, arch support) and let the 30-day money-back guarantee carry the risk. Mention sizing help ("between sizes? size up") only if the brand confirms it.
