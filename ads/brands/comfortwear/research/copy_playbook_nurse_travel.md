# Copy playbook: nurse + travel (2026-10-26)

Sources: our own winners (`winning_statics.csv`, `current_winners.csv`, `rising_winners.csv`, `video_transcripts.md`: Kyro 154/99/141/170/172/293/369/373, 203 UGC Emily RN, Spanish nurse script) and competitor/inspo ads pulled from Atria (`competitor_ads.csv`, `inspiration_raw.csv`, `brand_inspiration_longrunning.csv`: Orthora, "2026 Best Work Shoes", Dr. Rachel Moore, Carol Bennett, Nimbao, Vessi, Archies, Stepprs, Treatmedy).

**Claims filter (brand.json → claims_to_avoid):** winners also used "podiatrist/orthopedist recommended", "#1 recommended by podiatrists", "73% of healthcare workers", "120,000 / 500,000+ reviews", "45-day guarantee", "free returns", "relieves/heals plantar fasciitis". Borrow the STRUCTURE and voice, not those claims. Offer words only from approved_claims (the 30-day guarantee, 40% stack; Black Friday week = 60% OR buy-2-get-2-gifts).

## 1. What our winners say (nurse)

| Pattern | Evidence | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| **Condition + job in the first line, first person** | Emily RN ($4.2k-$100k, 2.9-3.05 ROAS): "I'm a nurse with plantar fasciitis. I've worn popular shoes which always ended up hurting my feet after 3 12s in a row." | Open with who + the pain: "I'm a nurse and my heels...". Name the shift unit ("3 12s in a row", "4 12s at the ER", "by hour 8"). |
| **Insider nurse vocabulary** | "12s", "the ER", "codes", "between patient rooms", "surgery", "limping to my car", "my floor", "charting" | Use the words nurses use, never "healthcare professionals". Quantify in shifts/hours, not in features. |
| **The tipping-point scene** | "No more limping to my car after a 12-hour shift"; Spanish script: "apenas pude llegar a mi coche"; "wait on the edge of the bed"; "stabbing heel pain" | The parking-lot walk / car / first steps are the emotional hook. Pain in body language, not medical terms. |
| **Peer proof** | "Three other nurses on my floor ordered them after seeing mine"; "designed by nurses for nurses" | "My coworkers asked", "the whole unit". Prefer peer voice over expert voice. |
| **Insider list of enemies** | Kyro 172: "Three things the healthcare industry doesn't want you to know" (running shoes, painkillers, $400 shoes); Kyro 141: insoles, massages tried | Name what they already tried (insoles, ibuprofen, running shoes, clogs) then reject it. |
| **Price anchor** | "$60... regret not buying sooner"; "Way cheaper than the $300 orthotics my podiatrist wanted"; "you don't need $400 shoes" | Contrast with orthotics/insoles/$400 shoes. Price in the headline only if on-offer. |
| **Feature list as a checklist** | Static winners ($858k / $457k / $229k at 4.34 ROAS): arch support, cushioning for heels, wide toe box, slip-resistant, free shipping with check marks | 3-5 short check lines, each = body part + benefit ("Cushioned heel"). |
| **Headline templates that scaled** | "2024's Favorite Nurse Shoes", "Trusted By Nurses", "Finally, No More Foot Pain!", "Save Up To 40% OFF", "48 HOURS LEFT - 40% OFF", "SELLING OUT - 4 DAYS LEFT!", "500,000+ Happy Customers" | Year/role label + authority; or urgency + the number. |
| **Spanish nurse** | $48k Spanish script: "Mi doctor me dijo que no había cura...", dagger-in-the-heel pain image, family income pressure, "mi esposo", friend recommends | Spanish is story-first, domestic stakes (family, income, husband), friend/comadre recommendation. |

## 2. What our winners say (travel)

| Pattern | Evidence | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ranked test vs named competitors** | Kyro 293 ($58k, 2.15 ROAS), 369 ($67k): "We tested five of the most popular ones" (Nike, Hoka, Skechers memory foam, sandals) | Name a mechanism per shoe (Nike = narrow toe box; Hoka = unstable on stone; Skechers = foam compresses by afternoon; sandals = no protection). Our shoe wins on firm sole + wide toe box + arch that holds at hour 9. |
| **Contrarian first line** | Kyro 373 ($61k): "The worst travel advice I ever got was just wear your comfortable sneakers." | Attack the common advice. |
| **Trip diary with places and consequences** | "Hokas in Lisbon... stabbing heel pain. Nikes in Paris... feet swollen by three. Two trips partially ruined." | Place + shoe + pain + cost ("spent two days on a bench"). Then "Three trips since, no pain, no benches". |
| **Numbers** | "20,000 steps a day", "50,000 steps in two days in Barcelona", "22,000 steps", "eight, nine, ten hours" | Always a step/hour number in the headline. |
| **Nurse-to-traveler bridge** | Kyro 154/99: nurse wears hospital shoes to Europe; "if they hold up through 12-hour shifts they hold up through anything" | Mash-up: nurse proof powers the travel claim. |
| **Disney / plus-size** | TOFV1_KW1 (5.65 ROAS): "A Disney day can mean miles of walking, hours standing in lines... Your regular 'comfortable sneakers' might feel fine at breakfast but hour 8 can be a very different story." | "Fine at breakfast, different story at hour 8"; lines/queues; wide toe box. |
| **Travel static copy** | "2026's Favorite Shoes For Traveling": "Travel smart. Travel comfortably." Bullets: all-day comfort, arch support, breathable and lightweight "from take-off to landing" | Airport, flight, "from take-off to landing", lightweight in the suitcase. |
| **Outcome line** | "Nice enough for restaurants, comfortable enough for eight hours of exploring"; "not the person sitting on a bench" | The FOMO payoff: seeing everything you flew there for. |

## 4. Rules for the next nurse + travel concepts

1. **Line 1 = who + pain in their words**, with a number: "3 12s in a row", "hour 10", "20,000 steps", "400 stone steps", "90-minute wait". Max ~8 words on a static headline.
2. **Nurse words**: 12s, shift, unit/floor, rounds, handoff, charting, codes, break room, night shift, the walk to my car, scrubs, CNA, travel nurse. **Travel words**: cobblestones, layover, terminal, take-off to landing, carry-on, sightseeing, queue/line, museum, castle steps, Disney/park day, benches.
3. **Name what they already tried** (insoles, ibuprofen, Hokas, running shoes, "comfortable sneakers") and fail it with a mechanism (foam collapses by hour 4-6, narrow toe box, unstable on stone).
4. **Scene over feature**: one concrete moment (limping to the car, sitting on a bench in Paris, standing in a 90-minute line). Then ONE proof line: peer ("three nurses on my floor"), step count, or a customer quote.
5. **Contrast pairs**: "Fine at breakfast, different story at hour 8", "Hour 1 / Hour 6 / Hour 12", "Lisbon in Hokas / Paris in Nikes".
6. **Wide toe box + room to swell + arch that holds** are the repeated product proofs; "cushioning that lasts 12 hours vs 2 for running shoes" is the winning comparison (state as comparison without numbers we cannot back; no medical claims).
7. **Offers**: static offer lines stay short ("40% OFF", etc.). BF week: 60% sitewide OR buy 2 pairs, get 2 free gifts (3-pack cushioned socks, free travel bag), never both.
8. **Spanish twin**: keep the same scene and structure, rewrite the voice: tú, family/income stakes ("mi familia depende de mi ingreso"), comadre/coworker recommendation, "turno de 12 horas", "me duelen los talones". Never literal translation.
9. **Headline bank (adapt, don't copy)**: "Hour 6 is when it starts." / "Fine at breakfast. Hour 8 is the real test." / "The walk to my car used to take 5 minutes." / "Three nurses on my floor asked." / "Lisbon in Hokas. Paris in Nikes. Then these." / "20,000 steps before lunch." / "Not the one on the bench."

## 5. Pattern review v2 (2026-10-26): what long-running winners and competitors have in common

Read from the 40 longest-running shoe/foot statics in Atria (`data/longrun_statics.csv`, sheets `longrun_statics_*.jpg`; 160-570 days live) plus our own winners.

1. **Features and benefits are always on the image.** Our winners (Dear Nurses callouts, 12-hour shift list, diagram, "Flash sale" with toe-box/arch/cushioning icons) and competitors (Clove pills, FitVille, Hike, "Built for hard concrete floors" bullet list: 12-hour comfort, arch support for standing all day, cushioning that doesn't collapse mid-shift, slip-resistant outsole, wide toe box) all list 3-5 short body-part benefits. Our v1 tests dropped them for scenes and wordplay.
2. **The real-looking ads run longest.** Stepprs (401 days): insoles on a wooden floor with handwritten sticky notes; a cardboard box with a handwritten "Buy 4 Get 4 Free" sign; a hand holding a sock with a story Q&A sticker; a woman's selfie at her kitchen counter; bare feet close-ups; a hand holding a candy bar. Phone-shot, uneven light, ordinary rooms, real older hands and feet. Glossy composites (our v1: studio-lit scenes, castle cartoons, perfect lighting) read as ads.
3. **Offers are quiet, small and often handwritten.** Stepprs: a sticky note "Now up to 60% off, ends in 48 hrs, 90 day money back guarantee". Our own long-runners: "$59.95 / 12-hour shift shoes / today only" as a small top line, "40% OFF + FREE SHIPPING" as small text under the shoe, one underlined line at the bottom of the comments ad. WearStand runs no discount at all, just a stock stamp. The big full-width offer block belongs to short-run promo templates.
4. **Copy is 3-7 plain words and says the thing.** "Dear Nurses, work 12-hour shifts pain-free." "Built for hard concrete floors." "Say goodbye to plantar fasciitis." "Neuropathy relief in 14 days." "End bunion pain in 30 days or your money back." "You'll forget us." "Foot Relief." "Selling out in 4 days." No wordplay, no props-as-jokes.
5. **One clear product shot, nothing else competing**: a single hero shoe/pair, labeled, on a plain or real surface.

### v2 rules (also in `brand.json` → `program.creative_rules_v2` and `quality_bar_ugc`)
- `"style": "ugc"` on new concepts: generate.py swaps the polished-photography rules for a "real phone photo" rule and attaches the benchmark only as a loose size/weight reference.
- Every concept: 3-4 feature/benefit labels (arch support, cushioned heel, wide toe box, slip-resistant grip, stretch-knit upper, firm sole), hand-drawn arrows or sticky notes.
- Offer: one small line/sticky/sticker in a corner; Black Friday = one small tag, either 60% sitewide or buy 2 get 2 gifts.
- Headline: <= 7 words, problem or benefit, plain words ("Heel pain after 12-hour shifts?", "Cobblestones hurt. These don't.", "Swollen feet after flying?").
- Banned headline devices: permission slip, admit one, be the one who, lesson plan, hour-8-is-the-test style puns.
