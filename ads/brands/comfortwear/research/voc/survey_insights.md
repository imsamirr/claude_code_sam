# ComfortWear post-purchase survey — insights for scriptwriting

Source: "ComfortWear Customer Feedback (Responses)" Google Form, 18,521 responses, Apr 15 2023 → Nov 18 2026.
Aggregated only (no emails / order numbers). Raw export kept out of the repo.

## ⚠️ Satisfaction reality check (affects which claims are believable)
> **Selection bias:** per `research/analysis.md` §9, this form is mostly routed to customers who gave a low rating, so it over-represents unhappy buyers (Trustpilot is 52% 5★, 21% 1★). Read the scores as "why people end up unhappy", not overall satisfaction. The 4–5★ answers (~4,000) are still a large pool of positive language.

- Comfort rating: **1/5 = 45.6%**, 2 = 17.2%, 3 = 15.2%, 4 = 7.8%, **5 = 14.2%** → only ~22% rate 4–5.
- 2023 was worst (8% 4–5); 2024–2026 steady at ~21–25%.
- Best-scoring segments: **CNA/caregiver (31.6% 4–5)**, **housekeeping (31%)**, bunions (27%), arthritis (26%), neuropathy (25%). Worst: retail/cashier (19%), restaurant (20%), nurse (20%).
- Many 1★ answers are about **shipping delays, wrong size, and refund/return friction** rather than the shoe itself, plus: "insole flat / not enough cushion in the forefoot", "foot slides / unstable", "heel collar folds in, hard to put on", "felt like heels / pitching forward" (rocker sole), "looks cheap".
- Implication for scripts: keep claims at "comfort / support / all-day wear" level; avoid over-promising "fix your PF"; never promise easy returns, refunds or fast shipping (already in brand rules). Setting expectations ("roomy toe box", "slip in", "stretch for swelling") fits what happy buyers actually liked.

## Why they bought (#1 reason themes, n=18,032)
- Comfort (plain) — the single most common answer
- **Saw the ad / video / read the comments & reviews: 9.1%** ("I saw the advertisement online & read all the comments", "everybody in the commercials said the shoes are great") → social proof and comment sections sell
- Walking / travel / trip: 8.1%
- On my feet / standing / concrete: 7.5%
- Pain at end of day / long shifts / 12 hours: 6.7%
- Knee / back / hip pain: 3.3%
- Wide toe box / width: 2.6%
- Price / sale: 2.1%; slip-on / easy to put on: 1.8%; looks/style: 1.5%

**Desire language**
- "Room for my toes so they don't get squashed"
- "Wear them all day without pain in my feet"
- "Walk around all day without having foot pain at the end of the day"
- "Easy to get on and off" / "not needing a shoehorn" / "in my profession I don't have 10 minutes to put on a pair of sneakers"
- "Comfortable or cute" → "nice looking and comfortable pair of shoes for travel"
- "To keep my feet in good condition and to have comfort for my entire body while I'm walking"
- Spanish-speaking buyers present: "En el anuncio parecían muy cómodos… antiderrapante" (banquet server)

**Gift buyers exist**: "My wife is a nurse and works 12 plus hours, I just wanted her to be comfortable for her whole shift." / "My daughter is a nurse… for a Christmas gift" / "My niece needed orthopedic shoes and wouldn't buy them, so I ordered these for her and a pair for myself."

## Objections to pre-empt in scripts (from unhappy answers)
1. "Is this a Facebook scam / Temu-quality?" → show real wear, real women, construction close-ups
2. Sizing ("ordered my size, too small/too big", "wide isn't wide enough") → sizing guidance in CTA/VO where allowed
3. Hard to get on (soft heel collar folds in) → demonstrate the pull tab / bungee slip-on correctly
4. "Not enough forefoot cushion / insole flattens" → don't overclaim cushioning
5. "Rocker sole felt like heels / pitching forward" → frame rocker as "rolls you forward so your heel isn't taking every step" and show walking naturally
6. "Ugly / looks like old-lady shoes" → color variety, style shots
