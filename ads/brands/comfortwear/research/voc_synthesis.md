# Comfort Wear voice of customer: synthesis (2026-11-18)

Sources (details, counts and 250+ tagged verbatim quotes in `research/voc/`):
- **Trustpilot** (`voc/trustpilot.md`): our page 3.7/5 on 3,151 reviews (52% 5★, 21% 1★); 500 reviews read (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026) + competitor pages.
- **Facebook / Instagram ad comments** (`voc/facebook_comments.md`): ~795 comments on our 31 highest-spend ads.
- **Reddit** (`voc/reddit.md`): 65 threads, ~2,200 comments (nursing, PlantarFasciitis, Disney, AskWomenOver40, PlusSize, bunions, neuropathy…). Nobody mentions Comfort Wear by name.
- **Amazon** (`voc/amazon.md`): 21 competitor products (Hoka, Skechers, Brooks, Orthofeet, Vionic, OOFOS, NB, Crocs, knit look-alikes incl. Orthora).
- **YouTube** (`voc/youtube.md`): 1,487 comments from 38 videos (PF doctors, Hoka for nurses, Skechers for seniors, Kuru, Orthofeet, Hike, travel, Disney, wide feet).
- Plus the post-purchase survey (`customer_survey_digest.md`, 18,521 answers) and the avatar (`avatar_personas.md`).

## 1. Objections, ranked across sources

1. **"Will I get my money back?" (trust / returns).** The #1 objection everywhere. Trustpilot: ~20% of reviews say the guarantee wasn't honored (partial 20–50% refunds, gift cards, "donate to Salvation Army and send the receipt", then nothing). Ad comments: ~55 refund complaints, cited by readers as their reason not to buy; ads disagree on 30 vs 45 days. Reddit: ~60 comments describe the identical trap at Hike and Orthora ("keep the shoes for 30% back", no return address, AI chatbot, chargebacks) and call the category "marked-up Alibaba shoes"; one bad brand burns trust for all ("I don't even want to try another company"). Older buyers blame themselves ("I'm old enough to know better").
2. **"Does it actually support, or is it a flimsy knit shoe?"** Amazon: knit look-alikes are returned for no support, not looks (Orthora 2.6★, 47% 1★: "Not for 2 hour shift work let alone 12 hrs"); fury when "orthopedic" feels flat. Ad comments ~55 "no support / made my PF worse" (mostly Instagram). Trustpilot: "no support", heel lift pushing toes forward, chemical smell, cheap feel.
3. **Sizing and width.** Survey: only 40% got a good fit (33% too small). Trustpilot 18% inconsistent sizing pair to pair. Amazon: #1 return reason across all 21 products (EU vs US confusion, "size 38, what is that?"). "Wide" isn't wide enough for plus-size / 60+ / 2E–4E feet (ad comments ~38, Reddit, YouTube).
4. **Is it a scam / fake reviews / AI ads / dropshipped from China?** Ad comments ~38; Reddit ("$11 on Temu"); Trustpilot (China shipping 7%, "$20 on AliExpress").
5. **Hard to put on.** The soft knit collar collapses, needs a shoe horn (Trustpilot 34 reviews, 21 of them positive overall; Amazon "the top is like a sock"; ad comments ~14). Breaks the easy-on promise that 60+ buyers value most. Reddit mocks false "hands-free" claims.
6. **Fit for MY job / dress code.** Teachers can't wear sneakers (~46 ad comments; "make the white sole black"); kitchen/food workers test slip-resistance hard (Amazon STQ 688 negative slip mentions); ER nurses: "I would never wear a mesh top shoe" (fluids); some need steel toe, waterproof (~25).
7. **Stability over squish.** YouTube's top comment (52 likes) is about seniors falling in high-stack soft shoes; Reddit: Hoka is both the most loved and the most blamed ("gave me PF / made me trip"); for PF "too squishy" and "too much arch" are top quit reasons.
8. **Durability.** Cheap knit dies in 1–3 months (soles split, knit stretches out "by the end of day 2 very loose"); premium foam (Hoka/Brooks) packs out in ~6 months, especially for heavier wearers; nurses rotate pairs.
9. **Price / checkout surprises.** "$59 then over $100 at checkout" (~19 ad comments); $60 sits between $12–34 dupes and $108–200 premium shoes.
10. **"Only for women?" / men's** (~31 ad comments); "will it help my PF / neuropathy / back / bunions?" is the #1 question (~50).

## 2. Why they buy competitors (and what they love)

- **Hoka** (Bondi, Clifton): cushion ("9/10 to 2/10 heel pain"), the default nurse shoe; but $150–175, packs out, feels unstable, ugly to some ("feels like Hokas but looks like anything else?").
- **Skechers** (Slip-ins, Arch Fit, Go Walk): easy on ("the official shoe of retirement", independence after strokes/arthritis), cheap, in stores; the fallback for unhappy ad-brand buyers; weak support for PF.
- **Brooks / New Balance / Asics**: trusted support, wide widths that are truly wide (NB 2E/4E); expensive, sporty look.
- **Orthofeet (Coral ~$70, 4.4★ on 39k TP reviews)**: closest comp: stretch knit + orthotic insole + wide toe box; easy returns; weak spots: squeaks, tight toe box despite bunion claims, laces seniors can't tie.
- **Kuru (4.8★)**: heel cup feel, 90-day wear test, truly wide; $150+.
- **Vionic / OOFOS / Crocs On-The-Clock**: arch support in casual looks / recovery softness / wipe-clean slip-resistant work shoe.
- **Hike / Stepprs** (ad brands): same buyer as us, same 25% 1★ share, but they reply to almost every negative review and invite reviews (3.5–3.8★).

## 3. Features they value (in order of mentions)

1. Real arch support you can feel (not flat "orthopedic"). 2. Cushion that is stable, not squishy. 3. Room: wide toe box that's genuinely roomy + room for swelling. 4. Easy on/off without bending (firm heel or pull tab). 5. Light weight. 6. Slip resistance that works on wet/greasy floors. 7. Looks normal, not "old lady"/"medical"/"diabetic" shoes; black-sole options for dress codes. 8. No break-in. 9. Breathable but wipeable / not mesh for fluids. 10. Durable for daily shifts. 11. Half sizes and true widths.

## 4. Benefits they report (their words map to outcomes)

Getting through a 10–14 hour shift without limping; no heel pain on the first steps; not sitting on the bench at Disney / keeping up with family; walking to the store again; knee, hip and back relief; no bending to tie laces; not looking like they wear medical shoes; feeling steady and not falling; buying every color once one fits.

## 5. Persona notes (add to `avatar_personas.md`)

- **Nurses / CNAs:** rotate pairs, add insoles to Hokas, wear compression socks, need fluid-safe uppers, no break-in, slip-resistant; trial windows persuade them.
- **Teachers:** dress code is the blocker (black, "not sneakers"); the "teachers stand still" hook gets mocked.
- **Food service / kitchen:** slip resistance proven on greasy floors or nothing.
- **Retail / warehouse / 65+ still working:** "what about us?" in ad comments; concrete floors; Spanish-language kitchen and factory workers are very active in comments.
- **60+ retirees:** stability and easy-on beat softness; fear of falling; feet widen and swell with age; easiest to burn with returns.
- **Plus-size:** "wide" is never wide enough; heavier wearers pack out foam fast; Amazon had no evidence on body size (use survey/Reddit).
- **Travelers (Disney, Europe):** cobblestones demand stability; want no break-in and a put-together look; name their step counts and trips.
- **Bunions:** a stated market gap: wide toe box shoes are thin "barefoot" styles with no arch support; they want both.

## 6. What this means for copy (brief StefanBrain with these)

- **Lead objection to answer: trust.** State the 30-day money-back guarantee plainly and consistently (always 30, never 45), make it feel real (simple, full refund), and never promise more than ops delivers. Social proof from real buyers who wore them on shifts.
- **Prove support, don't just claim "orthopedic":** show the arch, heel cup, firm stable sole; contrast with flimsy knit and squishy foam ("not a sock with a sole").
- **Stability for 60+**: steady, not wobbly-soft; a fall-prevention-adjacent angle without medical claims.
- **Sizing guidance** (once the brand confirms the facts): "between sizes? size up".
- **Don't promise**: extra wide, fits everyone, hands-free, instant relief, waterproof, steel toe.
- **Angles with demand and little competition**: bunions + arch support together; 65+ still working; teachers with a black-sole / dress-code-friendly colorway; food-service grip; "comfort that doesn't look orthopedic".

## 7. Business issues found (not copy; for the team)

1. **Refund practices drive the bad reviews** (Trustpilot ~20%, ad comments ~55, mirrored by Hike/Orthora complaints on Reddit). Ads promise "free returns" and 30 or 45 days. Fixing the policy or the promise would lift the 3.7★ and cut the top objection.
2. **Comment moderation:** Instagram comments on retargeting ads RTG B6/B3 are ~75–80% negative with no replies; Facebook looks moderated. Reply to negatives (Hike/Stepprs do and hold 3.5–3.8★).
3. **A reply page named "Asociación Nacional de Enfermeras Hispanas"** answers comments on our Spanish ads; it reads like a real nurses' association (impersonation/compliance risk).
4. **Product:** the collapsing knit collar (hard to put on) and pair-to-pair sizing variation are the top fixable product complaints, even from fans.
5. **Trustpilot:** the company doesn't reply to negative reviews or invite reviews; invites + replies would move the score.
