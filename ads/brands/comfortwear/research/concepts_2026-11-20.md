What this shows:
1. Stacks win when the collision is a **specific, dated, physical ordeal** (20,000 Disney steps while
   heavier and swelling) and an **expert explains why the obvious shoe fails**.
2. Stacks lose when the message match breaks (cruise ads ran Disney copy and pointed at the summer
   page), or when the format entertains but doesn't sell (drama skits had great hook and hold rates but
   no purchases).
3. Your biggest spender, HF7, is the least stacked ad. It's the clearest case for replacing it with a
   stacked version.

## Feedback on the two concepts you're testing

### Plus-size × NYC travel: good stack, make it NYC *at Christmas*

- **Why it should work:** it reuses your best persona (plus-size, $30.5K) and the travel/Disney
  skeleton. Christmas gives it a date and stakes: the Rockefeller tree, the 5th Ave window lines,
  subway stairs, 20–25K steps, standing in the cold.
- **Risk:** NYC is a smaller, lower-stakes trip than Disney unless the ad makes it *the* trip.
- **Mechanism:** subway stairs and cold-to-warm swelling. The stretch knit gives, and the built-in
  arch has nothing to flatten by hour 6.
- **Hooks:**
  - "If you're over 200 lbs and doing NYC for Christmas, those subway stairs are the part nobody warns you about."
  - "Hour 6 in Midtown: the line for the windows hasn't moved and your shoes feel a size smaller."
- **Must have:**
  - an NYC/holiday landing page, not the summer travel page
  - ad text and headline that match ("2026's Favorite Shoes For NYC at Christmas")
  - the expert in the first 3s over real NYC footage
- **Kill signal:** ROAS under 1.2 after $750.

### Church women × foot pain: the weakest collision, so add a dimension

- **Problem 1, the stakes are low:** a 1–2 hour service once a week is mild next to 12-hour shifts or
  20K steps.
- **Problem 2, there's an objection the ad must face:** "it's a knit sneaker, can I wear it to church?"
- **Make it a bigger swing** with one of these stacks:
  - **Church volunteers who stand all Sunday** (ushers, greeters, choir, kitchen ministry) × 60+ ×
    **Christmas Eve services**: long, standing, several services. Hook: *"Ushers stand through 3
    services on Christmas Eve. Here's what that does to a 65-year-old arch."*
  - **Sunday-to-Monday** × plus-size or nurse: one all-black pair that passes for church on Sunday and
    survives a 12 on Monday. Show black-on-black styled with a dress.
- **Kill signal:** ROAS under 1.2 after $500. If it's soft, fold "church" in as a b-roll scene inside
  the 60+ concepts rather than running it as a standalone.

## New concepts, ranked

Every concept uses the winning format:
- the expert composited over real occasion footage in the first 3s
- a competitor teardown with prices, and why each one fails
- hour-by-hour pain, then a payoff shot
- the product shown by about 0:40
- a proof block (customer quotes and star cards)
- the offer

**Compliance, from `brand.json`:** don't claim "podiatrist/doctor recommended", money-back
guarantees, fast shipping or unconfirmed review counts. The expert *explains*; they don't endorse.

### Tier 1: launch within 7 days (holiday timing)

#### C1. Plus-size × flying home for the holidays
- **Collision:** 2 miles of terminal, standing in the TSA line, 3+ hours seated → feet swell, and in a
  cramped seat you can't bend to loosen laces.
- **Awareness and angle:** problem-aware, **fear**.
- **Mechanism, "the TSA-to-baggage-claim fit":** the stretch-knit upper gives as feet swell, and the
  bungee laces and pull tab mean no bending or tying in the seat or at security.
- **Hooks:**
  - Fear: "If you're over 200 lbs and flying home for Christmas, your sneakers will feel a size smaller by baggage claim."
  - Curiosity: "Why the shoes that fit at TSA don't fit at your gate."
  - Contrarian (sensory): "Stop wearing running shoes to the airport. That squeeze across your toes at 30,000 feet is the foam and the mesh, not your feet."
- **B-roll:**
  - plus-size women walking long terminals, in the TSA line, sitting in a cramped seat
  - pressing on a puffy ankle
  - bending awkwardly to untie laces
  - payoff: hugging family at arrivals
- **Proof:** the expert explaining swelling (authority), customer quotes about extra-wide feet and
  toe room (testimonial).
- **Objection:** "Sneakers are fine for travel" → the size-smaller-by-landing demonstration.
- **Message match:** holiday-travel landing page; headline "2026's Favorite Shoes For Holiday Flights".
- **Evidence:** the plus-size bucket ($30.5K) and the swelling mechanism already used in NH3 and KW1.
  The travel occasion itself is untested.
- **RMBC score:** about 80 (specific 21 / mechanism 20 / proof 19 / CTA 20).

#### C2. Nurse × working Christmas × plantar fasciitis
- **Collision:** a short-staffed holiday 12 means more steps, no breaks and heel pain — and still
  standing at home to open presents.
- **Awareness and angle:** problem-aware, **identity** (the nurse who gives up the holiday).
- **Mechanism, "the hour-4 foam collapse":** running-shoe foam flattens by hour 4. ComfortWear's arch
  is built into the shoe, so there's nothing to pack down by hour 12.
- **Hooks:**
  - Identity: "To every nurse working Christmas Day this year."
  - Fear (sensory): "If your first step after a 12 feels like a nail in your heel, it's the foam, not your age."
  - Social proof: "Hour 1, hour 4, hour 12: what happens inside the 3 shoes nurses wear most."
- **B-roll:** decorated nurses' station, an empty hallway at 3 a.m., a hand pressing a flattened Hoka,
  a nurse walking in the front door at 8 p.m. to kids and a tree.
- **Proof:** customer language ("great for my 12 hour shifts"), an hour-by-hour demonstration.
- **Objection:** "I already have Hokas" → the comparison at hour 12, with the $165 vs $59.95 math.
- **Message match:** the Nurse page, with a holiday headline.
- **Evidence:** the Nurse tag at 2.54, Kyro390 at 1.84, the databank angle "12-Hour Countdown".
- **RMBC score:** about 82.

#### C3. Daughter buying for mom (65+) × arthritis hands × Christmas gift
- **Collision:** Mom can't tie her laces anymore and is unsteady when she bends. The gift is
  independence, not shoes.
- **Awareness and angle:** problem-aware, **desire / identity of the giver**.
- **Mechanism, "the no-bend slip-on":** bungee laces and a pull tab mean slipping them on standing up,
  with no tying and no bending. Add the wide toe box for bunions and the grip outsole.
- **Hooks:**
  - Desire: "The Christmas gift my mom actually uses every single day."
  - Fear (sensory): "She stopped tying her shoes. Then she stopped going on walks."
  - Curiosity: "Why podiatrists hate the velcro shoes we buy our parents." Keep this as an explanation,
    not an endorsement.
- **B-roll:**
  - mom's swollen knuckles struggling with laces
  - a daughter wrapping the box
  - Christmas morning: mom slipping them on while standing, then out walking with the grandkids
- **Proof:** a 78-year-old's quote ("instant balance and stability"), a demonstration.
- **Objection:** "Mom won't wear ugly orthopedic shoes" → show the grey/pink and purple colorways.
- **Message match:** a gift landing page, a Christmas offer, a gift-receipt mention (if true).
- **Evidence:** untested as a stack. Survey: 47% of respondents are 65+ and 11% have arthritis. The
  `gift_for_her` persona is untested in video. **Big swing.**
- **RMBC score:** about 78.

#### C4. Women 60+ × European Christmas markets × cobblestones and cold (replaces HF7)
- **Collision:** 4 hours standing on wet cobblestones in the cold, mulled wine in one hand, nothing to
  sit on.
- **Awareness and angle:** solution-aware, **comparison**. Same teardown as HF7, with an expert and a
  60+ stack added.
- **Mechanism, "the cobblestone roll":** a thick-foam shoe rolls side to side on stones. The rocker

- **Hooks:**
  - Comparison: "We tested 4 shoes at the Christmas markets in Vienna, and only one made it to hour 4."
  - Fear: "If you're over 60, wet cobblestones are not where you want to find out your shoes have no grip."
  - Desire (sensory): "Glühwein in one hand, warm dry feet, and no bench in sight. Here's the shoe."
- **B-roll:** real market footage (lights, crowds, cobblestones), a woman over 60 walking confidently,
  a close-up of the wet stones, the expert in the corner.
- **Proof:** the expert teardown, a real customer travel quote.
- **Message match:** a Christmas-markets or winter-Europe landing page. HF7 currently has no landing

- **Evidence:** HF7 is the top spender ($20.5K) at only 1.30 without an expert. Adding the expert and
