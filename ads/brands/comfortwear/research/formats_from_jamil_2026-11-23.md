# Formats and angles from Jamil Rashid's "9-figure creative system" video, 2026-11-23

Source: [x.com/jamilrashidd/status/2124537956710949194](https://x.com/jamilrashidd/status/2124537956710949194)
(18:50, a Loom walkthrough of a Miro board, posted 2026-11-22). I transcribed it locally and pulled
frames to read the board and the example ads.

## What he says, in short

1. **Hero unique mechanism.** Pick one mechanism and scale it hard. Don't keep testing new ones.
   - One mechanism should unlock 10–20 angles, each a new pocket of buyers.
   - You can **stack several angles in one ad**, as long as each traces back to the mechanism.
   - The mechanism needs proof or a logical chain the customer can follow, not "Claude slop".
   - From his board: *"Random symptom stacks → link back to mechanism."*
2. **Format fatigue.** A format works, everyone copies it, results fade, and you find the next one.
   - The cycle he describes: cartoon skeletons and doctors → Pixar-style song ads → AI avatars →
     long emotional Pixar story ads → **drama ads (now)**.
   - **Women watch drama and Pixar mini-movies more than men.**
   - A format that looks "late" in supplements can still be untested in another niche.
   - Keep a running list of working formats.
3. **Ads must look and feel different from each other.** His board says *"squint your eyes"*: if two ads
   look the same when you squint, they're not different tests. Spread creators across TOF, MOF and BOF.
4. **Research.**
   - Ad spy tools (he prefers TrendTrack over Atria and Foreplay).
   - **Organic TikTok and Reels.** He says several of his ads spent $500K+ on concepts taken from organic
     videos (example: an *"I'm Denise's husband"* drama, and the visual hook *"man gonna pop"*).
5. **Process:**
   - a lead strategist with equity or a profit share
   - strategist + editor pods, with a lead editor who keeps up with AI models
   - an ops manager
   - a ClickUp pipeline
   - daily updates from strategists and editors

   "Systems don't make winners, good creative does, but systems let you make more of it."

### The example ads he shows (Kaori, a persimmon soap aimed at women 60+)
Each one is a format you can borrow:
- *"GRADING PERSIMMON SOAPS (FOR MY MOM)"* (3:40)
- *"DON'T BUY PERSIMMON SOAP. And I have the evidence that a lot of it isn't real…"* (1:56)
- *"3 reasons I regret buying [my] persimmon soap"*
- *"Here are the 4 types of persimmon…"*
- *"I decided to buy it and not tell my [husband]. Now he's the one asking questions"*
- *"My sister is three years older than me and men still turn around when she walks into a room…"* (6:52)
- An AI Pixar-style grandma: *"OLD LADY SMELL, HERE'S…"*
- These run from **creator-style pages** ("Fabulous After 60", "Linda Doxey"), not the brand page.

## What this means for ComfortWear

### 1. Name one hero mechanism and tie every concept back to it
Your winners already lean on one idea without naming it.
- **Problem:** *foam flattens by hour 4* (in COM320, COM284, NH3 and Kyro390).
- **Fix:** *the arch is built into the shoe, so there's nothing to pack down.*

The other features (stretch knit, wide toe box, no-tie laces, rocker sole) work as **supporting
proof**, not separate mechanisms. Proposed name: **"The Hour-4 Foam Collapse"** (problem) →
**"Built-In Arch"** (fix).

Every concept in C1–C10, N1–N8 and F1–F9 should land on it, including stacked angles:
- **Holiday flight:** foam collapse + swelling.
- **Plus-size:** foam collapses faster under more weight.
- **Retail:** standing still crushes foam that was made to rebound.
- **Nurse:** hour 4 of a 12.

### 2. Drama is the hot format, and your drama ads failed: fix them, don't drop them
Your nurse dramas got good hook and hold rates but few sales:
- COM296 "Nurse wants to quit": 0.83 ROAS
- COM362 Spanish: 0.92
- COM338 "Husband tired of nurse wife…": 1.34

Jamil says drama is what's working right now, and mostly with women. The likely reason yours didn't
sell: **the drama never handed off to the mechanism or the teardown.**
- Test **drama in the first 10–15s**, then a hard pivot to the podiatrist teardown and the Hour-4
  Foam Collapse.
- Keep the story, and put the proof right after it.

### 3. The "squint test" on your account
COM320, COM284, KW1-style ads, NH3 and COM374 all look alike: a doctor in the corner over location
b-roll. That's why they fatigue together. New tests should look different at a glance: a different
creator, setting, format and color.

## New format concepts (each one is still a stack)

**Compliance applies to all of them:**
- no "podiatrist/doctor recommended"
- no money-back guarantee
- no unconfirmed review counts
- no fall-prevention or treatment claims
- creators must be real people. Run them as Meta partnership ads from the creator's own account. Don't
  invent persona pages or fake identities.

### F1. "Grading nurse shoes (for my mom)" (from Kaori's "Grading… for my mom")
- **Stack:** daughter (gift-giver) × nurse mom of 25+ years × Christmas.
- **Format:** the daughter grades 5 shoes A–F on camera: Hoka, Skechers, Crocs, Clove, ComfortWear.
  For each one, mom's quick reaction ("that one dies at hour 4").
- **Why:** the rating format already works in your account (HI16 "rates shoes" at 1.64). "For my mom"
  adds the gift and caregiver angle and feels like UGC, not a doctor.
- **Hooks:**
  - "Grading every shoe my mom has worn in 30 years of nursing."
  - "My mom's been a nurse since 1994. I made her rate her shoes."
- **Hero tie-in:** every F grade is "the foam was flat by lunch".

### F2. "Don't buy orthopedic sneakers until you do these 3 tests" (from "DON'T BUY… I have the evidence")
- **Stack:** women 60+ or plus-size × skepticism ("not really orthopedic" is in your 1★ reviews) ×
  demonstration.
- **Format:** hands-on tests on camera: the press test (does the arch flatten?), the heel-cup squeeze,
  and the foam-after-a-shift press. The competitors fail; ComfortWear passes.
- **Hooks:**
  - "Don't buy 'orthopedic' sneakers online until you do these 3 tests."
  - "Most 'arch support' shoes have no arch. Here's how to check in 10 seconds."
- **Must do:** check on camera that the shoe really passes every test before scripting. Never claim
  something you can't show.
- **Why:** it confronts your #1 trust problem directly and turns it into proof.

### F3. "3 reasons I regret buying ComfortWear" (reverse psychology)
- **Stack:** plus-size Disney or nurse × reverse hook.
- **Format:**
  1. "I wasted $400 on Hokas first."
  2. "Now everyone on my unit keeps asking where I got them."
  3. "I should've bought two colors."
- **Evidence:** your old tracker logged "Don't buy this" at a 47% hook rate. The format is proven in
  Kaori's 60+ niche.
- **Hook:** "3 reasons I regret buying these shoes before Disney."

### F4. "I bought them without telling my husband…" (drama → teardown hybrid)
- **Stack:** nurse or plus-size × marriage drama × the Hour-4 Foam Collapse.
- **Format:**
  - 0–12s: the drama. "I bought these and didn't tell my husband. Now he's the one asking questions:
    why I'm not limping to the car, why I took the stairs."
  - 12s onward: the podiatrist teardown.
- **Why:** it uses the format that's hot with women, with the mechanism hand-off your dramas lacked.
- **Variants:** "I'm [Name]'s husband…" (the organic example he shows), and a husband giving her the
  pair for Christmas.

### F5. The symptom-stack ad (his "random symptom stacks → link back to mechanism")
- **Stack:** women 50+ × several symptoms × one cause.
- **Hooks:**
  - "Heel pain at 6 a.m. Knees at 6 p.m. Lower back on Sunday. It might be one thing: your shoes."
  - "If your feet, knees and back all hurt after work, start at the bottom."
- **Format:** a podiatrist on body-map graphics. Each symptom lights up and traces back to a flattened
  foam sole.
- **Compliance:** say "can contribute to" and "comfort". No diagnosis or treatment claims.

### F6. Pixar-style mini-movie: "The shoes that sat on the bench" (emotional AI story)
- **Stack:** grandma 60+ × Disney with the grandkids × Christmas.
- **Format:** a 2–4 minute AI-animated story. Grandma saves all year for the trip, her feet give out on
  day 1, and she watches from the bench holding the bags. Next trip: new shoes, she rides with the
  grandkids at the fireworks.
- **Why:** Jamil says long Pixar stories are fatiguing in supplements but are untested in comfort
  footwear. Your avatar (women 55+) is the audience that watches them. You already have NH3's "bench
  with the bags" pain point.
- **Risk:** it costs more to produce. Test one.

### F7. Sister vs sister: "We both work 12s. Only one of us still limps to the car."
- **Stack:** nurse sisters (or teacher/nurse) × split-screen comparison × the Hour-4 Foam Collapse.
- **Inspired by:** *"My sister is three years older than me…"* (a 6:52 story ad).
- **Format:** split-screen through one shift, timestamped hour 1, 4, 8 and 12. Same job, different
  shoes.
- **Hook:** "My sister and I have the same job, the same feet and the same 12-hour shifts. Only one of
  us limps to the car."

### F8. "The 4 kinds of heel pain on a 12-hour shift" (from "Here are the 4 types of…")
- **Stack:** nurse or retail × plantar fasciitis × education listicle.
- **Format:**
  - The 4 types: first-step heel pain, hour-4 arch ache, end-of-shift burning forefoot, swelling.
  - Which one do you have? Three of the four trace back to foam collapse.
- **Hook:** "There are 4 kinds of foot pain on a 12-hour shift. Which one do you have?"
- **Compliance:** describe experiences, don't diagnose.

### F9. Partnership ads from real 55+ women and nurse creators
- **What:** take the best scripts (COM284, COM320, F1, F3) and run them as **Meta partnership ads** from
  real creators' accounts (a 60+ lifestyle creator, a nurse creator).
- **Why:** Kaori scales from creator-style pages. Jamil's board says to spread creators across TOF,
  MOF and BOF. It also makes ads pass the squint test.
- **Note:** Atria previously surfaced a "Nurse Finds" page running ads with your exact shoe. Check
  whether that's an affiliate, a creator partner or a knockoff.
