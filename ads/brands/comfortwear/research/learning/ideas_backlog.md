**Variety check:**
- Every story has a different narrator age, job, setting and family situation, and none repeats LF01–LF04.
- Hook types used: a shameful public moment (LF05), role reversal (LF06), insider expertise (LF07), a parent's warning (LF08), a secret (LF09), an odd-number fact (LF10), "nobody told me" (LF11), something watched through a window (LF12), a a quiet public moment of giving in (LF13), and a sacrifice for Christmas (LF14).

**Church concept guardrails (LF13):**
- Mention plantar fasciitis only as her own diagnosis and experience. No claim that the shoe treats or fixes it.
- Don't assume the reader's faith. Avoid lines like "If you're a believer…", since Meta's personal-attributes policy forbids implying a reader's religion. Tell it as her story.
- Images: no visible crosses or religious text in close-up. A generic small-town church interior is fine.

**Claims guardrails from research:**
- Don't promise "easy slip-on" or "hands-free". There are about 280 complaints about a tight collar.
- Don't promise extra-wide fit.
- Don't make slip-resistance the lead for kitchen jobs.
- No "walking on clouds", no medical cure claims, no podiatrist claims.
- Show a real company and the real 30-day money-back guarantee. Reddit distrusts cheap "ortho" brands.
