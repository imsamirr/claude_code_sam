# Cross-ad patterns that differ from our playbook

1. **Length ceiling is softer than 18k.** EverWell at 22.8k is rank 1 in 2 days. Julia Bennett's 31.6k evergreen has been active for 268 days, and Jessica Whitney's 16.4k ran 172 days. Our "past ~18k returns diminish" rule is not supported for 50+ health and caregiving audiences.
2. **Brand placement varies widely and isn't the driver.** Brand at 0% (RV, never named, lands on a listicle), 2% (YouthRite, founder), 30% (Steadfast), 38% (PhotoStick), 43% (Nolara), 46% (Fleece), 49–50% (BeetWise, Whisp), 60% (CellGel+, EverWell). The median of these is about 45%, earlier than our 60%. What makes an early brand land is who says it: a messenger handing it over, a tag read aloud, or a founder.
3. **Hook iteration on a fixed body is the standard scaling move.** Jessica Whitney (911 A/B; Linda Marsh × 3 openers), Volosso (ER-nurse body × 3 openers; RV body relaunched after 294 days), Julia Bennett (31.6k winner rewritten to 14.7k with a new opener). Winners last 120–300+ days when the hook is rotated and the body kept.
4. **New hook families our 14 don't cover:** (a) sunk-cost object image ($87k RV unused), (b) insider voyeur / "the one thing the rich family has" (nanny), (c) hard deadline plus found artifact (Friday 8 AM, laundry basket), (d) historical secret plus founder artifact (1944 nurses' footlocker), (e) professional irony (a 911 dispatcher who can't say the script). Revenge (13) appears twice at rank 1 (Whisp, EverWell), and both spend 15–20% of the body on the vindication scene, more than on results.
5. **Proof through measurement and logs, and the narrator's own doctor as verifier rather than messenger.** Pressure-map demo, a weekly BP notebook, a device-by-device timestamped recovery log, priced failed fixes, named competitor products as the failed fixes (SuperBeets, Nature's Way). Our "week one, nothing" holds in 3 of 10 (BeetWise, EverWell, Volosso CellGel+, the last with a day-4 relapse). Fast results appear in the others (YouthRite day 3, Whisp day 2, Fleece night 1).

Comfort Wear-relevant takeaways: the YouthRite "what nurses wore to survive 20-hour shifts" framing, the Volosso "sitting/standing equipment designed to hurt you; the companies knew" industry villain, the nanny "one thing the rich family has" aspirational angle (natural for travel and women's footwear), and the CellGel+ "two pinches" model (meds and PT treat one; what's under you treats the other).
