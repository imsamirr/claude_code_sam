# Stefan Georgi live call (Nov 17, 2026): "How I write 100+ good ads in a single day using AI"

**Source.** https://x.com/stefangeorgi/status/2122498096105992442. Stefan is the founder of StefanBrain. The call runs 1:43:46 with about 400 live viewers. It's a screen-share inside StefanBrain.

**Transcript.** `transcript.md` is a full machine transcript (faster-whisper small.en), grouped by minute. Product names are sometimes misheard: "Laura" = LURA, "Norse Organics" = Norse Organics, "Step and Brain" = StefanBrain.

**Demo brands.**
- **LURA:** made up for the call. A 12-in-1 vacuum cupping and red-light body massager for women aged about 52, sold on cellulite and lymphatic swelling.
- **Norse Organics:** a real acne brand, used only as a demo.
- **Glow Drops:** a self-tanner.

## 1. The big thesis: less context, more references (0:02 – 0:26)
Stefan ran the same prompt ("swipe this reference ad for LURA, outline first") with four different amounts of context:

| Version | Context in the project | Result |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | ~15-page AI-generated brand brief (voice guide, "mattress effect" mechanism, avatars, hook bank, compliance) | Jargon soup: "synchronized clinical modalities… 660nm… mattress effect" |
| V2 | ~7–8 pages (avatar section cut) | Slightly better, still jargon |
| V4 | ~1.5-page product one-pager (name, what it does, specs, box, pricing, guarantee) | Clearly better |
| **Final** | **No brief at all. Just 2 strong yapper ads he wrote for the brand, plus a 3-line project instruction** | **Best by far**: natural voice, story, one clear mechanism |

What he takes from it:
- "Context: you need less context, not more." He calls this "actually 95% of getting better outputs".
- **Big context docs secretly steer every output.** Buried lines ("no hype", "clinical big sister", "always explain both mechanisms", the mattress metaphor) make the AI restrained, repetitive or off-topic. You forget they're there and blame the tool.
- **AI-written context docs are full of "Claude-isms".** They make the copy sound like Claude.
- **Most StefanBrain churners have 50–100-page context docs.**
- **You no longer need avatar or pain-point matrices or big VOC dumps.** The model was trained on Reddit and the rest of the internet. VOC docs can also make it repeat the same phrases every time.
- **"Reference ads are king."** His winning yapper (~$1M spend in 4–5 days, $200–300k/day) took three prompts and under 10 minutes. He swiped another brand's ad, gave one round of feedback on the outline, and had it write the script.

## 2. The workflow he uses every time
1. **Set up a project** with 2–3 great reference ads, a few lines of instructions, and a short product one-pager only if info is missing. Optionally add a sample brief so it knows the deliverable format.
2. **Prompt:** "We're doing a new ad for [brand]. Here's a reference ad from another brand. Model the structure of the script closely, but for our brand. First outline the swipe; after I approve, you'll write it."
3. **Review the outline.** Catch structural problems here, and give short notes.
4. **Write.** Ask for "just the script — no storyboard, timestamps or production notes".
5. **Give one or two pieces of feedback, then rip.** The first ad in a new format is worth 15–30 minutes of back-and-forth. After that it's "next one, next one, next one". He calls it a snowball.

## 3. Turning one winner into 20+ ads (0:44 – 0:51)
- Adapt a proven ad to a new brand by keeping the story and changing only what the product requires.
- "Give me 20 more concepts that follow a similar structure. What changes is the spokesperson, the situation and elements of the story."
- When the AI drifts, name **the core dominant emotion** of the original. In his case it was jealousy and the high road (the ex sees her looking great). Then ask again. You get the farmer's market, dog park, wine bar, airport lounge, tailgate and lake-house versions.
- Ask for concepts as "big idea, situation, main character, what happens — a few sentences max", not full outlines.
- Then: "Write the yapper for concept 20", then 19, then 18, and so on. That's how 40–100 ads come out of an hour or two.

## 4. Formats he covered
**Song ads**
- Many winning song ads **don't use a classic rhyme scheme**. Ask for "less classic rhyme structure".
- Ask for the same structure with a new situation, location and characters.
- Running the video analyzer, rather than only pulling the transcript, tells StefanBrain it's a song.

**Yapper ads: his two "golden nuggets"**
1. **Write it like people speak.** Seven sentences in a row can start with "And". Use "and she's like… and I'm like…", in a "Valley-girl-light" voice for female audiences.
2. **The right creator makes a huge difference.** His winner had flopped until "the best yapper" re-recorded the exact same script. About 80% of that account's top yappers are now one woman. Find your racehorse and keep riding her. Women beat men "pretty much every time".

**The yapper story engine.** Almost every yapper he writes reveals the cause in one of three ways:
1. At an appointment with an expert. She apologizes for her body, and the expert explains what's really going on.
2. On the phone or texting with a friend who happens to be an expert.
3. At brunch or in person with an expert friend.

Occasionally it's a family member, or her own discovery journey.

**Other yapper rules**
- **Pick ONE dominant pain point per ad**, e.g. cellulite *or* lymphatic swelling, never both.
- **Watch the transitions**, e.g. the expert explains, and she says "wait, what does that even mean?"
- **Casual vs. authority yapper:** the only difference is confidence. The casual yapper *discovers* the truth. The authority yapper ("As a cosmetic chemist, please stop…") tells it with certainty.

**Casting age.** On a telemedicine landing page for a 52-year-old audience, a woman in her late 20s to 30s, and slim, beat women in their 40s, 50s and 60s. He cites a 50–60¢ RPV lift. His reasoning: "they see themselves as who they are in their head." You can write a yapper in a 25-year-old's voice and have a 51-year-old deliver it.

## 5. Hooks (0:54 – 1:08)
**His "money prompt"** (also saved as a StefanBrain snippet):
> "Give me 30 new ad hooks and concepts for [brand], problem aware, [format] ads like the references. Avoid generic ones that every LLM would give. As you evaluate initial ideas, tell yourself to make them weirder, scroll-stopping, include unique situations when relevant, to be 10X more creative and 10X less generic."

**Hooks he rated as winners**
- "I just spent 20 minutes sitting on the bench inside a dressing room, pretending I needed another size…"
- "My husband tried to put his hand on my thigh while we were driving home from dinner and I literally swatted his hand away like a stray wasp." He gave it 10/10: relatable situation, hidden insecurity, vivid simile.
- "My husband walked into our bedroom… me lying upside down with my legs plastered against the wall like a flipped-over beetle and asked if I was in a cult."
- "I spent $35 on a spiked foam roller from Amazon… only to find out I was doing the exact opposite of what my legs needed."

**What he pushes back on**
- AI tropes like "we need to have a serious talk", "you and I need to talk" and "you need to see this".
- Hooks that over-explain or close the loop.

**His fix:** "For every concept, give 1–2 options that are a single sentence, curiosity-heavy and completely open-ended." For example: "So the other day my boyfriend's golden retriever tried to lick my face." That's the whole hook. What happens next is the open loop.

**How he works through a list:** skim only the spoken hook, copy the good ones to a doc, and note interesting phrases from the weak ones.

## 6. Stem hooks (1:12 – 1:41): his skill, still in progress
**What a stem hook is.** Like a stem cell, it's a proto-hook (the core concept) before it becomes any specific format. Generic "give me 20 hooks" requests fail because **a hook depends on its format**.

**Anatomy (from the skill document on screen).** It starts with **the Outside Trigger**: a mundane habit, familiar object, overlooked friction or counter-intuitive everyday scenario. The other components weren't readable on screen.

**The 8 hook buckets** (2 stems per bucket, 12–16 stems per run):
1. Environmental / Daily Habits
2. Timing & Usage Riddles (when, or how often)
3. Friction & Mechanics (physical paradox: more effort gives a worse result)
4. Competitor & Status-Quo Attack (what conventional solutions hide)
5. Micro-Human / Relatable Quirks (unspoken moments of shame and coping habits)
6. Location & Micro-Environment
7. Long-Term Fear / Ticking Clock
8. The eighth bucket wasn't readable on screen.

**Process**
1. Generate the stems.
2. Self-revise: "make them weirder".
3. Pick the ones you like.
4. Translate each into up to **6 formats**: casual yapper, faceless AI voiceover, authority yapper, drama, song, and long-form native caption ad.
5. Write the scripts: "1.3, format 1, option 3 — write it, following the structure of the yapper ads in the project files".

**Faceless AI voiceover hook pattern.** Good: "Here's what using breath mints does to your teeth." Bad: "The psychological mint loop keeping millions of adults trapped…"

**Example: Glow Drops self-tanner.** The stem was the "first-date white towel crime scene". It produced:
- **Casual yapper:** "I climbed out a third-story bathroom window last weekend so a guy wouldn't see what my fake tan did to his white Ralph Lauren towel."
- **Authority yapper:** "As a cosmetic chemist, please stop believing a tan that stains your towels is just part of the process."
- A **song** version and a **drama** version.

**On StefanBrain.** It's best to give it a drama-ad or song-ad transcript as a reference. Otherwise it defaults to generic structures.

## 7. Rules and memory (1:27 – 1:31)
- Over-engineered rules have downstream consequences. A forgotten hard rule ("never reveal the product until X") later fights a new format, and the AI looks dumb.
- If you save learnings, ask the chat for a **short** handoff list of rules, prune it to the essentials, and keep voice rules soft.
- Long-running chats are fine. Chat memory carries feedback forward.

# What this means for ComfortWear (how we'll change our process)

**1. Our StefanBrain project is the "15-page brief" case.**
- The ComfortWear project carries a large knowledge base: the 38 KB files we pulled, a playbook, brand rules, and a compliance and voice guide.
- It also still has a hard rule saying never name competitors negatively. That rule fought Samir's approved Hoka / On Cloud / Skechers teardowns.
- My briefing messages to StefanBrain were also long: pattern lists, rules and structure.
- By Stefan's own test, all of this makes the copy worse and more "clinical".

**Proposal: a lean project, "Good_ComfortWear".** It would contain:
- **2–3 winning scripts only:**
  - COM284, "We asked 500 plus-sized women at Disney" ($6.1k at 2.58 ROAS).
  - The 203 UGC ad ("$60… way cheaper than the $300 orthotics"; $99.9k spend at 3.05 ROAS).
  - Our best yapper or nurse winner.
- **A half-page one-pager:**
  - Product truths.
  - The current offer: Black Friday 60% off through Dec 5, or 2 pairs + 2 free gifts, never both.
  - The exact guarantee wording.
  - The 9 real colorways.
- **A 3-line instruction.**

**2. New per-task prompt (Notion briefs).** For each task we send StefanBrain:
- Samir's concept line.
- The reference ad link or transcript.
- "Model the structure closely for ComfortWear. Outline first."

I review the outline against `winning_patterns_for_briefs.md`, which becomes **my QA checklist, not StefanBrain's context**. I send 1–2 short notes. Then StefanBrain writes it. I format the result into the Notion brief: Strategist's Note, 3 hooks, and the Script | Visual table.

**3. Variation engine for winners.** Use COM284 as an example. Name its core emotion: "I'm not the one slowing everyone down." Then ask for 20 new situations: Disney, NYC, cruise port days, grandkid's graduation, Vegas Strip, nursing double shift, pickleball, a church choir trip. Write one ad per situation. This is the same play as Samir's "500 women" task series, but done systematically.

**4. Yapper ads for ComfortWear**
- Write in spoken voice ("and she's like…").
- Use one of his 3 reveal setups. A podiatrist or nurse friend fits us naturally.
- **One pain point per ad:** plantar fasciitis *or* bunions *or* swelling, not all at once.
- Watch the "what does that even mean?" transition.
- **Casting matters as much as copy.** When a yapper flops, re-shoot it with a different creator before killing the concept. Then keep using the creator who wins.

**5. Hooks**
- Run his "make them weirder" prompt on every hook batch.
- Ask for single-sentence open-loop versions.
- Cut "we need to talk"-style AI tropes.
- Our past winners already fit his rule ("If you're a woman over 200 lbs…", "stop taking shoe advice from people who don't walk in your body"). Situational micro-shame hooks are the gap. Examples: hiding feet under the table at brunch, "volunteering" to hold the bags at Disney, or taking the elevator while her sister takes the stairs.

**6. Test, don't adopt blindly: casting younger.** His test was a telemedicine landing page. Our survey says 85% of buyers are 50+, and the plus-size identity call-outs win for us (COM284). Worth a controlled test, e.g. COM284's script delivered by a woman in her 40s vs. her 60s. Not a rule change yet.

**7. Stem hooks**
- Once his skill ships inside StefanBrain, use it per angle. Format list: casual yapper, authority (podiatrist/nurse) yapper, faceless AI VO, drama, song, native caption.
- Bucket 4 (Competitor & Status-Quo Attack) is where our Hoka/Skechers teardown lives.
- Bucket 3 (Friction & Mechanics) fits "softer foam = more heel pain".
