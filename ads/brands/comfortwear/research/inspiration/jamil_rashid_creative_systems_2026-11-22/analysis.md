## The six parts of his system

### 1. Hero unique mechanism (0:01–0:03): "the root of everything"
The board diagram flows in four steps:
1. **5+ angles**, each a symptom or problem.
2. **One unique mechanism**, the "root cause of every problem".
3. **Proof**, clinical or logical backing.
4. **USP**: how the product fixes that mechanism.

His points:
- "1 mechanism unlocks 10 to 20 angles. Each angle is a new pocket of buyers. Novel mechanism = light-bulb moment."
- Brands that scaled to eight figures a month **stuck to one mechanism and hammered it**, rather than testing new mechanisms. Resilia's single mechanism carried its weight-loss, brain-fog and energy angles.
- You can stack several angles in one ad, as long as each one traces back to the mechanism.
- The mechanism needs proof or a chain of logic the customer can follow in their head. "Some people's mechanisms are literally just pure Claude slop." Even the US market is sophisticated enough to see through that.

### 2. Creative team structure (0:03–0:06)
- **Lead strategist / CMO** on top. Give them equity, profit share or a small percentage of the team's spend, so they're actually motivated to help other strategists crack winners. They set the overall direction back to the hero mechanism. "Most strategists are decent — they can write a brief, swipe, use Claude." The gap is direction.
- **Strategists** split by funnel stage (top of funnel / bottom of funnel) or by SKU. The ratio is about 3 editors per strategist, and editors should be hired to keep up with brief volume.
- **An ops manager or creative assistant** runs the pipeline, assigns briefs, handles payroll and deadlines, and flags who is under- or over-performing.
- **A lead editor** handles quality control and stays current on AI production, because the models keep changing (Kling, HeyGen, Higgsfield…). Editor failures are often just "using the wrong model".

### 3. Pipeline (0:06–0:08)
Briefs move through ClickUp in this order:
1. Brief in progress.
2. Brief complete.
3. Assigned to an editor by the ops manager, by capacity.
4. Edit in progress.
5. Ready for review.
6. Needs revisions (they'll send work back 3–5 times).
7. Final asset approved.
8. Sent to the client and uploaded to Meta.

Build this before you need scale. ClickUp automations also let you track KPIs.

### 4. Daily updates (0:08–0:10)
These go in Slack:
- **Ops summary for the lead:** what's stuck, and what's been in revisions too long.
- **Editor start-of-day and end-of-day posts:** to keep a "$2k/month" editor accountable.
- **Strategist daily:** briefs uploaded, ads in progress, ideation, and progress against the month's target. He hated doing this at Resilia, but says it kept him accountable.

### 5. Formats and format fatigue (0:10–0:13)
- **The cycle:** a format works → everyone copies it → results fade → find the next format → repeat.
- **Timeline of hot formats:**
  1. Cartoon skeletons and doctors.
  2. Pixar-style song ads, around the same time as AI avatars.
  3. Long, emotional 10-minute Pixar story ads.
  4. **Now: drama ads.**
- **Fit has three dimensions:** niche × avatar × format.
  - Supplements is the most brutal niche.
  - In easier niches (hygiene, skincare, jewelry), a format that feels "late" to you may still be untested.
  - **Women watch drama ads and Pixar mini-movies more than men do.**
- **"In-market check":**
  - What is spending in your niche?
  - What is spending in other niches that you can bring over first?
  - Keep a running "2026 Working Formats" library (his board has around 30 frames). Use it to pair mechanism → angle → ideation → format → avatar.

### 6. Research engine (0:13–0:17)
- **Ad-spy tools** to find new brands and new formats that are scaling: filter to active video creatives and look at whitelisted pages. Formats he saw this way:
  - Pixar song ads.
  - AI "founder studio" ads.
  - Claymation.
- **Organic content** (Reels and TikTok) is where his agency got many winners. Over $500k in spend came from a few concepts lifted from organic. His example is an organic drama reel with a funny visual hook ("Grandpa, is that man going to pop like a balloon?"). Take the hook and visual ideas into ads.

### Close
"Systems don't make winners, good creative does, but systems let you make more of it." His three actions:
1. Tighten the creative process: daily updates, ClickUp, automations.
2. Spend hours researching competitors, other industries and organic content.
3. Find your hero mechanism and test it in formats that are currently working for other brands.

# What this means for ComfortWear

**1. Name one hero mechanism and route every angle through it.** We already run many angles:
- Plantar fasciitis, bunions, arthritis and neuropathy.
- Travel and Disney, nurses, plus-size, GLP-1, pickleball and assisted-living fear.

Across our briefs, though, the mechanism shifts from ad to ad: rocker sole, arch, foam collapse, toe box. By Jamil's rule we should pick one root cause and make every angle trace back to it.

The candidate already sits in our winners (Kyro 369, COM284): **"Soft foam collapses under your weight by early afternoon, so your arch and heel take every step on concrete. ComfortWear's arch support holds its shape all day and the rocker sole takes the pounding off your heel."**

Each angle is then a symptom of that one cause:
- Heel stab (plantar fasciitis).
- Bunion pressure (as feet swell).
- Knees and back hurting.
- Sitting out at Disney.
- Nurse hour 8.
- A plus-size woman's weight crushing foam faster.

This lines up with the masterclass rule (one mechanism, promise before mechanism) and with Stefan's "one dominant pain point per ad". So: one pain point per ad, but always the same root cause. **Proposal:** I draft the hero-mechanism statement and its proof chain through StefanBrain for Samir to approve, then use it as the fixed spine of every brief.

**2. Format fatigue: test drama and story formats.**
- Our winners are UGC survey ads, competitor teardowns and doctor/nurse narrators.
- Jamil says drama ads are what's working now, and that women (our 85%-women, 50+ buyers) over-index on drama ads and Pixar mini-movies.
- Stefan's call showed the same set: drama, song, faceless AI voiceover and yapper.
- Footwear is an easier niche than supplements, so the "late" formats (Pixar story ads, song ads) may still be fresh for us.
- **Suggested tests:**
  - A drama ad, e.g. a daughter planning Disney who notices Mom is "holding the bags" again.
  - A Pixar-style story of a grandmother who sat out the trip.
  - A song ad written in the loose, non-rhyming structure.

  Each would be swiped from a reference that's currently scaling.

**3. Add organic content to the research loop.** Pull hooks and visual gags from organic Reels and TikTok aimed at women 50+, the Disney-mom and nurse communities. StefanBrain's short-form research tool can pull these. Organic gives a different hook supply than the ad library.

**4. Pipeline (mostly confirms what we have).**
- Samir's Notion Creative Strategy Tracker already has Strategist and V-Editor columns and status flow.
- Worth considering: an explicit "Needs revisions" stage, and a weekly "working formats" library page in Notion with references by format.
