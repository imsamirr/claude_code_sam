## The framework in one page

**Hook sells nobody.** It buys attention and pre-qualifies; the other 57 seconds sell. Line 1's only job is to make line 2 inevitable.

**Desire is found, not created** (Schwartz). If the desire isn't in reviews, tickets, Reddit or surveys, there's no concept. Specific casting doubles as proof (Carlton's one-legged golfer: if *he* gained 50 yards, it's the secret, not the athlete).

**Differentiator filter:** is it a proposition, is it unavailable to competitors, will it move a stranger? "Family-owned / made in X / great service" fail. Delete rather than rewrite claims any competitor could make.

**Research yields five things:** the Accommodation (what she changed in her life to work around it) → Problem beat; the Failed Solution (what, cost, why it stopped working) → Objection; the "Nearly Didn't Buy" → Risk reversal; the Word (her nouns, not ours); the Moment (the day she went looking) → Lead/urgency. Never let AI invent objections; use it to retrieve real quotes.

**Awareness and sophistication.** Message-to-market match across script, visuals and primary text. Sophistication 3 = unique mechanism; 4 = enlarge the mechanism; 5 = identity ("who she becomes"). Higher-stage techniques win even in early markets.

**Offer > market > mechanism > proof > copy.** Five offer elements: the Thing, the Terms, the Reversal, the Reason for Now, the Effort.

### 1:04:10 — price + guarantee (the part Samir flagged)
Price framing, four ways:
1. **Anchor against the alternative.** Not "was $89, now $69", but "one appointment with a specialist costs $200 and they'll tell you to take this anyway".
2. **Reduce to the ridiculous**, e.g. per-day cost against a coffee.
3. **Price the problem, not the product**: "you've already spent more than this on two things that didn't work".
4. **Make effort the price**: remove friction.

Guarantee rewrite rules:
- Specific, not boilerplate.
- Slightly uncomfortable for the seller.
- Matched to the mechanism's timeline.
- Makes her follow the protocol ("send back the empties and we refund all three").

Most offer upgrades are reframes of what the business already does, so the strategist can make them without new approvals.

### The nine beats
1. Hook.
2. Problem: the consequence or accommodation, not the symptom; 10–15s; never resolve it inside the problem.
3. Promise: plain outcome, in her words.
4. Mechanism.
5. Objection: failed solutions and category skepticism.
6. Proof.
7. Offer.
8. Risk reversal.
9. CTA: name the action, what happens next, the effort, and a reason for now.

Three rules that run through the beats:
- **The promise always comes before the mechanism.**
- **A smaller promise you can prove beats a bigger one you can't** (Ben Settle).
- **Specifics beat adjectives**: numbers, named third parties, physical details, durations, proper nouns. Look for the boring factory truth no one else says, as Schlitz did with live-steam-sterilized bottles.

**Most ads die at the bridges:**
- **Hook → body.** A generic hook stapled onto a body breaks the line.
- **Body → product.** Introduce the solution at the category or ingredient level before naming the brand: "you can buy those three separately… or one capsule at night".

**Proof ladder, strongest first:**
1. Demonstration on camera.
2. Third-party verification.
3. Volume of specific social proof ("4,800 reviews; the most common word was 'finally'").
4. Named, persona-matched testimonial.
5. Mechanism explanation.
6. Founder assertion.

**Belief ≠ desire.** Close on identity ("The women who keep it are the ones who started before they could see it").

**Story is the vehicle, not a beat.** In a story she follows "what happened next" instead of auditing claims. Four archetypes: Failed-Solutions Journey, Discovery, Transformation with a Witness, Confession. Story killers are a clumsy product bridge, an actor-ish narrator, and **no stakes**. Write in first person, past tense, and never resolve the story in the hook.

**Say it out loud:**
- One idea per sentence; front-load verbs.
- Grade 5–7 reading level; always use contractions.
- Long-long-short rhythm; avoid constant "not X, but Y" contrasts.
- Stand up and read it at pace: stumble → cut; out of breath → shorten; embarrassed → it's marketing-speak.

**Format comes last.** Find the load-bearing beat:
- An invisible edge (supplements) needs mechanism visuals.
- **A visible edge (footwear) needs demonstration.**
- An edge in the terms needs the offer up front.

Longer ads scale to colder audiences by cycling problem → promise → mechanism → objection → proof, with several CTAs.

**AI writes the average unless fed real research.** Use it for retrieval, transposition, variant volume and structural audits.
