## What Stefan teaches (checked against the transcript)

1. **"You need less context, not more."** He gave StefanBrain four versions of one brand: a 15-page brief, a 7–8-page brief, a page-and-a-half brief, and *just 2 reference ads*. "The 2 reference ads wins." The long briefs made the copy stiff and scientific. This was his side-by-side judgment of the outputs on the call, not an ad split test.
2. **The sports car analogy.** StefanBrain "is kind of like a high-performance sports car… the steering is very responsive. You don't need to overcorrect." Heavy context and rules oversteer it.
3. **Hidden rules leak into everything.** A line buried in a brief ("we always explain the dual reality of…") makes every ad cover both topics. "We need to pick a lane… always pick one dominant pain point." Hard rules have "downstream consequences" weeks later (e.g. "never reveal the product until the mechanism beat"). Keep any rule doc short, essentials and compliance only.
4. **Customers who churn from StefanBrain almost always have 5,000-word or 50–100-page context docs**, often AI-written, so the output starts "sounding like Claude".
5. **Outline first.** "I will pretty much always have the LLM… outline whatever it is we're gonna do first. And then I will review that, give feedback to the outline, and then have it write it."
6. **Swipe a reference ad**, e.g. "here's a reference ad from another brand. We're going to model the structure of the script closely… for our brand."
7. **Hooks:** ask for many concepts with "avoid generic ones that every LLM would give… make them weirder, scroll stopping, include unique situations… 10x more creative." Cut AI tells like "you and I need to have a serious talk". Use open loops (e.g. "I almost broke up with my boyfriend last month while we were staying at this amazing Airbnb").
8. **Stem hooks.** One raw human situation, branched into six formats: casual yapper, faceless explainer, authority yapper, drama, song, native long-form caption.
9. **Yappers sound like real people:** messy, lots of "and". The creator matters hugely. The same script flopped until the brand's best creator recorded it, and then it spent about $270k in a day.
10. **Write younger than the demo.** Older buyers "see themselves as… in their 20s or 30s." A late-20s model beat 40s, 50s and 60s models on a 52+ telemedicine page (a "50 or 60 cent RPU lift").
11. **VOC dumps aren't needed for writing.** The model "was trained on like all of Reddit". Use research to find angles and hooks, not as a prompt dump; big quote files make it recycle the same phrases.
12. **"Airplane takeoff."** Spend the back-and-forth on the first one or two ads of a format; once it "knows what's going on", it "starts ripping": "next one, please".

## Where this collides with how we set up DentalPULSE (decisions for the owner)

| Our current setup | Stefan's advice |
|---|---|
| The DentalPULSE project has 50+ product files, long instructions, and a growing style rulebook (video_script_style.md, masterclass_lessons.md). | Less context: 2 reference ads plus a short brief. |
| Rule: "Name bacteria and yellow stains in every ad." | Pick one lane per ad. This rule is the "dual reality" trap he describes. |
| Rule: "Serious and credible for a 60+ reader." | Tone rules make it "weirdly polite"; write the way real (younger-feeling) people talk. |
| One-shot "write the full script" asks. | Outline first, give feedback, then write. |
| Big VOC files (Reddit, Amazon, YouTube). | Use them to find angles, not as writing context. |

Compliance rules (claims, no competitor names, 5-minute cycle, the offer) still need to hold. Stefan keeps "essential… compliance" boundaries.
