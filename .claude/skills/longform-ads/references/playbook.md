## 0. The one-paragraph version

Every winner is a **first-person story told by someone the reader trusts more than an advertiser**. It opens **in the middle of the worst moment**, and the reader's own "safe" behavior turns out to be the cause. The story **removes the reader's guilt** ("you did everything right"). It catalogues **failed fixes**, so every past purchase is explained. Then the narrator hits **rock bottom**.

Next, a **stranger or insider hands over a reframe**: the real cause lives somewhere the current fix can't reach. That **mechanism** is explained through **one household analogy**. The **ingredient or category** comes before the brand. Then **cheaper versions of the same thing get disqualified**: wrong form, wrong dose, heat, frequency. The brand arrives **as the only thing that passes**, usually around **60% of the way through**, in **one short isolated sentence**.

From there the copy runs a **week-by-week results timeline** that starts with an honest "week one, nothing". A **doctor re-test** follows ("whatever you're doing, keep doing it"). The **opening image comes back reversed**. Then **guarantee, scarcity and a P.S. stack**, one objection per P.S.

## 1. The master structure (with % positions)

Medians across ~95 ads. Very long ads (30k+) stretch the story half and keep the commercial half about the same absolute length.

| # | Beat | % of copy | Job | Real examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | 0–3% | Stop the scroll with an oddly specific scene, number or confession. Often matches the image. | "The bag of frozen peas on my feet was supposed to be my kids' dinner." / "I fill 340 statin prescriptions a week. I won't fill my own." |
| 2 | **Reading contract + identity** | 2–10% | Age, job and years give credibility. "Let me back up." "I know this is long. Please read it." | "I'm 47. I've been a nurse for 19 years." / "My husband is 74. He has worn dentures for eleven years." |
| 3 | **Life shrinking** | 5–25% | 2–4 *specific* losses, each planted for a payoff later. | Skipped the kid's game, the farmers market, the cruise, Saturday open play |
| 4 | **"Did everything right" + failed fixes** | 10–30% | Removes self-blame. Names products and prices. Every past purchase gets "explained" later. | "Fish oil. Turmeric. Magnesium. Nothing changed." / "$800… two car payments" / named rivals: Polident, SuperBeets, Miralax |
| 5 | **Dismissive authority** | 10–35% | Builds the villain without attacking doctors. | "Keep an eye on it." "Monitor it." "That's just aging, Don." "Bloods came back fine." |
| 6 | **Rock bottom** | 20–40% | Emotional floor, usually a car, couch, bathroom floor or garden. Time-stamped. | "on one knee in the dirt, grey in the face" / "sat in my car in the parking garage for a long time" / "Nana, are you broken?" |
| 7 | **The encounter** | 25–50% | Someone *other than the narrator's doctor* hands over the key: a young doctor on the evening round, an 83-yr shoemaker, a hygienist in an FB group, an Amish grandmother, a stranger in the Whole Foods aisle. | "asked a question that nobody else had thought to ask in eleven years" |
| 8 | **Mechanism, part 1: the real cause** | 30–55% | The longest single block (15–25% of copy). Reframes the problem and explains why every failed fix failed. | "The tablet works on the surface. It cannot penetrate into the acrylic." |
| 9 | **Villain** (woven in) | 20–60% | The box, the industry, the "monitor" system, cheap Amazon versions. Doctors are well-meaning but downstream. | "If they told us the real solution upfront… we would buy it once and be done." |
| 10 | **Ingredient / category named** | 40–57% | Before the brand. | "Betalain." / "Ultrasonic cavitation." / "True Ceylon cinnamon." |
| 11 | **Mechanism, part 2: why cheaper versions fail** | 45–70% | The most-used device in the whole set. Wrong form, heat, dose, species, frequency, or fillers. | 12 | **BRAND** | **median ~60%** (range 28–92%) | One short, isolated line. It arrives as a search result, a hand-off or a checklist survivor. | "I found a company. Alevia." / "That company is Rosabella." / "The only pair that passed was CUURT." |
| 13 | **Skeptic beat** | right after the brand | "God, I was skeptical." "I nearly boxed it up." | |
| 14 | **Results timeline** | 60–85% | Day 3 / week 1 (nothing) / week 2–3 (first sensory sign) / week 4–8 (each planted loss paid off) / month 3 (labs). | "Week one, nothing." → "my fingers feel like fingers again" → "eGFR 48 → 56" |
| 15 | **Doctor reversal** | 75–90% | The same authority that dismissed now validates. | "Whatever you're doing, keep doing it." (in 15+ ads) / "Improving. Not managed. Improving." |
| 16 | **Testimonial ring** | 75–90% | Spouse, sister, neighbor, coworker try it too. | |
| 17 | **Callback close** | 85–95% | The opening image reversed. | Peas back in the freezer; the son's second drawing; "You didn't say maybe." |
| 18 | **Mirror + crossroads** | 85–95% | "If you're reading this and you see yourself in…" / "One path… Another path." | |
| 19 | **Offer / guarantee / scarcity** | 90–97% | The guarantee is framed against pharma or tied to the story timeline. | "No statin ever came with a money-back guarantee." / "90 days, the same window the trials used." |
| 20 | **P.S. stack** | 95–100% | 1–6 P.S.s, one objection each: already on meds, stubborn spouse, knockoffs, timeline, share-with-a-loved-one. | "If a woman you love has started smiling with her mouth closed…" |

## 2. Hooks: 14 formulas that recur

The common move: **the reader's assumed-safe behavior is the cause of the disaster**.

1. **Insider refusal:** "I fill 214 prescriptions for children's ADHD medication every month. I won't fill my own son's."
2. **Compliance paradox / death timestamp:** "My hunting buddy quit drinking in July. He was dead by Halloween." / "My husband died on a dialysis chair… April 28th at 3:47 PM."
3. **Shameful domestic image** (matches the static photo): "My son drew me on the couch with two green squares on my feet."
4. **Photo misdirection:** "No. Nobody punched me. I know what this photo looks like."
5. **"By the end you'll be furious" + numbered preview:** "…there are three things happening right now. One." (Sculptique and NovaPod use the identical template.)
6. **Profane fury + permission:** "I'm fucking furious writing this. But my husband said if it stops even one person…"
7. **Specialist "stop doing X" / "it's not X":** "Please STOP sizing up your tennis shoes." / "The most dangerous sign of kidney failure is not back pain."
8. **Misdiagnosis reversal:** "Turns out I don't have plantar fasciitis, and you might not either."
9. **Scam reversal:** "SNATCHED IS A SCAM. That was the title of the post I wrote last month."
10. **Cryptic line from a loved one:** "My husband told me he 'missed my scent.'" (248 days)
11. **Victory-turned-horror:** "I lost 47 pounds. And I lost my face doing it."
12. **Result-first + loop:** "Twelve hours. Twenty-two thousand steps. Zero pain. To understand why, you need to hear about a bench outside Space Mountain."
13. **Revenge / social vindication:** "For 11 months my ex told our friends I was 'struggling'. Last weekend, I shut him up."
14. **Interactive task:** "Right now. Go grab your shoes. Flip them over."

Hook mechanics:
- Line 1 is ≤15 words.
- Lines 2–5 are fragments that raise a question.
- An explicit promise ("by the end…", "the truth turned up on a shelf in our garage") opens the master loop.

## 3. Open loops: how they keep people reading 3,000 words

1. **The master loop.** A promised reveal planted in lines 1–3 closes at 50–95%. Examples: "five words", "why I drive to an Amish farm", "the woman who saved my husband's life", "why he's still waiting on my answer".
2. **Plant/payoff ledger.** Each "life shrinking" loss gets paid off *by name* in the results timeline. The farmers market → the game → Portugal. "You always say maybe" → "You didn't say maybe."
3. **The object bookend.** This is the most reliable device across all sources. A physical object from the hook returns at the end: the pill organizer, the tablet box, the sticky note, the stool, the Christmas card stamped DECEASED, the coffee mug he couldn't wrap his hands around.
4. **The "question nobody asked".** "How does he clean his dentures?" / "Did your wife wear dentures?" It closes immediately but opens the mechanism.
5. **Explicit IOUs.** "Let me back up." "I'll get to the beet powder." "Remember that. It matters later." "Then she said something that stopped me cold." "He wasn't finished."
6. **Single-word cliffhangers** on their own line. "Fistula." "Parasites." "Betalain." "Nuzzle."
7. **False-resolution loop.** The narrator tries the cheap version, it seems to work, and then the lab number gets *worse* (LDL 191). This is the strongest anti-competitor device.
8. **Deadline loop.** "My appointment was in nine days." This builds suspense into the results section.
9. **Relationship loop** underneath the sales story: an estranged sister, "Nana, are you broken?", a husband's "Another miracle cure?". It resolves *after* the product works, so the product gets credit for the emotional resolution.
10. **Stacking.** By ~40% there are 3–4 loops open (the death, my own number, the stranger's tip, the "qualifier"). They close in reverse order.

## 4. The mechanism: the engine of the whole ad

**A two-layer mechanism, always.**
- **Layer 1, the hidden cause:** the problem lives where the reader's current tool can't reach.
  - Biofilm *inside* porous acrylic.
  - The gut 30 ft from the mouth.
  - The toe box shape, not your foot.
  - "-osis not -itis."
  - Oxidized LDL, not the LDL number.
- **Layer 2, why the usual fixes fail:** the drug or product "manages the number, not the cause". *"The number is the smoke, not the fire."* *"Silences the alarm, never puts out the fire."* *"Stops the damage but doesn't repair it."* The product is positioned **"alongside, not instead of"** meds in the safer copy.

**Then re-explain each failed fix under the new model.**
- Foam rebounds overnight, so "you're restarting the clock".
- Sizing up gives "a running start into that same point".
- More CPAP pressure means more contaminants.

**Simplify with ONE household analogy carried through the whole ad.**
Rust / sliced apple, kitchen sponge, kettle limescale, the alarm vs the fire, a leaking roof vs the ceiling stain, a fuel tank, a frayed rope, a fortress wall, sniper vs shotgun, a garden hose at a house fire, a check-engine light covered with tape. The best ones are **drawn**: a napkin sketch, three circles on exam paper.

**Coin a name** for the problem: "Collapse Fatigue", "the Scavenging Cascade", "the Triple Drought", "fasciosis".

**Give a free self-test** the reader can do tonight: thumb in the heel, flip the shoe, trace your foot, check the label for sodium. This turns the reader into a participant.

**Use visible proof.** Brown water after the first ultrasonic clean, four hairs in the hand, a filament test going from 3/10 to 8/10, lab values to the decimal.

**Disqualify the category.** This is what makes *this brand* necessary. There are five failure reasons:
1. **Heat destroys it** (cold-pressed vs spray-dried, punicalagins, anthocyanins).
2. **Wrong route or form** (capsule vs liquid, swallowed vs topical).
3. **Under-dosed** (2–4% vs 10%; 5 mg vs 300 mg).
4. **Wrong species or spec** (Cassia vs Ceylon; 35 vs 40 kHz; 395 vs 253.7 nm; blue LED vs UV-C).
5. **Fillers, sugar or proprietary blends.**

Then the copy gives a **3–5 point criteria list**. "Five things. All five. At once." / "Four for four."

**Cross-cultural or ancient proof.** Sardinian grandmothers, Amish farms, Japan, 5,000 years in Central Asia: "when independent traditions arrive at the same conclusion…"

## 5. The product bridge: exactly how they hand off to the product

**Rule 1: ingredient first, brand second.** ~70% of ads name the ingredient or category 5–25 points before the brand.
- Betalain at 38% → Rosabella at 56%
- Pomegranate at 42% → Alevia at 70%
- Ceylon at 52% → Metabolae at 71%
- Elderberry at 52% → Elvera at 64%

**Rule 2: the brand never arrives as an ad.** It comes through a gatekeeper:
| Bridge type | How it reads |
|---|---|
| **Criteria → only one passed** | "I looked into different brands and found one small American company called X that does it the right way." / "The only pair that passed was CUURT." |
| **Unbranded expert prescription → match** | The doctor writes "Type V… 30mg HA… L. acidophilus…" on a pad, then "Everything she told me to find was in one capsule." This keeps the expert clean. |
| **Trusted-human hand-off** | A sister, son, church friend, a widow who *hands over a bag*, a stranger in the shop. The brand is spoken in **their** dialogue or written on a scrap (boarding pass, hall pass, counter pad). |
| **Exclusive partner** | "They'd partnered with one company. Only one. That company is Rosabella." |
| **Crowd source** | A Reddit thread with 600 comments, an FB group, "the same device kept coming up, comment after comment", overheard in a smoothie line. |
| **Experienced before named** | The cabin pillow, the mudroom boots, the box on the garage shelf. The brand turns up on a tag. |
| **Fake-organic EDIT** | "EDIT: My bad, I didn't even mention the brand. My DMs are full 👇" at ~95%. Ran 120–210 days. |
| **Never named** | The body sells the click and the brand lives only in the LP/URL (Koprez, Everly-dad, NovaPod furious wife). |

**Rule 3: the brand sentence is short and alone on its line.** "I found Everly." / "Ravea." Then an immediate skeptic beat: "God, I was skeptical."

**Rule 4: brand-first only works when the brand IS the story** (scam reversal, an investigator) or for product-aware retargeting.

## 6. Proof & specificity checklist

Every strong ad includes most of these:
- [ ] The exact age (clusters at 44–74)
- [ ] Town or state, job + years ("drove a school bus twenty-six years")
- [ ] Named relatives and a named doctor
- [ ] Dates and **times** ("3:47 AM", "April 28th", "22 minutes")
- [ ] Dollar totals of failed fixes ("$12,000… Radiancy was $49"; "$89k/yr dialysis vs $2 a day")
- [ ] Before/after numbers (LDL 172→124; BP 148/92→124/80; probe 5-5→3-3; 22,000 steps)
- [ ] A sensory detail at the turn ("metallic taste", "dirty water", "my fingers feel like fingers again")
- [ ] An honest ceiling ("I'm not telling you my kidneys healed"; "Week one, nothing")
- [ ] A humility marker ("I'm not a doctor. I'm a welder."; "I don't work for X. They don't know I exist.")

## 7. Emotional engine

**Dominant engines:**
- Guilt or grief over a loved one
- Rage at a trusted authority or product
- Humiliation, often public kindness that stings (the principal's stool, the "more reasonable for you" sticky note)
- Love and legacy (grandkids, "breaking the chain")

**The turn:**
- It is always a **sensory proof moment**, not a claim.
- Long runners lean on **guilt-removal**: "it's not behavioral, it's sensory" (UpAiry, 322d), "it was never your feet", "it wasn't my body".

## 8. Style rules

- **Lines:** one sentence per line is dominant. 1–3 sentence paragraphs at most. Fragments for rhythm: "Flat. Wrong." "Normal. Unavoidable."
- **Reading level:** grade 3–6.
- **Dialogue carries the mechanism.** The expert *says* it; the copy never lectures. Quotes are often reported without quote marks.
- **Repetition:** anaphora in the villain and CTA sections ("Not… Not… Not…", "Everything the dentist said. Everything the box said."). One-word callback lines: "Geography." "Years." "Eleven years."
- **Profanity:** only as a hook pattern-interrupt, never throughout.
- **Voice:** regional voice on purpose (UK, AU, Southern US) to match the target geo. Watch out: one GB-targeted NovaPod ad says "000", which is the Australian emergency number.

## 9. Length norms

| Use | Characters | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Presell / native testimonial (LP carries the story) | 2–6k | 400–1,100 |
| **Sweet spot for longevity** | **5–14k** | **1,000–2,700** |
| Full advertorial (tragedy/widow flagship) | 15–35k | 3,000–6,500 |
| Mega-length (works only as real fiction) | 30–47k | 6,000–8,700 |

- **Ads running 150+ days span 2.1k–42.6k characters, median ~9k.**
- **Past ~18k, returns diminish** unless it's genuinely novelistic: the PiPi Tea 42.6k ad ran 162d, but the 33k and 45k extensions of the Purelia template lasted ~32d.

## 10. What separates long-runners from short-lived ads (Atria run data)

1. **One simple upstream mechanism, stated in a sentence.** Examples: wrong organ, dirty air, it's sensory not behavioral.
2. **One tight emotional engine and one named loved one.** Don't widen to "5 aging symptoms". Carter's "not back pain" (116d, active) beat his "5 symptoms" spin-off (31d).
3. **Softer closes survive.** The #1 Everly ad (216d) has no offer, no guarantee and no link. Pet-Lab (446d) has no offer. Pure discount copy died in 1–7 days. **The story sells the click; the LP sells the offer.**
4. **Pain-first beats product-first** on the same product. The NovaPod story ran 46d vs 20d for the spec-first cut. The CUURT chiropractor ran 117d vs 23d for the BOGO siblings.
5. **Desirable stakes beat humiliation stakes.** Nimbao's Disney and cruise ads ran 103d (active) vs 44–45d for the pharmacy and teacher ads.
6. **Disguised first-person narrators beat founder/brand voice with spec sheets.** KIN ran 11d vs Repellix 133d and KittySupps 210d. The Nuzzle customer story ran 107d vs the founder version at 85d.
7. **Originals outlive their spin-offs.** Elvera's original ran 198d vs ≤66d for its variants. Radiancy ran 214d vs 57d for the Ploise clone. Iterate hooks and narrators, but expect the control to keep winning.
8. **Aggressive "industry scam / furious" angles spike and burn.** The OrthoFit exposé reached rank #1 but ran only 28d. Medication-refusal and anti-doctor copy died in under 10 days (beetroot 4d).
9. **Continuity errors correlate with dead ads.** The Airbnb ad named the narrator "Stephanie" while her husband calls her "Jenna" (5d). Koprez had a clinician POV in a patient P.S. (0d). Elvera's brother died at 58, then became a father who died at 63.
10. **Market and offer fit matter as much as copy.** Identical hearing-aid copy ran 193d in the US ($249, Medicare) and 34d in Canada ($300).

## 11. The modular system the best brands use

**CUURT pattern: one mechanism block, many narrators.** CUURT shares one mechanism, objections and offer block across four ads, each with a different narrator and hook:
- husband ×2
- a surgeon
- the founder

All four rank in the top 4% of the brand's 400 ads. To copy it:
- Write the **mechanism + bridge + proof + offer + P.S.** once.
- Then write 4–8 different **narrator + hook + life-shrinking + rock-bottom + encounter** front halves to bolt onto it.
- Test long and short cuts of the same story side by side. Orthora launched 2.1k, 5.4k and 7k versions on the same day.

## 13. Don't copy these (compliance)

Several high performers use:
- fabricated doctor personas
- made-up forum quotes
- disease-treatment claims ("edema gone in 2 days")
- medication-avoidance advice
- real-brand name-drops (Hyatt, Marriott, NATO)
- domestic-violence misdirection imagery

Copy the **structure**, not those claims. Rosabella is the cleanest model of compliance hygiene. It uses an asterisked claim, an FDA disclaimer, a dated Amazon rating, "nobody here is going to promise you a transformation", and "alongside, not instead of your medication". Several brands also add a "This story is a dramatization" line.

## 14. Learned from running the Comfort Wear program (Nov 2026)

- **The opening is the ad.** The user's top priority is the hook and the open loops in the first 10 lines. Every concept ships with 3 alternate hooks, and each one tests a different hook type.
- **Ranked by our own data:**
  - Comparison and test formats (each popular product priced, each with its own way of failing, then "$60 beat the $180 pair") carried about $100k on travel.
  - "Running shoe vs a standing job" carried the nurse angle.
  - The best efficiency was a persona-narrowed version: plus-size + Disney survey at 3.22 ROAS and $33 CPA.
- **What lifetime data showed:** Europe/cobblestone travel (about $370k) was the biggest historical angle, but it was invisible in a 90-day window.
- **The competitor format that worked:** Orthora's nurse long-form.
  - The pain is shown through what the kids see at home.
  - The headline is the hook line.
  - It adds single-income stakes and a list of how she hides it at work.
  - The brand is held back until a late "EDIT:".

  This is a structure to borrow, not lines.
- **Image look the user wants:** native, real iPhone photos. Deep focus, flash, clutter, crooked framing. No cinematic glow.
- **Feedback mining found:**
  - Many buyers are 65+ and still working.
  - Complaints about a tight collar mean no "easy slip-on" promise.
  - Requests for wide widths mean no "extra-wide" claims.
  - Unhappy buyers still provide most of the usable story material.
- **What Reddit distrusts:** cheap "ortho" brands shipped from abroad, "walking on clouds" lines, unnamed podiatrists, endless sales, and recycled testimonials. Answer with a real company and the real guarantee.
