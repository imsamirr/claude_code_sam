## 1. The 4 sales (none of them is the product)

> "Most people get it wrong when they think the job of a cold-traffic ad is to sell the product… It has to
> make 4 sales (and none of them is the product)."

The product is the *result* of four smaller agreements. If one is missing, the ad stalls at that step.

1. **Content sale**: "grabbing the avatar's attention and making them want to hear what your ad has to say, all the way to the end." → *Is this worth my time?*
2. **Hope sale**: three parts:
   - "what's been stopping them from solving the problem" (why everything failed)
   - "the action that needs to happen" (what has to change)
   - "a specific mechanism exists (usually rare, unique or hard to find) that performs that action" (the thing that does it)

   → *Is there finally a real reason this could work?*
3. **Value sale**: the product delivers that mechanism "under the ideal conditions", and is the only one that does all four of these:
   - raises the odds
   - makes the dream scenario tangible
   - shortens the time
   - cuts the effort

   It does all this "at a price that feels dramatically lower than what it could cost the avatar to never solve the problem." → *Is THIS the one, and is it worth it?*
4. **Impulse sale**: "the risk of NOT taking the offer is bigger than the risk of taking it" and "a lot to gain by buying right now… a lot to lose by putting it off." → *Why now, and why is it safe?*

> "A sale is a process of persuasion. Persuading is presenting an idea to someone and giving them reasons
> why that idea is true."

So every sale is made with **reasons why**. "Ad psychology" = the full set of reasons.

**For statics:** one image cannot carry 17 elements. Assign them:

| Where | Carries |
|---|---|
| **The image** (2 seconds) | Content sale in full (format, topic, concept, visual hook, copy hook) **plus one** Hope-sale beat (usually point of contact or problem mechanism, e.g. "Your running shoes weren't built for 12-hour shifts"). Offer ads add the Impulse beat (60% OFF, ends date). |
| **Primary text** | Identification → point of contact → problem mechanism → function mechanism → solution mechanism → conditions → USP → features (as levers) → anchoring → risk reversal → gain now → lose later. |
| **FB headline / description** | The single strongest reason or the offer. |

An image that only shows product + discount has made the Impulse sale with no Content or Hope sale.
That is the "basic" look the user kept rejecting.

## 2. The 17 elements (ad psychology)

| # | Element | His definition (verbatim core) | Static-ad translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Format | "The reason the avatar gets comfortable, lowers their guard and doesn't even suspect someone is trying to sell them something." | Native-looking layouts beat "ad-looking" ones: notes app, text message, comment bubble, whiteboard, receipt, letter, doctor/nurse explainer, POV story, UGC photo. Pick one proven in the market. |
| 2 | Topic | "The reason the avatar might care about what your ad has to say." | A fact, news item, question or situation she already cares about (e.g. "Can I use my FSA on shoes?", "Why your feet hurt more at hour 10", "Disney's new park opening"). |
| 3 | Concept | "The reason the avatar feels compelled to watch your ad to the end and can't leave even if they wanted to." | Characters + narrative + topic in one logline. In a static: the tension the image sets up and the primary text resolves ("Don't wear these unless…", "The nursing shoe industry lies about your pain"). |
| 4 | Visual hook | "The reason the avatar snaps out of autopilot and stops everything." | The one unexpected visual: the rotten banana equivalent. For us: shoe graveyard pile, flattened running-shoe foam, nurse limping to the car at 7 pm, swollen feet in a shoe at hour 12, a bench at Disney with everyone's bags. Must fit the format and the concept. |
| 5 | Copy hook | "The reason the avatar decides whether to keep watching your ad or not." | The headline. Use a structure proven in the market ("This is what happens to… when…", "Don't X unless…", "Your X weren't built for Y", "X industry lies about…"). |
| 6 | Identification | "Feels understood, like they're talking to someone who gets them as well as they get themselves." | Her private sentences: "I'm only 54, why do I feel 80 getting out of bed?", "limping to my car after a 12", "the bench holding everyone's bags". |
| 7 | Point of contact | "What the avatar already suspects is causing their problem." | What she already blames: her shoes, hard floors, getting older, her weight, standing all day. Never fight it; confirm it. |
| 8 | Problem mechanism | "The missing reason that proves their suspicion right, and the real reason their problem happens." | Connect the dots she half-knows (see §6 for ours). |
| 9 | Function mechanism | "The action that needs to happen to fix it, and the main reason other solutions can't do it." | What has to change, and why insoles / running shoes / clogs can't do it. |
| 10 | Solution mechanism | "The one solution that performs that action, and the reasons that prove it." | The shoe's construction that does that action. |
| 11 | Conditions | "Why other solutions don't offer this mechanism, and what makes it rare, exclusive and/or hard to get." | Answer "can't I just buy X?" before she thinks it. |
| 12 | USP | "Why your product is the best (or the only) place the avatar can get the mechanism under the right conditions." | Simply the answer to Conditions, feature by feature. "I don't need to make up any differentiator." |
| 13 | Features | "Why they increase the odds, make the dream tangible, minimize the effort and/or shorten the timeline." | Every feature must name its lever. A feature with no lever stays out. |
| 14 | Anchoring | "Not solving the problem costs more (financially, emotionally, in time) than the price." | $59.95 vs the $160–$220 pairs in her closet; cents per shift; the trip she sat out; the shifts she can't afford to miss. |
| 15 | Risk reversal | "Why the risk of NOT BUYING is bigger than the risk of buying." | Not "30-day guarantee" (everyone has one) but why 30 days is enough to prove it on her feet, plus the risk of another season in shoes that already failed. |
| 16 | What they gain now | "The reasons the avatar comes out ahead by buying now." | Current offer (60% OFF, LEAKED10, free shipping & returns). |
| 17 | What they lose later | "The reasons the avatar loses out by buying later." | Offer end date, a trip or shift season coming up. Only true scarcity. |

## 4. Steps 1–2: Premises and Target

**Premises** = facts we already have from day one: brand, product, ingredients/components, attributes,
price, guarantee, what they gain now, what they lose later. "We never start from zero." For us these
live in `brand.json` (`ad_psychology.premises`) and must be exactly true (no invented stock stories).

**Target: market → segment → avatar.**
- *Market* = "a specific group of people who share the same desire." Pick ONE per ad.
  Choosing it, he minimizes risk: copy what already has traffic (our highest-spend ads and landers, or the
  market with the most competitor growth on Atria). "What you gain by finding a brand-new market is usually
  less than what you gain by landing a new concept, format or hook in a hot market."
- *Segment* = a cluster inside the market sharing a condition, identity or behavior. Two ways: reuse the
  segments that work for us or competitors, or find new ones on Reddit.
- *Avatar* = two layers:
  - Psychographics: primary desire, problem, secondary desires, pain points, dreams, fears.
  - Identity: demographics, behavior and perspective (beliefs, biases, enemies, allies, language).

  His controversial point: deep psychographic research is low-leverage, because the winners
  (ours and competitors', from any year) already contain it, and "an avatar's psychographics don't
  change that much." Spend research energy on **behavior**: news, videos, trends, shows, other health
  conditions. "That's where the best topics, formats, concepts and hooks come from."

**For us:**
- Psychographics are already written in `research/avatar_personas.md`, `voc_synthesis.md` and the survey digest. Reuse them; don't re-research.
- New research time goes to behavior: what she is watching or reading right now. Examples: Black Friday, FSA deadlines, Disney openings and crowd news, nurse staffing news, travel season, viral TikTok foot trends. These feed Topic, Format and Hook.
- Our markets (one per ad): standing-all-day workers (nurse is the biggest segment), Disney / theme-park travel, Europe / bucket-list travel, plus-size walking, active retirees, foot conditions (plantar fasciitis, bunions, neuropathy).
- Our proven segments: nurse 12s, plus-size at Disney, grandma at Disney, Europe cobblestones, long flights.

## 5. Step 3: the Brief (what goes in `dr_brief`)

He fills every element with a specific, sourced answer. The worked example shows the moves:

- **Point of contact:** use what every ad in that segment already blames. "I don't need to convince him of anything new. I just need to connect the dots."
- **Problem mechanism:** a chain of 2–3 facts she half-knows that land on the real cause.
- **Function mechanism:** why the obvious fix she already tried (the blue pill) can't work *for her specifically* ("wasn't made for men with diabetes"). Specific to the segment = she feels understood.
- **Solution mechanism:** "I barely have to work here": name the thing and what it does at the root.
- **Topic:** from behavior research (a real search question in the segment, e.g. "Can you mix Viagra with metformin?").
- **Format:** reuse validated looks from 1–2 running ads. Add *one* bet (GTA VI style riding the release) = "a high-leverage bet… with multiple elements that have already been validated."
- **Concept:** a logline (characters + narrative + topic). The angle that creates an "uncontrollable urge" calls out a habit she probably has and amplifies the fear.
- **Copy hook:** first pick a proven hook STRUCTURE (many ads use it, it fits the concept), then fill it with the segment's own slang.
- **Visual hook:** congruent with format and concept, and still breaks expectation.
- **Conditions:** pre-empt "I'll just buy X at the store" by explaining why the mechanism is hard to get in the right form.
- **USP:** "simply the answer to the Conditions," product attribute by attribute. No made-up differentiators.
- **Features:** "Nobody buys hawthorn. The guy buys what hawthorn does for him." Map each feature to odds, dream, effort or time. Be honest about time ("I'm not going to promise results on the first night… explain why it takes time").
- **Anchoring:** cost per day vs the financial, emotional and time cost of never solving it.
- **Risk reversal:** reframe the guarantee as "more than enough time to prove it in your own body."
- **Gain / lose:** concrete extras now, real reasons later is worse. (His scarcity is the brand's real stock situation; ours must be true too.)

## 6. Step 4: Writing

> "The brief decides WHAT gets said. The reference only decides the ORDER and the RHYTHM. That's exactly why
> most people who 'let AI write it' get bad results: they skip the brief and expect the model to guess the
> psychology for them."

For us: StefanBrain writes (CLAUDE.md rule). We send it the reference (format_ref / based_on) **and** the
`dr_brief` (facts and reasons the avatar wants to hear). We do not send it tone, framework or sample lines;
the words, order within the slots, and rhythm stay StefanBrain's. The brief is facts, not style.

## 7. Risk management across a batch

> "Minimize risk where leverage is low. Raise the bet where leverage is high." … "I usually run a few
> conservative-risk ads, a lot of moderate-risk ones and some aggressive-risk ones. The risk is proportional
> to the number of bets placed in that ad."

- A **bet** is any element that has not been validated in this market: a new format, new concept, new topic, new mechanism or new segment.
- **Risk levels:**
  - `conservative`: 0 new bets. A proven format, concept, hook structure and segment, with a fresh visual or iteration.
  - `moderate`: 1 bet (usually the concept or the format look).
  - `aggressive`: 2 or more bets.
- **Batch mix:** ~25% conservative, ~55% moderate, ~20% aggressive. Market and segment are almost never the bet; concept, format and hook are where the bets go.
- Every concept records `risk` and `bets: [...]` so verdicts teach us which bets pay.

## 8. ComfortWear application (premises + mechanism stack; reuse every batch)

All claims below follow brand.json and CLAUDE.md (approved: 30-day money-back guarantee, 500,000+,
HSA/FSA eligible, doctor- and podiatrist-recommended; never cure/treatment, instant relief, extra-wide,
shipping speed). The mechanism is about **shoe construction**, never about healing the body.

**Premises:**
- **Brand / product:** ComfortWear, Ortho Stretch Cushion Shoe.
- **Components:**
  - stretch-knit upper
  - wide toe box
  - built-in arch support
  - cushioned rocker sole
  - elastic no-tie bungee laces
  - slip-resistant grip
- **Price:** $59.95 (was $120).
- **Guarantee:** 30-day money-back guarantee.
- **Proof:** 500,000+ customers; doctor- and podiatrist-recommended; HSA/FSA eligible.
- **Gain now (Black Friday 2026):**
  - 60% OFF SITEWIDE (or buy 2 pairs, get socks + travel bag)
  - code LEAKED10 for an extra 10%
  - free shipping & returns
- **Lose later:** the Black Friday price ends (state the real end date). No stock stories unless confirmed.

**Mechanism stack** (sourced from avatar_personas.md §1.3 "The Real Enemy" and VOC):
- **Point of contact (7):** "It's my shoes / it's the hard floors / I'm just getting older."
  - Confirm *shoes*.
  - Reframe *age*: "it's not your age, it's your shoes" (Konjoun pattern).
- **Problem mechanism (8):** her "comfortable" sneakers were built for something else.
  - Running shoes are made for short forward runs, not standing.
  - The toe box tapers and pinches.
  - The raised heel tips her weight onto the balls of her feet.
  - The soft foam feels great in the store but compresses by hour 4–6.
  - Her feet swell through the day, so a shoe that fit at 7 AM clamps by 2 PM.
- **Function mechanism (9):** she needs three things that last all shift or trip: support that doesn't go flat, room that gives as feet swell, and a sole that helps roll each step forward. The fixes she tried can't do all three:
  - Insoles go inside the same narrow shoe.
  - Clogs and stiff orthotics don't give or absorb.
  - Memory-foam slip-ons collapse.
- **Solution mechanism (10):** Ortho Stretch puts the three in one shoe:
  - built-in arch support and a cushioned rocker sole
  - a stretch-knit upper with a wide toe box that gives as feet swell
  - no-tie laces, so nothing to retie when feet change size
- **Conditions (11):** "Why not just buy insoles or bigger sneakers?"
  - A bigger size slips at the heel.
  - Insoles take up the room her toes need.
  - Most "comfort" shoes pick one of support or give. Ortho Stretch is built for both.
- **USP (12):** the answer to Conditions, feature by feature (no invented tech names: the user rejected "Terminal Arch Support" and similar).
- **Features → lever (13):**

  | Feature | Lever | Why |
  |---|---|---|
  | Arch support + rocker sole | odds | Support that's there at hour 10 |
  | Stretch knit + wide toe box | odds / dream | Toes flat at hour 12; no pinching bunions |
  | No-tie laces | effort | Slip on |
  | Slip-resistant grip | dream | Confidence on hospital tile or cobblestones |
  | Time | time | Be honest: "from the first shift" only as a real quote (Trustpilot: "my feet didn't hurt after day 1!"); never "instant relief" |
- **Anchoring (14):**
  - $59.95 vs a closet of $160–$220 pairs that failed by lunchtime.
  - About 38¢ a shift over a year of 12s.
  - Emotional costs: missed shifts, sitting out Disney day 2, the trip of a lifetime on a bench.
- **Risk reversal (15):** 30 days is enough to wear them through 10+ shifts or a whole trip. The bigger risk is another season in shoes that already failed.
  - **Caution:** VOC shows a strong "return trap" fear and real refund friction, so never promise "easy returns". The approved wording is "free shipping & returns" plus the 30-day guarantee.
- **Gain / lose (16–17):** the current offer and its real end date.

**WHO (messenger) options:**
- a nurse coworker
- a doctor or podiatrist character (approved)
- a grandma back from Disney
- a plus-size woman who tried everything
- the ComfortWear team note (letter, apology, "we messed up" only if true)

**HOW (format) options, all validated:**
- letter
- text message / notification
- comment bubble
- whiteboard
- face split
- "Don't buy unless…"
- "X industry lies"
- "Your X weren't built for Y"
- two-column vs
- review-on-a-bench
- FSA POV

## 9. Second pass, sentence by sentence (2026-11-20): what §0–§8 left out

The user re-sent the thread and asked again for every sentence to be understood and applied. §0–§8 cover
the frame. These are the details inside his worked example that change what we actually do. Each item is
written as: his words → what it means → what we change.

### Target

1. **"If you try to talk to everyone... you talk to no one."**
   - Meaning: one market, one segment and one avatar per ad.
   - We change: `dr_brief.who` names one persona from `avatar_personas.md`. A concept that "works for nurses and travelers" is two concepts.
2. **Choosing a market for a running brand: "find their landers with the most traffic"** and "analyze every ad they've launched in the last 60 days… whichever market shows up most."
   - Meaning: the market is read off where the money already goes, not chosen by taste.
   - We change: the market comes from our own spend (the §1b pull and the winners). For a new market, he wants at least 3 competitors in it, or he "cuts it right away".
   - We only open a new market (e.g. a new job or condition) when at least 3 advertisers run it on Atria. Log the count in `angle_gap`.
3. **The poker rule: "minimize risk in low-leverage stages and raise the bet in high-leverage stages."**
   - Meaning: low leverage = market, segment, avatar, mechanism. High leverage = concept, format look, hook.
   - We change: a `bets` entry naming a new market or segment needs a reason in the concept. Default bets are `concept`, `format`, `hook`, `topic`.
4. **The avatar spec is one line he can act on:** "a man, between 40 and 60, American, married, Christian, who has type 2 diabetes and has been dealing with ED for a few years."
   - Meaning: demographics, faith, family role, a co-condition and how long she's had the problem.
   - We change: `dr_brief.avatar` is one line of this kind, e.g. "woman, 58–66, American, married grandmother, churchgoer, plus-size with a bunion, 3+ years of foot pain, Disney trip in spring". Duration matters: years of failed fixes = more skepticism (§0).
5. **Identity research is a fixed checklist.** He lists every question.
   - Demographics: sex, age, nationality, language, race/ethnic group, religion, marital status, family role.
   - Behavior: competitors' ads that catch her, general videos that catch her, trending topics, hobbies, news, movies/shows, other health conditions.
   - Perspective: beliefs, biases, enemies, allies, language.
   - Meaning: this is where he spends his research time, especially behavior.
   - We change: the §1 research refresh answers the *behavior* questions for the batch's personas. Write them to `weeks/<folder>/behavior.md`: one line each, with the source (Atria ad, search question, news link, TikTok trend). Topic, format and hook ideas cite this file.
6. **"Steal other people's research… ClickBank VSLs that performed in the past… advertorials and ads (from any year)."**
   - Meaning: psychographics live inside old winners; they "don't change that much".
   - We change: when a persona's pains feel thin, read long-running footwear/orthotic advertorials and long-form ads before writing anything new. Use Atria (long `days_running`, any start date) and our own `research/longform_angles.md`. Don't re-run surveys or deep VOC for psychographics.
7. **"The ads and funnels aimed at the same segment already carry the small differences, language patterns and nuances."**
   - We change: before briefing a segment, read 3 running ads aimed at exactly that segment. Copy their *nouns* (what she calls her feet, her shoes, her shifts) into `dr_brief.language`. Example: he used "johnson" because he "saw a few ads referring to the avatar's penis" that way.

### Mechanism

8. **Point of contact = the consensus of the segment's ads.** "Every ad aimed at this segment points to high blood sugar as the main culprit."
   - Meaning: don't pick the point of contact from logic. Pick what that segment's ads already blame.
   - We change: `point_of_contact` cites at least 2 running ads (ours or competitors') that blame the same thing.
9. **Problem mechanism = "connect the dots", not "convince".** "I don't need to convince him of anything new."
   - Meaning: a chain of 2–3 facts she already half-believes.
   - We change: every link in `problem_mechanism` must be something VOC or the survey shows she already believes (e.g. "my feet swell by afternoon", "the foam goes flat"). A link she has never heard is a bet: add it to `bets`.
10. **Function mechanism uses three kinds of evidence:**
    - a news story ("men with ED who have diabetes… get weaker results from the little blue pills");
    - a search question that shows a fear ("Can you mix Viagra with metformin?");
    - her own lived comparison (a friend without diabetes for whom it works).
    - That gives the line "the little blue pill wasn't made for men with diabetes".
    - Meaning: the obvious fix fails *because of who she is*, and she has seen it work for someone else.
    - We change: `function_mechanism` names the fix this segment already tried and why it works for others but not for her. Examples:
      - Hokas work for runners, not for 12 hours standing on tile.
      - Insoles work in a roomy shoe, not in a tapered one.
      - Skech\*rs work for errands, not for Disney hour 8.
    - Cite the evidence type: news, search question or lived comparison.
11. **Solution mechanism: "I barely have to work here."**
    - Meaning: if 8–10 are right, the solution is one sentence: name the thing and what it does.
    - We change: if `solution_mechanism` needs more than two sentences, the problem or function mechanism is weak. Fix those first.

### Content (topic, format, concept, hooks)

12. **Topic follows the segment's narrative style:** "Most of the ads… had an informational narrative… So I'm going to prioritize topics built around a specific 'fact'." And: "I usually list way more topics."
    - We change: write 5+ candidate topics per persona in `behavior.md` and pick one. Match the style the segment's winners use (story vs fact). A search question is the strongest topic source.
13. **Format = recombine validated pieces:** "From this first ad… the setting and the dynamic between the two characters… From this second ad… the style."
    - Meaning: format has three parts: (a) look, (b) narrative style, (c) characters and roles. Each part can be borrowed from a different running ad.
    - We change: `dr_brief.format` names the source of each part, e.g. "look: Hike notes-app ad m…; narrative: our COM320 informational; characters: podiatrist + nurse patient".
14. **The one bet is "a hypothesis with something behind it… in the right market… with multiple elements that have already been validated"**, riding a moment ("GTA VI just came out and the timing is perfect").
    - We change: an aggressive or moderate bet must state its evidence ("animation works in this market" → which ads) and, if it rides a moment, which moment and its date. "It's new" is not a reason.
15. **Concept = characters + narrative + topic, then an angle that calls out a habit she probably has and amplifies the fear** ("Don't mix metformin with Viagra unless you want to lose your erections forever").
    - We change: `dr_brief.concept` is a logline. `dr_brief.angle_habit` names the habit she has today (wearing running shoes to work, sizing up, buying insoles, saving "good shoes" for trips).
16. **Copy hook: structure first, words second.** "The first thing I do is pick the hook structure… I found tons of ads using this structure, in different ways, in different formats."
    - We change: `copy_hook_structure` names a structure with at least 3 running examples (ours or Atria ids), e.g. "This is what happens to your feet when…", "Don't wear X unless…", "Your X weren't built for Y". StefanBrain fills it with the segment's own nouns (item 7).
17. **Visual hook: take a validated one and push it to match the copy hook** ("a rotting banana to crank up the shock").
    - We change: `visual_hook` names the validated source and the one intensifier, e.g. "flattened running-shoe foam → foam split open with a crack". The intensifier must stay true to the product (no fake damage on our shoe).

### Value

18. **"This is where most ads fall apart… the ad turns into an ingredient catalog."**
    - Static version: the shoe with six feature callouts.
    - We change: the image carries at most one feature, and only one that proves the mechanism beat on the image. Features go in the primary text, each with its lever.
19. **"If the Hope Sale was done right, the Value Sale almost takes care of itself."**
    - We change: QA order. If the value copy feels like it needs a lot of convincing, the hope sale (7–11) is the problem.
20. **Conditions answer the do-it-yourself substitute:** "Cool, I'll just buy some cayenne pepper at the grocery store." Then he lists the 3 conditions the substitute can't meet.
    - We change: `conditions` names her DIY substitute and the conditions it misses:
      - She buys a size up → the heel slips.
      - She adds insoles → they take the toe room.
      - She buys cheap "comfort" sneakers → the foam goes flat.
      - She buys clogs → no give, and hard on cobblestones.

### Writing

31. **Mini-blocks: "chunks of the reference ad split up by the job each one does, for example: hook, belief break, mechanism reveal, product presentation."**
    - "The brief decides WHAT gets said. The reference only decides the ORDER and the RHYTHM."
    - Meaning: the reference contributes its invisible structure, not its words.
    - We change: every concept built on a reference carries `reference_blocks`: the reference ad's parts in order, each named by its job.     - `ads/stefanbrain.py copy` sends it with the slot. StefanBrain follows that order and rhythm; the `dr_brief` decides what is said, and the words stay StefanBrain's. This is structure taken from a proven ad, not a framework of ours. It is the step he says people skip when "AI writes it" badly.

    - We change: StefanBrain gets no concept without a filled `dr_brief`. `copy` warns when a slot has none.

### What to ignore

- The claims about himself and the revenue promise ("$100k, $200k, $300k a day") are credibility, not method.
- His product-specific choices don't apply to us: sexualized character styling ("showing cleavage"), disease mechanisms and stock stories. Our claims rules and brand rules stay as they are.
- He borrows a commercial game's characters for the format bet. We don't use other companies' characters or IP. The lesson we keep is "ride a cultural moment with a validated format type" (e.g. animation, a TV-show-style layout we make ourselves).
