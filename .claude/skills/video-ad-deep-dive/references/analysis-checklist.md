# Analysis checklist (per video ad)

Fill this in for every analyzed video, then compare winners against weaker ads. A pattern counts only
if it separates them.

## Numbers to pull
- Spend, ROAS, CPA, AOV, CPM, link CTR, click-to-purchase
- Hook rate (thumbstop), hold rate, p25 / p50 / p75 / p95 play rates, average play time
- Week-over-week ROAS (prior 7d → last 7d), frequency

## Hook (0–3s, from the hook sheet)
- First frame: who or what is on screen? A real location, a person talking, a product, a graphic, or
  text only?
- Is the expert or authority figure in frame? Composited over location footage, or standalone?
- Headline overlay: exact wording, style (bar or box, highlighted colours), how long it stays up.
- Spoken first line, word for word. Is it a direct callout ("We asked 500 plus-sized women…") or soft
  and euphemistic ("If you're heavier than your feet would prefer…")?
- Is the brand's own product shown in the first 3s?

## Structure (from the transcript, with timestamps)
- When the expert first appears (seconds).
- When the product is first shown and first named (seconds). Compare that with the p25 and p95 rates:
  roughly what share of viewers ever see it?
- Format: competitor teardown, "we asked N people", expert explainer, rating /10, drama or skit,
  UGC testimonial, listicle.
- Competitors named, with prices. Are names shown clearly or blurred?
- Is our own price named as an anchor?
- **Mechanism**: why the obvious alternative fails, and why ours doesn't.
- **Time-stamped pain** ("by hour 5", "day two") and future pacing.
- **Payoff line** ("still walking at the fireworks").
- **Offer and guarantee as spoken**: discount %, gifts, HSA/FSA, guarantee days, shipping.
- **Close and CTA.**

## Body b-roll (from the body sheet; body frame k = second 4×(k−1))
Count and describe:
- people who look like the target customer, at the occasion or destination
- expert handling competitor products and our product
- unboxing or handling competitor products, including cutting or squeezing to "test" them
- medical or how-it-works graphics (anatomy, red glow, arrows, cutaways)
- pain acting (bench, rubbing, icing, grimace) and payoff shots (rides, fireworks, still going)
- occasion "grind" shots (crowds, lines, cobblestones, shifts)
- product-in-use shots (point of view, flexing, stretching, insole press) vs studio shots
- selfie or UGC talking clips, and social-proof graphics (reviews, Reddit, star ratings)
- AI-looking or mismatched footage (wrong park or location, stock that contradicts the voiceover)

## On-screen text and end card
- Caption style: boxed, phrase by phrase, keyword colour highlights.
- End card: a static discount card, or the expert kept in a corner over b-roll? What does it say
  (discount, guarantee, badges)?

## Message match (the most common leak)
- Does the video's occasion and audience (Disney, cruise, Europe, nurses, plus-size, Spanish) match:
  - the ad text (`body`)?
  - the headline (`title`)?
  - the landing page (`link_url`)? Read the slug; fetch the page if it's ambiguous.
- Does the offer in the video match the ad text, the end card and the site? Also check the guarantee
  length across ads.
- Season: are winter or holiday ads pointing at "this summer" pages, or the reverse?
- Language: do Spanish videos land on Spanish pages for the same persona?

## Credibility checks
- Claims the video can't back up ("seven shoes" when six are shown).
- Footage that contradicts the voiceover.
- Inconsistent prices.

## Proof
- Does the video include customer proof (reviews, star cards, testimonial clips), or only claims?
- Compare with the account's review/objection-handling statics, if any, and how they perform.

## Fatigue
- Week-over-week ROAS and CPM trends. Is the biggest spender also the weakest?
