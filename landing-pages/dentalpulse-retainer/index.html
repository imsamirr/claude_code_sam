Brushing your retainer is scratching it, and the scratches are what turn it cloudy

Most of us were told to "just brush it" at the orthodontist. But toothpaste is abrasive, and a clear retainer is soft plastic. Every scrub leaves micro-scratches you can't see.

Those scratches are where the trouble starts. They trap plaque, minerals and bacteria, and over a few months your once-invisible retainer goes milky, dull and faintly yellow.

The DentalPULSE Pod never touches your retainer with bristles. It cleans with 42,000 Hz sound waves and plain tap water, so there's nothing to scratch it.
2

That white film? It's back by lunchtime, because a brush can't reach where it lives

Run a fingernail along the inside of your retainer. That chalky, slightly slimy layer builds up in the tooth wells, along the edges and, on wire retainers, right where the wire meets the acrylic.

A toothbrush skims the flat surfaces. It can't get into the grooves, and it definitely can't get under the wire.

Ultrasonic cleaning works differently. At 42,000 vibrations per second, it creates millions of microscopic bubbles that collapse against every surface the water touches: inside each tooth well, around every clasp, under the wire.
